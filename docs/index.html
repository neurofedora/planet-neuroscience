    Normative and neural network models of multisensory integration

    arXiv:2611.32829v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Animals continuously detect scents, sights and sounds, which their brains combine into diverse multisensory streams. Multisensory integration accelerates learning, increases reaction speeds and helps to resolve sensory ambiguities. In studying this process, the field has developed a continuum of models; from concise mathematical equations, with a handful of variables, to artificial neural networks, with billions of parameters. In this review, we divide this spectrum into two classes: normative models start from a hypothesis about how multisensory signals should be integrated, while neural network models aim to emulate how circuits could integrate them. Together, these models demonstrate that observers can integrate cues in a statistically optimal fashion, and help to explain experimental findings, including the diversity of multisensory neuron response profiles and network architectures. In this review, we provide a didactic overview of multisensory models, complete with accessible equations and Python code, and compare their assumptions and explanatory power. We detail what has been learned from these models. Finally, we discuss how the ideas and insights from classical theoretical models can be reconciled with those from artificial neural networks, and how this convergence should shape future research.

    Direct Conditioning of Audio Diffusion Transformers on fMRI Reveals Cortical Contributions to Sound Reconstruction

    arXiv:2611.12603v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Reconstructing audio from brain activity remains challenging. Existing brain-to-audio pipelines typically map neural responses to predefined stimulus representations before synthesis. While recent work in the visual domain has explored direct conditioning on brain activity, this approach has not yet been extended to audio diffusion models. We introduce MAAS (Multi-Area Auditory Synthesis), an end-to-end framework that conditions a pretrained audio diffusion transformer on ROI-wise fMRI responses without first supervising a decoder to predict an external stimulus representation. On a 7T fMRI dataset, we demonstrate consistent improvements over baselines, including two-stage fMRI-to-latent conditioning, across both acoustic and semantic evaluation metrics. In addition, we introduce a fidelity metric grounded in a computational model of auditory neural processing, which allows us to quantify the contribution of individual auditory cortical regions to reconstructed audio representations.

    The Self-Assembling Brain: Emergent Routing and Its Open Challenges

    arXiv:2611.31629v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Biologically inspired alternatives to backpropagation, such as feedback alignment, relax the requirement for exact synaptic weight symmetry but still presuppose a fixed, pre-wired correspondence between forward and feedback pathways, an impasse Liao et al. (PNAS, 2026) term "architectural circularity." This commentary examines their Self-Assembling Motif (SAM), which reframes credit assignment as a self-organizing process driven by local synaptic rules. By linking ascending inference and descending feedback streams through initially random cross-stream connections, SAM supports local bridge alignment and emergent pathway alignment during stable training without fixed neuron-to-neuron pairing.     The cyborg fly: reflexes survive engineered actuators, coordination needs stand-ins

    arXiv:2611.31644v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Two wiring diagrams now cover the whole central nervous system of an adult fruit fly: one female, one male. Can such a diagram, run as a network of spiking neurons, drive a body? And if we swap the body's muscles for engineered actuators, what survives? We ran both connectomes, BANC and MaleCNS, as leaky integrate-and-fire networks of about 160,000 neurons each, and closed the loop through simulated sensors, actuators and bodies for walking and flight. The actuators are port-Hamiltonian, so energy is audited at every step, and swapping one for another changes only a configuration file. Null networks shuffled at five levels, from the whole nervous system to synapse counts, test whether a result depends on the wiring. The wiring produced specific functions: sugar on the labellum drove the proboscis motor neuron, leg joints resisted imposed movement, leg circuits oscillated, a looming disc drove a take-off signal on its side (in MaleCNS), and descending neurons throttled and steered flight. The resistance reflex survived 20 of 20 actuator settings in MaleCNS and 19 of 20 in BANC, including a Hill-type muscle pair and a geared DC motor; the actuator set walking speed and energy cost instead. Coordinated six-leg walking, flight stability and the correction of perturbations did not emerge. Each needed a labelled engineered stand-in, which the connectome could command. Such stand-ins can also manufacture an apparent dependence on wiring: in a rebuild of a published closed loop, odour navigation came from a hand-written rule, and the network entered only through one valence number. The two animals differed on some functions, and the differences traced to wiring, such as a haltere drive of the flight power muscles found only in MaleCNS. A whole-CNS connectome, embodied, gives reflexes, rhythms and command signals, but not the coordination that turns them into behaviour.

    Network topology imposes spectral constraints on collective nonequilibrium responses

    arXiv:2611.32130v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Collective nonequilibrium dynamics in Langevin systems depend on both network topology and stochastic driving. Understanding the role of topology in responses to stochastic driving and in the emergence of nonequilibrium is essential. Starting from the principle of microscopic reversibility and linear Langevin dynamics on networks, we derive a mode decomposition for both the asymmetry of short-time cross-correlations and heat currents at steady state. This decomposition reveals two sources of nonequilibrium---fluctuations of individual modes and correlated fluctuations between distinct modes. For reciprocal networks with independent local driving, we derive two nonequilibrium response operators that yield explicit expressions for the global asymmetry intensity and the entropy production rate. These operators are weighted Laplacians independent of stochastic driving. Their spectra determine attainable upper and lower bounds on nonequilibrium at fixed driving heterogeneity. These bounds are exact for asymmetry intensity at arbitrary deviations from equilibrium and hold to leading order near equilibrium for entropy production rate. Finally, we find that the hierarchical modular organization of human structural connectomes broadens the brain's nonequilibrium operating range, with resting-state brain activity supporting this topological advantage for sustaining a lower nonequilibrium level. Our study provides a basis for understanding biological network organization and designing networks with targeted nonequilibrium operating points.

    Accuracy of Noise Ceiling Estimators for Brain Scores

    arXiv:2611.32792v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: The internal activations of modern machine learning models align surprisingly well with the activities recorded in real brains. In the last five years, the alignment between encoding models and brain activities, often called brain scores, has steadily increased. Because brain recordings are noisy, there exists an upper bound to the brain scores that any model can achieve. This bound, called the noise ceiling, is crucial for two reasons: it determines whether further improvements in model performance are possible, and it enables meaningful comparisons of brain scores across benchmarks. The field has developed multiple approaches to estimate noise ceilings, but the statistical properties of existing estimators remain poorly understood, leaving it unclear in which experimental settings they provide reliable estimates. Here, we introduce a unified mathematical framework for brain recordings, use it to derive closed-form expressions for four commonly used noise ceiling estimators, and provide theoretical predictions for the behavior of each estimator. We validate the theoretical predictions with simulations that we compare to noise ceiling estimates in existing fMRI and EEG datasets. We find that the existing methods underestimate the true noise ceiling in different settings. We conclude with recommendations for best practice in different situations.

    EEG-World: Context-Conditioned Dynamics of the Watching Brain

    arXiv:2611.32825v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Brain encoding models predict neural responses from audiovisual input, but typically overlook how ongoing brain activity shapes an individual's response. We introduce EEG-World, a recurrent world model that forecasts 10 seconds of EEG band-power dynamics from 6 seconds of preceding EEG and the subsequent audiovisual stimulus. Pretrained on continuous EEG, EEG-World learns shared neural dynamics and adapts to unseen individuals through lightweight subject-specific readouts. Using 2,584 hours of EEG from 78 participants, we show that nonlinear recurrent forecasting surpasses strong history-aware linear baselines at scale. EEG-World achieves a forecasting correlation of 0.261 versus 0.233 for the strongest history-and-video linear model, with a 3.3-second forecasting-horizon advantage. Its learned dynamics transfer to unseen participants, outperforming the linear baseline in 23 of 24 individuals after calibration. While ongoing EEG history accounts for most predictive performance, controlled stimulus analyses reveal an additional video-dependent component. This component also enables evaluation of visual representations, with V-JEPA 2 improving clip-locked prediction by 35% over combined CLIP and DINOv2 features. Our results demonstrate the value of transferable neural dynamics for individual EEG forecasting and highlight the importance of separating state-driven from stimulus-driven predictability.

    Visualizing the geometry of labeled high-dimensional data with spheres

    arXiv:2107.00731v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Data visualizations can summarize high-dimensional distributions in two or three dimensions. Dimensionality reduction entails a loss of information, and what is preserved differs between methods. Existing methods preserve the local or the global geometry of the points, and most techniques do not consider labels. Here we introduce "hypersphere2sphere" (H2S), a new method that aims to visualize not the points, but the relationships between the labeled distributions. H2S fits a hypersphere to each labeled set of points in the high-dimensional space of the original data and visualizes each hypersphere as a sphere in 3D (or a circle in 2D). H2S perfectly captures the geometry of up to 4 high-dimensional hyperspheres using spheres in 3D (or 3 using circles in 2D), and approximates the geometry for larger numbers of distributions, matching the sizes (radii), and the pairwise separations (between-center distances) and the overlaps (along the center-connection line). The resulting visualizations are robust to sampling imbalances. Leveraging labels and the sphere as the simplest geometrical primitive, H2S provides an important addition to the toolbox of visualization techniques.

    Causal pieces: analysing and improving spiking neural networks piece by piece

    arXiv:2504.14015v3 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: We introduce "causal pieces", a novel concept for analysing spiking neural networks (SNNs), inspired by "linear pieces" used to study expressivity and trainability in artificial neural networks (ANNs). Causal pieces partition the input and parameter space of a feedforward SNN with single-spike coding into distinct regions where the same subnetwork causes the output spikes. For networks of current-based leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neurons with large membrane time constants, we show that within each causal piece, output spike times are locally Lipschitz continuous with respect to inputs and network parameters. We further prove a lower bound on the approximation error that depends on the number of causal pieces. Thus, the number of causal pieces is a measure of the approximation capabilities of SNNs, which is valid despite spike-time discontinuities and applies to networks with both excitatory and inhibitory synapses. Empirically, we find that parameter initialisations yielding more causal pieces on the training set strongly correlate with SNN training success across multiple benchmarks, including Yin-Yang, Fashion-MNIST, and EuroSAT. Moreover, simulations with standard single-spike LIF neurons indicate that our findings extend beyond the theoretically analysed regime. These results establish causal pieces as a powerful and principled tool for analysing and improving the computational capabilities of SNNs.

    A Mathematical Theory of the Jacobian Lens

    arXiv:2610.31955v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Recent work from Anthropic introduced a new interpretability tool, called the Jacobian lens (J-lens, Gurnee et al. 2026). This method identified linear directions in the hidden states of frontier language models that had properties reminiscent of working-memory-like representations. However, it is unclear what mechanistic properties of such features make them detectable by the J-lens. To this end, we introduce a mathematical theory of the J-lens and show that it preferentially identifies coherent features--directions that act like leading, spatially coherent finite-time Lyapunov modes. Small perturbations along these directions maximally amplify across layers, remain roughly constant across token positions, and are consistent across different contexts. While we are agnostic to whether these properties are necessarily associated with a "Global Workspace'', the analytically derived preferences of the J-lens nevertheless explain prior empirical results. We validate our analysis on a Bayes filter and a toy transformer, each optimally predicting next tokens emitted from a Hidden Markov Model (HMM). We show how modulating task parameters that change the coherence of features in the Bayes filter correspondingly and predictively modulates the response of the J-lens applied to both model classes. We also introduce a family of J-lenses built to emphasize different temporal scales of information processing in LLMs, which we call $\omega$-J-lenses.

    A child who has seen a few sums can add numbers of any length: finite experience has become a program, a procedure with memory that holds for inputs of every size.
