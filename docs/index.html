    When AI Speaks the Student's Language: Toward a Theory of Linguistically Responsive Generative AI in African Higher Education [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) is rapidly reshaping higher education, yet dominant approaches to AI-enabled learning often treat language as a neutral medium of interaction rather than a constitutive dimension of learning, identity, knowledge construction, and educational participation. This assumption is particularly problematic in African higher education, where multilingualism, code-switching, linguistic hierarchies, and unequal recognition of indigenous languages shape students’ educational experiences. This conceptual paper advances Linguistically Responsive Generative AI (LR-GAI) as a theoretical construct and develops a theory of how linguistic responsiveness can shape the educational value of GenAI in multilingual contexts. Drawing on Sociocultural Theory, Translanguaging Theory, and the Capability Approach, the paper argues that access to GenAI does not necessarily translate into equitable educational capability when learners’ linguistic repertoires are poorly accommodated by AI systems. It distinguishes linguistic responsiveness from simple multilingual processing or translation and conceptualises LR-GAI through five interrelated dimensions: linguistic accessibility, multilingual responsiveness, semantic and cultural contextualisation, pedagogical responsiveness, and linguistic agency. The paper proposes the Linguistic Mediation Proposition, which posits that the educational value of GenAI is partly determined by the alignment between learners’ linguistic repertoires and the linguistic responsiveness of AI-mediated learning environments. From this proposition, the paper develops theoretically grounded propositions linking linguistic responsiveness to learner–AI interaction, knowledge construction, epistemic and linguistic agency, and educational participation. The resulting framework challenges language-neutral models of AI in education and provides a theoretical foundation for designing, evaluating, and governing GenAI systems that support linguistically diverse learners in African higher education.

Methodology A descriptive analytical approach was used, and data were collected through a questionnaire administered to a sample of 150 administrative leaders in private banks. Investing in digital infrastructure and expanding the use of digital applications contributes to supporting operational efficiency, increasing responsiveness to technological changes, and enhancing banks’ readiness for market requirements.

    Mixed Plasmodium Infections: Report of Four Cases from the Military Hospital of Tunis [version 3; peer review: 2 approved with reservations]

    Background Malaria is a life-threatening vector-borne disease caused by five species of the genus Plasmodium (P.) It can present as mixed infections that remain underreported and pose diagnostic, therapeutic and prognostic challenges. The objective of the present study was to describe the epidemiological, clinical, and biological features of four cases of mixed Plasmodium infection. Methods This retrospective descriptive study included cases of mixed Plasmodium infections diagnosed at the Parasitology Laboratory of the Military Hospital of Tunis between 2022 and 2025. Diagnosis was established using May–Grünwald–Giemsa–stained thin blood smears, Giemsa–stained thick smears, and rapid diagnostic tests. Plasmodium species were identified according to standard microscopic morphological criteria. Results We report four cases of mixed Plasmodium infection diagnosed in military personnel returning from deployment in Central African Republic. The patients were aged 31–52 years (mean age 38.8 years) with sex ratio 3. They presented within 10–30 days post-return with fever (38–42°C) associated with headache, myalgia, abdominal pain, and/or vomiting. All had adhered to malaria chemoprophylaxis, and three reported no prior malaria episodes, while one patient had a history of six previous attacks. Rapid diagnostic tests were positive for pan-Plasmodium pLDH in all cases; two patients were also positive for HRP2. Peripheral blood smears confirmed mixed infections. Parasitemia was generally low (<1% in three cases, 1% in one case). Treatment consisted of artemether–lumefantrine (Coartem®) for all patients, combined with primaquine for those with P. ovale infection. Clinical evolution was favorable in all cases, and follow-up blood smears on days 3, 7, and 28 confirmed parasitological clearance, with only rare degenerated trophozoites observed in one patient on day 3. Conclusion Mixed Plasmodium infections can influence disease severity, treatment outcomes, and drug resistance. Accurate diagnosis and radical therapy are keys to preventing relapses.

Methods Six classification models were compared to classify graduates according to whether they secured employment within six months or experienced longer waiting times, while education-related determinants of employment transition were also examined.     Does Inquiry-Based Learning Improve Students’ Critical Thinking? A Meta-Analysis Accounting for Control Group Variations [version 3; peer review: 3 approved with reservations]

    Background Inquiry learning is widely recognized, through empirical studies, as an appropriate instruction in enhancing students’ critical thinking, yet the results were varied across context. The previous meta-analysis did not include the control group variations as a potential moderator and the studies subject domain was limited only to science subjects. Consequently, it is difficult to generalize the effectiveness of IBL in enhancing critical thinking. This meta-analysis aims to investigate whether inquiry learning is effective in improving the students’ critical thinking skills and examine the moderating roles of each study characteristic. Methods The literature search applying the PRISMA protocol 2020 was conducted by utilizing SCOPUS, ERIC, and DOAJ databases. A total of 57 studies from 51 articles, published from 2015 to 2025, were synthesized using a random-effects model with standardized mean difference (SMD). Results The analysis revealed that IBL has a large and significant effect on enhancing students’ critical thinking (g = 1.336; 95% CI [1.061, 1.611]). However, substantial heterogeneity was observed (I 2 = 92.09%), suggesting variability across contexts. Moderator analyses revealed that the main moderator, control group variations, was statistically significant in moderating the effectiveness of IBL (Qm = 5.21; p = .022). in contrast, subject domain (Qm = 1.43; p = .698), education level (Qm = 1.11; p = .774), and country (Qm = 3.33; p = .650), were insignificantly moderating the effectiveness of inquiry learning. Conclusions The present meta-analysis highlighted that IBL is effective in improving students’ critical thinking. However, the effectiveness of IBL was relative to the type of control group variations. Its effect on critical thinking was greater when compared with teacher-centered learning but smaller when compared with other student-centered learning.

    An Ubuntu Perspective on Community Engagement in Indigenous Knowledge Systems Research: Insights from South Africa [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Community engagement is widely recognised in research, yet in many African contexts, it remains procedural rather than meaningful, highlighting the need for approaches that foster genuine relationships. This is especially evident in indigenous knowledge systems research, where communities are often treated as sources of data instead of as partners. Such approaches can reinforce power imbalances and weaken trust. Ubuntu grounded in relationality, reciprocity, mutual respect and collective responsibility offers a cultural rooted lens for creative community engagement. This paper explores how Ubuntu can strengthen community engagement in indigenous Knowledge System research. Methods A conceptual research design was adopted, drawing on literature from Ubuntu philosophy, community engagement in research and indigenous knowledge Systems. Literature was drawn primary from Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar and SABINET, covering 2015–2026. An initial search of 10 200 studies was identified, with 10 studies returned for inclusion based on their relevance to the study. A thematic synthesis was undertaken to identify recurring principles, point of convergence and relationship across the literature. These concepts were integrated to develop a conceptual framework positioning Ubuntu as a guiding lens for meaningful community engagement. Results The conceptual synthesis indicates that Ubuntu provides an ethical and relational foundation for research. Its principles of respect, reciprocity, relationality and relational accountability support the co-creation of knowledge, shared decision-making, and sustained engagement. The framework highlights practical strategies such as early stakeholder involvement, collaboration with traditional leaders, knowledge holders, use of local languages, shared decision-making and community-centred dissemination. Discussion Ubuntu challenges extractive researcher-community relationships by emphasising mutual benefit, collective responsibility and respect for indigenous knowledge. Its application can contribute to more equitable and culturally responsive knowledge generation while supporting broader efforts to decolonise research and centre Afrocentric vales and lived realities. Conclusion Ubuntu provides a promising culturally grounded framework for strengthening meaningful community engagement in Indigenous Knowledge Systems research. By positioning communities as co-researcher and knowledge partners, it can shift engagement from procedural compliance towards relational practice. As a conceptual framework, it provides a foundation for empirical testing and practical application across diverse African research contexts.

    Evaluating the Legal Instruments Used in the Conduct of Elections in Nigeria: A Doctrinal and Empirical Analysis [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background The legal framework for Nigeria’s electoral process has been challenged on numerous occasions regarding election-related issues such as voter registration, political funding, voting methods, and the electronic transmission of results. The ability of this framework to support free and fair elections continues to be a subject of dispute. Methods A combination of the doctrinal review of the Constitution, the Electoral Act 2026 and an empirical survey of electoral stakeholders (political party members, INEC staff, legal practitioners, civil society representatives) were conducted on April 2026. Using Cochran’s formula to determine sample size, a target sample size of 385 was determined. This target sample size produced 256 validated questionnaires (66.6%). Results The majority of participants (89.5%) indicated they had knowledge of the existing legal documents. Further, 70.7% believed the legal documents to be adequate for establishing credibility for elections; however, 91.5% of all participants agreed that amendments would be required at some level. The Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS) was identified by the largest percentage of participants (71.5%) as the most recognized form of technology, and further, 53.5% indicated they believed the current forms of technology are lacking. Similarly, there existed a statistically significant relationship between participant perceptions that legal documents provided credible elections and participant perceptions that technology played a significant role. Conclusions Nigeria has a fairly complete legislative structure supporting elections, but that support is weakened by poor implementation of those laws, a continuing disagreement as to whether the collation should be done manually or electronically, and public perception of INEC being dependent on institutions. Doctrinal and empirical reforms include making electronic transmission mandatory (binding).

    Background Country risk for the business sector can manifest itself through higher financing costs, more restricted access to credit and adjustments to the capital structure; whilst international studies have identified effects on corporate investment and indebtedness, the relationships in the case of Peruvian mining companies, as well as the moderating role of profitability and the concentration of short-term debt, remain to be explored. Methods To this end, a quantitative, longitudinal and explanatory-predictive approach was adopted, using fixed-effects panel data models for each firm, comprising 210 firm-year observations. Conclusion It is concluded that the transmission of sovereign risk to companies’ financial structures was neither direct nor uniform, and that exposure to exchange rate risk was a more significant factor in explaining the financial vulnerability of Peruvian mining companies.

    Clarifying the Confusion: A Secondary Method Comparison Analysis of the Insulin Index and C‑Peptide Index in Endocrinology and Clinical Nutrition. [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Introduction Insulin resistance (IR) and hyperinsulinaemia are escalating global health issues linked to numerous diseases and medical conditions. As there are limited medications available to address these issues, dietary approach holds an appeal. This is where the Insulin Index (II) comes into play. The II evaluates the postprandial insulin response to foods, offering valuable insights into their insulinaemic effects. Similarly, the C-Peptide Index (CPI), which assesses the postprandial C-peptide response, is also occasionally utilised in clinical nutrition and metabolic research. Purpose This study presents a secondary quantitative method-comparison analysis evaluating whether the II and the CPI provide interchangeable measures of postprandial β-cell secretory response. Methodology Published articles were used solely as data sources for paired II and CPI measurements. Discussion Less than 30 food pairs with both II and CPI were extracted. A Wilcoxon signed-rank test revealed a notable difference between II and CPI (z = −2.482). The median value of CPI and II was 54.78 and 48.53, respectively. The BA analysis further highlighted substantial bias (4.749) and variability between the two indices. Conclusion These findings imply that II and CPI should not be used interchangeably in clinical and research contexts. From an endocrine perspective, the differential interpretation of these biomarkers reflects distinct aspects of pancreatic β-cell activity. Future research should investigate the comparative differences of various foods within the same cohort of subjects, as well as those with diabetes and varying medication regimens. Furthermore, developing a dedicated CPI database could enhance its clinical usefulness.

    “Eat, Save, Share”: An ESD–Deep Learning Program For Food-Waste Prevention In Adiwiyata Elementary Schools [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    This study reports field results from Eat, Save, Share, a light-touch intervention that pairs cafeteria choice architecture with classroom data-literacy micro-routines in Adiwiyata elementary schools (Bandung, Indonesia). Participants were elementary school students in Grades 4–6, approximately 9–12 years old, across six classes. Over six weeks, we conducted weighed plate-waste audits (not visual estimates), pre–post Sustainability Consciousness Questionnaire (SCQ), observer-rated implementation fidelity, dashboard-based feedback loops, and qualitative observations/interviews. Operational outcomes improved: average plate-waste grams per student per day fell from baseline to endline (≈22–31% reductions), and zero-leftover trays increased (+13 to +18 percentage points). Item-level tracking informed next-week portion right-sizing and serving-order adjustments (e.g., vegetables-first; S/M/L defaults). SCQ rose on the total score (Δ ≈ 0.32) and across subscales Knowingness, Attitudes, and Behaviour with the largest gain in Behaviour (Δ ≈ 0.38), alongside small-to-moderate effects and acceptable reliability. Intention items (portion control, compost/eco-enzyme participation) increased concurrently. Class-level models linked higher fidelity on mindful (portion checks, brief reflections) and meaningful (cross-subject, data-based tasks) routines to larger waste reductions; joyful formats related to stronger behavioural gains. Each additional week of dashboard use predicted more zero-leftover trays, and autonomy-support indicators aligned with higher SCQ totals. Qualitative accounts described calmer queues, fewer untouched items, and pragmatic adaptations (tasting spoons, portion tickets, parent messages) that sustained feasibility. Overall, findings indicate internalization rather than short-term compliance and demonstrate a practical enactment of Education for Sustainable Development’s whole-institution approach. The mechanism is scalable within routine school operations. Future work should examine longer follow-up, cost–benefit, and equity impacts across diverse schools.

    Effectiveness and feasibility of a 12-week Zoom-delivered home-based exercise programme for university students during COVID-19: a quasi-experimental pre–post study [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background During COVID, many Physical Educational (PE) programmes were put on hold. However, to allow students to graduate, and to keep them fit and healthy, home-based exercise (HBE) programmes were needed. Objectives This study measured the effectiveness and feasibility of a 12-week HBE programme delivered via Zoom at an international college in Thailand. The course was held eight times each trimester for nearly two years with up to 25 participants on each course. Methods The study used a one-group pre–post quasi-experimental design. Once a week for nine weeks, participants performed a 45-minute workout of calisthenics and light weights in class, and another 45-minute videoed workout as homework. The volume and intensity of the workouts were progressively increased. There was a pre-test (Week 2) and a post-test (Week 12). The tests consisted of four physical fitness (PF) items: Sit-ups, Push-ups (maximum repetitions in one minute); Squats (maximum repetitions in two minutes); The Plank (number of seconds to exhaustion or a maximum of 120 seconds). The self-reported data [N=841] were analysed via SPSS. Results All four test items showed a 30% or more improvement in performance. The results were very statistically significant (p < .001). For example, for squats for females [N=464], the pre-test mean was 46.4 ± 14.2 and the post test was 60.9 ± 13.8. Effect sizes were large to very large (d = 0.59–1.09). Conclusions In this study, HBE was associated with significant improvements in PF. However, causality is limited by the single-group design and self-report measures. Of note, programmes which combine live coaching, motivational mini-lectures, and varied calisthenics may increase adherence and improve PF. Future studies should test which components drive effect.

    Exploring Cognitive and Affective Outcomes Following Bilateral 6‐Hydroxydopamine Lesions of the Substance Nigra or Striatum in Rats

    Our results demonstrate massive degeneration of tyrosine hydroxylase-immunoreactive neurons was observed in the SNpc, striatum, and ventral tegmental area with 6-OHDA infusions in either the SNpc or striatum. Rats administered 6-OHDA to the SNpc exhibited cognitive impairments, anxiety-like behaviors, and impacted the maturation of newborn hippocampal neurons.


    ABSTRACT

    The majority of animal models of Parkinson's disease (PD) focus on motor symptoms that are induced by unilateral injections of such neurotoxins as 6-hydroxydopamine (6-OHDA) in nigrostriatal dopaminergic pathways. However, motor changes that are induced by unilateral 6-OHDA injections may interfere with the identification of cognitive and affective dysfunctions induced by dopaminergic neurodegeneration. To select an appropriate method for studying nonmotor symptoms of PD and potential neuroprotective treatments, the present study compared behavioral effects of bilateral 6-OHDA infusions directly in the substantia nigra pars compacta (SNpc) or striatum in rats. A battery of behavioral tests, including affective and cognitive tasks, was performed for 22 days after 6-OHDA nigrostriatal lesions. The massive degeneration of tyrosine hydroxylase-immunoreactive neurons was observed in the SNpc, striatum, and ventral tegmental area with 6-OHDA infusions in either the SNpc or striatum. Concerning functional outcomes, 6-OHDA infusions in the striatum decreased general exploratory activity 7 days after the lesion. Rats that received 6-OHDA in the SNpc exhibited cognitive impairments and despair-like behavior. A decrease in the number of newborn neurons was found in the hippocampus in rats that received 6-OHDA in the striatum, indicating a alteration of neuron maturation. 6-OHDA infusions in both the SNpc and striatum impacted the maturation of newborn hippocampal neurons. These results indicate that bilateral injections of 6-OHDA in the SNpc might be useful for studying nonmotor symptoms of PD.
