    Pixel-Wise Statistical Mapping with Voltage-Sensitive Dye Imaging Reveals Differential Hippocampal Circuit Effects of Bisphenol A and Related Phenolic Analogs

    Exposure to environmental chemicals such as bisphenol A (BPA) may affect neural circuit function in subtle and spatially distributed ways that are difficult to attribute to a single circuit element. Here, we developed a voltage-sensitive dye (VSD) imaging analysis pipeline for acute male mouse hippocampal slices. This approach measures stimulus-evoked fractional changes in VSD fluorescence to generate pixel-wise normalized maps, averages response magnitude within anatomically defined regions of interest (ROIs), and visualizes control-versus-compound differences using pixel-wise statistical maps before and after false discovery rate (FDR) adjustment. We applied this pipeline to compare the acute effects of BPA with those of two structurally related phenolic analogs. Evoked responses were quantified by their peak amplitude and time integral across hippocampal trisynaptic pathways at two stimulation levels determined from input–output relationships, with or without the receptor antagonist gabazine (SR-95531; 10 µM). The phenolic analogs reduced circuit responses across multiple ROI-defined conditions, whereas BPA produced comparatively limited effects. These alterations varied with pathway, subfield, age, response metric, and inhibitory state. These findings demonstrate the utility of integrating ROI averages with pixel-wise statistical maps for detecting condition-dependent circuit-level differences between structurally related compounds and indicate that BPA-related phenolic analogs can differ from BPA itself in their acute effects on hippocampal circuitry.

    Numerical Evaluation of the Coupling Between Several Directional Coupler Designs [version 2; peer review: 1 approved with reservations]

    Background Power division, switching, modulation, and wavelength multiplexing in integrated photonics are made possible via directional couplers. Nonlinear effects, material characteristics, and waveguide geometry all affect how they couple. Because of their microstructure cladding, photonic crystal fibre (PCF) couplers provide stronger field confinement and possibly better coupling than traditional two-core waveguides. A numerical comparison of linear and nonlinear coupling in waveguide and PCF couplers is presented in this paper. Methods Coupled Mode Theory was used to simulate the neighbouring-core interaction, and COMSOL's FEM was used to obtain even and odd supermodes. For both coupler types, effective indices, coupling coefficients, and coupling lengths were retrieved. Evaluation of nonlinear behaviour, such as power-dependent decoupling and critical power thresholds, was made possible by incorporating self-phase modulation into the CMT equations. Results The PCF coupler provided substantially stronger coupling than the standard waveguide. At a wavelength of 1.55 μm, the PCF attained a coupling length of 1107 μm and a coupling coefficient of 0.001418 μm−1, compared to 3875 μm and 0.000405 μm−1 for the waveguide. Improved field localization and intercore interactions increase coupling in PCFs. Nonlinear calculations revealed that the PCF requires less critical power (29 kW) to accomplish decoupling than the waveguide (83 kW). Conclusion Both architectures showed reduced intercore transfer at high powers due to nonlinear phase mismatch, consistent with Jensen’s hypothesis. PCF couplers outperform the traditional waveguides in both linear and nonlinear regimes. This work provides a direct, side-by-side comparison of a conventional integrated waveguide coupler and a microstructured Photonic Crystal Fiber (PCF) coupler. We map out exactly how structural geometry fundamentally dictates the scaling of the nonlinear coefficient ( γ ) and alters the critical switching threshold ( Pc ), which gives the potential of nonlinear PCF couplers for use in high-speed optical communication, switching, modulation, multiplexing, and wavelength division multiplexing (WDM) applications.

    Spectrum of Lower Respiratory Tract Infections and Antimicrobial Resistance Pattern in Head and Neck Cancer Patients Undergoing Chemoradiation [version 3; peer review: 2 approved, 1 approved with reservations]

    Background Head and neck cancer (HNCs) is a major health issue worldwide, and India has contributed to approximately 2.4 lakh new cases in 2022. Definitive chemoradiation is the standard treatment for locally advanced disease but carries a risk of lower respiratory tract infections (LRTI) that add to morbidity, hospitalization, cost, and possible delay in treatment. The increasing incidence of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) has also contributed to management burden. This study aimed to assess the microbiological profiles and antimicrobial resistance patterns of lower respiratory tract infections in patients with head and neck cancer receiving chemoradiation. Methods Patients who underwent definitive radiotherapy with or without chemotherapy and who developed LRTIs were included in the study. Sputum and tracheostomy suction tip cultures were obtained and processed using standard microbiological techniques such as Gram staining, biochemical tests, and VITEK-2 automated systems. Antimicrobial susceptibility was tested according to the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) and EUCAST recommendations. Clinical and treatment-related factors were documented and compared using SPSS version 23.0, with descriptive statistics, chi-square tests, t-tests, ANOVA, and logistic regression models. Results Pseudomonas aeruginosa was the most frequently isolated pathogen (35.0%), followed by Klebsiella pneumoniae (16.7%), and Acinetobacter baumannii (10.0%). The pathogens were strongly resistant to fluoroquinolones and third-generation cephalosporins but were susceptible to carbapenems and aminoglycosides in the majority of isolates. Pseudomonas aeruginosa was the most frequent pathogen in all age groups and chemotherapy regimens (p<0.001). Conclusion LRTIs in patients with HNC treated with chemoradiation were mainly caused by multidrug-resistant Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Klebsiella pneumoniae. Resistance patterns are crucial for directing empirical antibiotic therapy, minimizing treatment delays, and enhancing clinical outcomes.

    The Phantom Naturalist: What do we know about the nineteenth-century natural history collector Alfred Crossley? [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Born in Halifax, UK, in 1839, and shipwrecked on Madagascar as a youth, Alfred Crossley had returned to the island by 1869 and in the ensuing eight years made hugely important collections of mammals, birds, butterflies, and other natural history specimens. Many of these are now in major museums in the UK and Europe (including numerous holotypes), but unknown quantities went to private collectors and are now lost. Unfortunately, Crossley’s original documentation has also disappeared; and apart from his collections themselves he left few traces of his journeyings, which included Cameroon and Mauritius (at least) as well as Madagascar. This article gathers everything that is currently known about the collector and his travels in order to reconstitute his collecting itineraries, to determine his specimen localities, to understand why and how he left so few traces, and to correct some previously published misperceptions. It also attempts to shed light on Crossley’s mysterious death in Madagascar in 1877.

    Predictors of Nursing Informatics Competency Among Registered Nurses Working in Clinical Settings in Tamil Nadu, India [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background The integration of digital health technologies requires nurses to possess informatics competency to support safe, high-quality care. In India, evidence on factors associated with Nursing Informatics competency among nurses remains limited. This study assessed competency levels and identified independent factors associated with lower competency. Objective To describe professional characteristics, assess Nursing Informatics competency, and identify factors associated with competency using multivariable regression analysis. Methods A quantitative cross-sectional study was conducted among 400 registered nurses working in two government hospitals in Tamil Nadu, India. Participants were recruited using convenience sampling. Data were collected using a questionnaire and the Nursing Informatics Competency Assessment Tool (NICAT), comprising computer literacy, informatics literacy, and informatics management domains. Targeted competency-based training and workplace exposure to digital health systems are recommended, particularly for experienced nurses.

    Background Public financial management has increasingly moved beyond a narrow compliance-oriented perspective toward broader governance arrangements involving transparency, oversight, participation, digitalization, and multiple accountability actors. Methods A bibliometric-systematic literature review was conducted using Scopus-indexed journal articles published in English between 2022 and 2026. A second-stage screening identified 39 studies with direct thematic relevance for the systematic review and in-depth full-text thematic synthesis. Results Publication output increased markedly after 2023 and peaked in 2025. Conclusions Collaborative financial governance is conceptualized as an integrated governance architecture in which these six domains operate as complementary mechanisms. The framework extends conventional public financial management by linking institutional control, fiscal openness, stakeholder involvement, digital support, and accountability to strengthen the legitimacy, responsiveness, and effectiveness of public resource management.

    A Decade of the Journey of MPDSR Implementation in Bangladesh: Best Practices And Innovations [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background The implementation of Maternal and Perinatal Death Surveillance and Response (MPDSR) in Bangladesh has evolved significantly since its inception in 2010. This study reviews the best practices in MPDSR, focusing on its process, implementation, scale-up, and innovations over a decade of implementation. Methods The study also conducted a comprehensive review of government national MPDSR reports from 2019 to 2024. Additionally, national MPDSR guidelines, avialble published literatures on MPDSR in Bangladesh, government reports, and meeting minutes were examined. Results MPDSR system in Bangladesh began as a pilot project in one district and expanded gradullay to cover all 64 districts by 2024. Initially focusing on maternal and neonatal deaths, MPDSR later incorporated stillbirths into its scope of works. Over the study period from 2019 to 2024, MPDSR reported 2927 maternal deaths and 7250 neonatal deaths, achieving average review rates of 83% for maternal deaths and 55% for neonatal deaths. Implementation operates at both community and facility levels, with timely death notification and verbal autopsies at the community level, involving frontline health workers and social autopsies to engage communities. Facility-level notification within 24 hours, facility death reviews are conducted within seven days, involving health care providers in the health facility. MPDSR committees oversee data review and local actions with a non-blaming approach. Revised National Guideline in 2021 aligned with global standards, emphasizing stillbirth reviews, midwives’ roles, and community engagement, overcoming blame culture etc. MPDSR video conferences found useful and effective for remote monitoring and developed video toolkits for consistent capacity development of healthcare providers. Integration into the DHIS-2 system allows real-time data monitoring and analysis across districts. Conclusions Bangladesh’s MPDSR system has made significant steps in reducing maternal mortality, integrating midwives, engaging communities, and deploying innovative tools, contributing to global efforts to achieve Sustainable Development Goals.

    Hackathon on predictive modelling in systems pharmacology: design, implementation, and outcomes [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background The growing availability of multi-omics data and machine learning methods creates both an opportunity and a challenge for the systems pharmacology community: the opportunity to model drug action at unprecedented mechanistic resolution, and the challenge of equipping a new generation of researchers with the computational literacy to do so. There is a particular need for training formats that bridge classical pharmacometrics with omics data science, and that foster interdisciplinary community building. Methods We organised a 4-day intensive hackathon on Predictive Modelling in Systems Pharmacology, bringing together 16 early-career researchers from the Horizon Europe CANVAS and French PEPR DIGPHAT consortia, spanning biology, bioinformatics, and computational science. The event pursued the dual aim of fostering a multidisciplinary consortium community and providing hands-on training that bridges classical pharmacometrics with omics data science. Three scientific tracks were delivered: (i) pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) modelling, (ii) drug perturbation modelling through network diffusion in gene regulatory networks (GRN), and (iii) machine learning for pharmacogenomics. A theory-to-practice pedagogical framework structured each day, with morning workshops grounding participants in methodological foundations followed by afternoon coding sprints on real-world datasets. Results All three tracks produced working computational outputs within the hackathon timeframe. In the PK/PD track, all teams successfully identified a two-compartment structural model and key covariate effects. In the GRN track, participants implemented a network diffusion algorithm on gene regulatory networks, yielding patient-stratified therapy recommendations. In the pharmacogenomics track, four out of seven teams surpassed the logistic regression baseline in Matthews Correlation Coefficient on an external validation set. Conclusions The hackathon demonstrated that a structured multi-track format combining community-targeted outreach, theory-to-practice pedagogy, and real-world datasets constitutes a replicable model for interdisciplinary computational pharmacology training at the European level.

    Diagnostic Accuracy of Pure-Tone Audiometry and Otoacoustic Emissions for School-Based Hearing Screening in Children: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background School-based hearing screening can identify hearing loss that develops after birth or becomes apparent later in childhood. Pure-tone audiometry (PTA) and otoacoustic emissions (OAE) are widely used, but diagnostic performance varies across thresholds, technologies, populations and reference standards. Methods We searched PubMed, Scopus, the Cochrane Library and Google Scholar from database inception to 19 September 2026, with supplementary discovery using Elicit, OpenAlex and ASTA. A single reviewer conducted screening, extraction and risk-of-bias assessment, with structured second-pass verification. Eligible studies evaluated PTA or OAE in school-based or school-entry settings against an appropriate audiological reference standard and reported interpretable diagnostic-accuracy data. Risk of bias was assessed using QUADAS-2. PTA studies with reconstructable 2 × 2 data were synthesised using a Reitsma-type bivariate random-effects model fitted by restricted maximum likelihood; OAE evidence was synthesised narratively. Results The search identified 2,198 records; 262 duplicates were removed and 1,936 unique records were screened. Fifty-five reports were sought, 26 were not retrieved and 29 full texts were assessed. Fifteen reports representing 14 unique studies were included. Six independent PTA datasets were included in the primary meta-analysis. Pooled sensitivity was 77.2% (95% CI 58.3–89.1%) and pooled specificity was 92.5% (95% CI 74.8–98.1%). Replacing the 15-dB Robler threshold with the 20-dB threshold yielded sensitivity of 75.3% and specificity of 93.2%. Prediction ranges were wide, indicating substantial between-study heterogeneity. Eight studies were classified as having high overall QUADAS-2 concern. OAE evidence was too heterogeneous and sparse for a defensible pooled estimate. Conclusions PTA shows relatively high specificity but variable sensitivity for school-based hearing screening. Performance depends on screening threshold, implementation, population and reference standard. Current OAE evidence is insufficient for a reliable pooled estimate. Future studies should use standardised thresholds, complete verification, clearly defined reference standards and full 2 × 2 reporting.

    FlyHack: Stress-Testing Mechanistic Claims in a Connectome-Constrained Visual Model

    arXiv:2610.13018v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Connectome-constrained neural networks make biological components directly accessible to intervention, but their mechanistic conclusions can still depend on the comparison model, intervention, circuit representation and trained solution. We stress-test these dependencies in flyvis, a \emph{Drosophila} motion-vision model, using its 50-model ensemble, task-matched causal CNN controls and replicated interventions. At the level of model behaviour, three-level reverse-phi reversal in flyvis is strongly period- and velocity-dependent and also appears in task-matched CNNs; neither family reproduces the fly's stationary-sawtooth response. Adversarial direction reversal requires a perturbation bound of 0.21--0.27 times stimulus contrast in flyvis versus 0.13--0.25 in CNNs, but the gap over a parameter-matched CNN largely closes for larger CNNs. Circuit conclusions also vary with representation and ensemble member. Greedy cell-type sets meet a model-relative T4/T5 tuning criterion in 25 of 50 models, while T4/T5 output silencing reduces the tested optic-flow behaviour to at most two of eight clips in 40. In six models where the cell-type search first succeeds, the corresponding connection sets can be pruned to 13--31 added classes; under an additional two-sided response-amplitude check, only two of those six first-success cell-type sets still meet the tuning threshold. These results support a narrower use of connectome-constrained models: they make mechanistic hypotheses directly testable, but the resulting claims must be scoped to the model family, intervention, representation and trained solution.

    Excitability across scales: a unifying EI model

    arXiv:2610.13275v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Neuronal excitability is commonly modeled using different principles at the cellular and population scales, making it difficult to study how microscopic mechanisms shape macroscopic activity. We introduce a spatiotemporal excitatory-inhibitory (EI) model that unifies these scales through a common mixed-feedback architecture. The model retains the conductance-based circuit structure of Hodgkin-Huxley models and describes intrinsic and synaptic currents through memductances whose temporal and spatial voltage dependence encodes dynamics at the relevant scale. In its minimal form, temporal excitability arises from fast excitatory feedback opposed by slower inhibitory feedback, while spatial excitability arises from short-range excitation opposed by longer-range inhibition, recovering the core lateral-inhibition motif of Amari neural fields. We show that combining these mechanisms gives rise to population excitability in strongly heterogeneous populations. In particular, the model generates population spikes that are localized in both space and time and possess population-level scales distinct from those of individual neuronal spikes, despite substantial cell-to-cell parameter variability. We then show that both intrinsic temporal excitability and spatial synaptic feedback are required for coherent mean-field events and robust spatiotemporal population rhythms. Finally, selective modulation of memductances in a subset of the population tunes the frequency of the population rhythm, illustrating how changes at the cellular scale can shape macroscopic dynamics. By separating circuit architecture from modeling scale, the proposed framework provides a physically interpretable approach to analyzing and designing neuronal excitability across scales.

    Decoding Alignment without Encoding Alignment: A critique of similarity analysis in neuroscience

    arXiv:2605.05907v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: RSA and CKA are standard metrics for comparing neural representations across brain regions, organisms, and deep learning models. We demonstrate a fundamental weakness: these decoding-based metrics are insensitive to encoding manifold topology---the internal functional organization of a neural population. In a controlled MNIST experiment, RSA, CKA, and Procrustes $R^2$ remain statistically unchanged when encoding topology is causally manipulated via an auxiliary clustering loss, while the two model populations differ significantly in attribution patterns, weight-graph assortativity, and out-of-distribution robustness.     Cultural evolution shaped human's uniquely sensitive disgust avoidance

    arXiv:2008.13211v5 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Disgust is an emotion that serves to avoid contaminants, and is central to the behavioural immune system. While disgust-motivated avoidance occurs in bonobos and chimpanzees, humans show uniquely high levels of contamination sensitivity. There is some disagreement over to what extent human disgust avoidance is a genetic adaptation or shaped through social transmission, and whether parental modelling plays a role in the latter. Here, we address this debate by simulating 100,000 years of cultural and biological evolution in various scenarios. We modelled disgust as a trait that governed the extent to which individuals forewent potentially contaminated nutrition. This lowered their risk of gastrointestinal illness, at the cost of increasing starvation risk and birth-interval. As environmental levels and costs of contamination rose, so did relative fitness of high disgust-avoidant individuals. Crucially, cultural transmission between generations operated at a higher rate, even if parental modelling was eliminated. These results underscore the plausibility of cultural evolution's role in shaping human's uniquely sensitive disgust avoidance, and serves as an illustration of emerging theories that paint affective and cognitive mechanisms as socially transmitted rather than genetic adaptations.

    ABO Blood Group and HIV Infection in Sub-Saharan Africa: Disentangling Biological Signal from Population-Level Confounding [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) bears the world’s greatest HIV burden, prompting sustained interest in host genetic modifiers of susceptibility, including the ABO blood group system. Yet reported associations between ABO phenotype and HIV infection remain inconsistent, and whether they reflect true biological effects or uncontrolled population structure is unresolved. Methods We conducted a PRISMA-informed structured narrative review of studies examining ABO phenotype and HIV infection in SSA, searched across PubMed/MEDLINE, Google Scholar, Scopus, Web of Science, Embase, and African Journals Online. Given marked heterogeneity in design, denominators, and reporting across eligible studies, a formal meta-analysis was not undertaken. Instead, for studies reporting extractable population-level ABO frequencies, we explicitly tested whether HIV-associated phenotype patterns were distinguishable from background distribution. Results Fifteen studies from nine SSA countries met inclusion criteria. Blood group O was the most prevalent phenotype both in the general population and among HIV-positive individuals in every study reporting background data. Among six studies with quantifiable denominators, only one (Korir et al., Kenya) used an independent background comparator; its HIV-positive proportion (45.6%, 95% CI 39.8–51.4%) fell squarely within the background range (45–49%), showing no detectable excess. The remaining five studies derived HIV and background estimates from the same sample, precluding independent confounding assessment. A notable outlier — 46.2% blood group AB among an HIV-exposed infant cohort (Okonkwo et al.) versus an expected 3–5% — represented the largest deviation observed, though based on a small subgroup requiring replication. Only two of fifteen studies reported adjusted estimates. Conclusion Existing SSA evidence does not support a reproducible, confounding-independent association between ABO phenotype and HIV infection; apparent group-O associations largely reflect background prevalence. Standardized reporting of adjusted estimates and population phenotype distributions is needed going forward.
