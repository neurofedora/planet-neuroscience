    Financial Architecture, Government Effectiveness and Carbon Emission Risk in Foreign Capital Mobilization in Sub-Saharan Africa [version 2; peer review: 1 approved with reservations, 1 not approved]

    Background Sub-Saharan Africa faces a persistent external-finance constraint that limits investment, employment, technology transfer and progress toward sustainable development. Rather than treating the regressors as separate determinant questions, this study investigates a broader empirical phenomenon: whether foreign capital systematically responds to domestic financial architecture, institutional credibility and environmental conditions in heterogeneous Sub-Saharan African economies. Methods Annual data for 44 Sub-Saharan African economies from 2004 to 2022 were analysed using a sequential panel design. Cross-sectional-dependence and slope-heterogeneity diagnostics motivate second-generation CIPS unit-root testing, Westerlund cointegration and heterogeneous estimation. CS-ARDL serves as the baseline estimator; CCEMG and AMG assess estimator robustness, System GMM addresses dynamic endogeneity concerns, panel quantile regression examines distributional heterogeneity and Dumitrescu-Hurlin tests assess Granger-predictive direction. Results Financial inclusion, the domestic-credit proxy interpreted conservatively as financial intermediation capacity, and government effectiveness are positively associated with both FDI and remittance inflows in the long run. In the FDI equation, the positive CO2 term and negative squared term indicate concavity. The implied turning point is approximately 28.0 metric tons of CO2 per capita, above the sample maximum of 9.814; therefore, the estimates do not establish a within-sample inverted-U reversal. Carbon emissions are negatively associated with remittances. Conclusions The results support a common pattern of foreign-capital responsiveness to domestic conditions, while the significant slope heterogeneity implies that reported coefficients are average heterogeneous effects rather than identical relationships for all SSA economies. Policy implications are therefore framed as evidence-consistent priorities rather than causal prescriptions.

    Environmental Action-Based Learning for Sustainability Competencies: EMKONTAN Model on Environmental Literacy, Creative Thinking, and Collaboration among Pre-Service Biology Teachers [version 3; peer review: 2 approved]

    Background Sustainability transitions require educators who can translate environmental knowledge into creative, collaborative, and action-oriented learning. This study examined the effectiveness of the Environmental Mapping, Conservation, Action, and Evaluation learning model (EMKONTAN) in strengthening environmental literacy, creative thinking, and collaboration skills among pre-service biology teachers. Methods A quasi-experimental pretest-posttest non-equivalent control group design was implemented in an environmental science course involving three learning conditions: EMKONTAN, problem-based learning, and regular instruction. Of 120 eligible students, 108 students with complete pretest and posttest data were included in the analysis. Environmental literacy was measured using an adapted environmental literacy instrument, creative thinking through essay-based tasks, and collaboration through an observation rubric. One-way analysis of covariance was used to compare adjusted posttest means after controlling for pretest scores, followed by least significant difference post hoc testing. Results The EMKONTAN group achieved the highest adjusted means for environmental literacy (76.30), creative thinking (96.18), and collaboration (78.89). The learning model had statistically significant effects on environmental literacy, F(2,104) = 126.54, p < .001, partial eta squared = .709; creative thinking, F(2,104) = 274.51, p < .001, partial eta squared = .841; and collaboration, F(2,104) = 161.33, p < .001, partial eta squared = .756. Conclusions These findings suggest that an environmental action-based learning cycle that integrates problem identification, field observation, action planning, implementation, monitoring, evaluation, and follow-up can support sustainability competencies in teacher education. The study contributes a contextual instructional model for preparing future biology teachers to design participatory environmental learning in developing-country higher education settings.

    Gamification and the Development of Metacognitive Processes: A Systematic Literature Review [version 3; peer review: 1 approved, 1 approved with reservations]

    Background Gamification has encouraged growing interest in educational research because of its potential to improve motivation, participation, and self-regulated learning. However, although gamification has been widely studied in education, the specific relationship between gamification and metacognitive processes remains conceptually fragmented and inconsistently operationalized across empirical studies. This systematic literature review examines how gamification-based strategies support metacognitive processes, such as planning, monitoring, and evaluation, in educational contexts (García-López et al., 2023; Gkintoni et al., 2024). Methods This review was conducted using the PRISMA 2020 methodology (Page et al., 2021). Searches were conducted in Scopus and the Web of Science using the terms gamification, education, and metacognition. Only peer-reviewed, open-access articles published between 2019 and 2024 were included. After removing duplicates (n = 115), 253 records were examined by title and abstract screening, 181 were excluded because of irrelevance and 72 full-text articles were assessed for eligibility; one article was excluded at this stage due to confirmed plagiarism. Finally, 71 studies were included in the final thematic synthesis. A thematic analysis was used to extract information on theoretical frameworks, methodologies, metacognitive components, and higher-order cognitive processes. Results The findings revealed that gamification was frequently associated with metacognition by activating processes, such as self-regulation, monitoring, and decision-making. The central elements of gamification, such as immediate feedback, progressive challenges, autonomy, and reward systems, were commonly reported alongside outcomes related to metacognitive awareness and active participation. However, many studies did not explicitly specify the instructional mechanism or the metacognitive scaffolding used, which limits the precision of attributing metacognitive effects directly to gamification. The review studies also reported higher-order thinking outcomes (e.g., problem-solving and critical thinking) particularly when gamified activities incorporated cognitive challenges and structured feedback (Ask et al., 2023). Future research should expand the scope of these databases and include non-open-access studies to reduce potential biases. Conclusions Gamification strengthens metacognitive processes when instructional design integrates meaningful goals, feedback mechanisms, cognitive challenges, and reflection opportunities. Evidence suggests that educational environments based on gamification are effective in promoting the planning, monitoring, and evaluation skills essential for autonomous learning and metacognitive development. Future research should expand the scope of these databases and include non-open-access studies to reduce potential biases.

    Assessing Data Management and Compliance in Large Research Collaborations via Knowledge Bases:  A Semi-Structured Interview Approach [version 2; peer review: 2 approved with reservations]

    Background Large-scale research collaborations rely not only on robust research strategies but also on structured (meta) data management and systematic knowledge exchange. Ensuring compliance with ethical and legal requirements is essential from the beginning. This includes obtaining informed consent and adhering to data protection laws such as the GDPR (for Europe), as well as following Open Science and FAIR principles, particularly when working with personal data. Additionally, the systematic assessment and documentation of project objectives, data characteristics, and other project-specific features are essential for supporting the coordination and long-term development of such collaborations. Methods In this paper, we introduce a methodology designed to identify commonalities across research projects and to enhance data governance within large research consortia. The approach consists of three components: (1) a semi-structured interview designed to help communicate ethical obligations, data protection requirements, and Open Science principles to researchers while systematically collecting metadata on planned studies, data types, participant groups, and methodological procedures; (2) structured processing and organization of the collected information; and (3) visualization of project interrelations through knowledge graphs. The methodology was implemented within a collaborative research centre in linguistics. Results The collected metadata were systematically structured and used to construct knowledge graphs capturing interrelations among projects, data types, methodologies, and participant groups. These visualizations support the identification of potential collaborations, infrastructure planning, and data reuse. Conclusions The proposed methodology offers a systematic workflow to assess data management practices, while supporting compliance and transparency from early stages of collaborative research. Knowledge graph visualizations provide a practical tool for identifying potential synergies, and supporting data reuse.

    A guide to selecting high-performing antibodies for PKN2 (UniProt ID: Q16513) for use in western blot and flow cytometry [version 2; peer review: 1 approved with reservations]

    Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis is a chronic, progressive interstitial lung disease characterised by fibrotic remodelling of the distal alveolar interstitium and irreversible loss of lung function. Rapid decline in forced vital capacity has been significantly associated with rs115982800, a single nucleotide polymorphism in PKN2. Identifying high-quality research antibodies is therefore essential to enable robust investigation of PKN2 biology and its translational potential. Here, we systematically evaluated eleven commercial antibodies for western blot and flow cytometry using a standardised knockout-validation approach in human HCT 116 cells, comparing readouts in PKN2 knockout lines with isogenic parental controls. These experiments are part of a broader collaborative initiative addressing antibody reproducibility by characterising commercial antibodies for human proteins and making the results openly available. While antibody performance and protocol conditions may vary between laboratories, this study provides a resource to guide selection of the most suitable reagents for investigations of PKN2 in health and disease.

    Family Dynamics And Pro-Social Competence Of College Students In Osun State, Nigeria [version 2; peer review: 1 not approved]

    Background Pro-social competence, which includes behaviours such as cooperation, helping others, tolerance, and social responsibility, is essential for students’ successful social adjustment and interpersonal relationships. Family environments are often considered important in shaping these competencies; however, evidence regarding the influence of family dynamics on pro-social competence among college students remains limited. This study investigated the relationship between family dynamics and pro-social competence among college students in Osun State, Nigeria. Methods A descriptive survey research design of the correlational type was adopted. The study involved 400 college students selected through simple random sampling. Data were collected using a structured questionnaire measuring family dynamics, including socio-economic status, parenting styles, and family structure, as well as students’ pro-social competence. Descriptive statistics, including frequencies and percentages, were used to determine the level of pro-social competence among respondents. Multiple regression analysis was employed to examine the relationship between family dynamics and pro-social competence. Results The findings revealed that the level of pro-social competence among college students was generally high, with 42.3% of respondents demonstrating a high level of pro-social competence. Regression analysis indicated that family dynamics, comprising socio-economic status, parenting styles, and family structure, did not significantly predict pro-social competence among the students (R = 0.079, p > 0.05). The results suggest that variations in family dynamics were not associated with significant differences in students’ pro-social competence. Conclusions The study concluded that family dynamics, as measured by socio-economic status, parenting styles, and family structure, were not significant determinants of pro-social competence among college students in Osun State, Nigeria. These findings imply that factors beyond the family environment may play a more substantial role in the development of pro-social competence among college students. Further studies are recommended to explore other social, educational, and individual factors that may influence pro-social development.

    Seated balance training in older adults with the Balanseat, a motorized chair device; A Randomized Controlled Trial [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background With an aging population and increasing workforce shortages, technological innovations may provide valuable support in addressing balance and gait impairments in older adults. The Balanseat is a motorized chair-like device designed to improve balance and gait through contralateral movements of the trunk, pelvis, and thighs. Previous studies suggest potential benefits, but evidence from controlled trials is lacking. Methods This randomized controlled trial evaluated therapy adherence to Balanseat training and its effects on gait, balance, and fear of falling in older adults with mobility problems. Thirty-three older adults were randomized to either Balanseat training (20 minutes, twice weekly for six weeks) or usual daily activities. Outcomes included therapy adherence, the Timed Up and Go test (TUG), 10-Meter Walk Test (10MWT), Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB), Functional Reach (FR), and Falls Efficacy Scale (FES). Results Therapy adherence was 94%, with 67% of participants completing all sessions. No statistically significant effects on gait, balance, or fear of falling were demonstrated. Mean changes in the intervention group did not exceed the respective minimal detectable changes. No adverse events occurred. Conclusions Excellent therapy adherence to Balanseat training was observed. No statistically significant effects on gait, balance, or fear of falling were demonstrated, although the smaller-than-planned sample limits conclusions regarding these functional outcomes. Future studies should consider adaptations to the training protocol and be adequately powered to evaluate functional outcomes. Trial registration (17-12-2020): https://trialsearch.who.int/Trial2.aspx?TrialID=NL-OMON52787.

    Digital Mindfulness as a Pedagogical Strategy for Student Engagement in Hybrid Learning: A Systematic Review [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Hybrid learning requires students to sustain attention, regulate emotions, and use technology purposefully across physical and virtual spaces. Digital mindfulness has mainly been studied as a means of reducing psychological distress; its role as a pedagogical strategy for engagement has not been systematically synthesised. We aimed to identify the forms of digital mindfulness reported in relation to student engagement, how interventions are designed and implemented, the mechanisms through which they support engagement, and the direction of reported effects. Methods A systematic review following the PRISMA 2020 statement was conducted across Scopus, Web of Science, and ERIC for records published between January 2021 and July 2026. Eligible studies were English-language research articles in Scopus-indexed journals reporting quantitative or mixed-methods evidence. Screening was performed independently by two reviewers (Cohen’s kappa = 0.81), methodological quality was appraised with the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool, and the evidence was analysed through thematic and narrative synthesis. The review followed PRISMA 2020 and was registered retrospectively on the Open Science Framework (https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/ZNSMK). Results From 412 initial records, 14 articles met the inclusion criteria. Three forms of digital mindfulness were identified: psychological or dispositional capacity (36%), structured digital interventions (36%), and mindful agency in technology use (28%), operationalised through learning management systems, mobile applications, and guided practices supported by monitoring and mentoring. Digital mindfulness supported engagement by strengthening attention regulation, emotional management, motivation, self-efficacy, autonomy, participation, and persistence, with 79% of studies reporting positive effects, 14% limited effects, and 7% no significant relationship. Conclusions The study proposes an integrative conceptual model linking digital mindfulness to the behavioural, cognitive, and emotional dimensions of engagement, with practical implications for hybrid learning design. The evidence is limited by heterogeneous engagement measures and the dominance of online-learning and Asian research contexts.

    MRI-based machine learning models for differentiating Parkinson’s disease and Progressive Supranuclear Palsy [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Parkinson’s disease (PD) and Progressive Supranuclear Palsy (PSP) are neurodegenerative disorder which have Parkinsonism as core clinical feature. PD and PSP are often misdiagnosed in early phases by conventional magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Hence the objective of the study is to evaluate accuracy of MRI based machine learning (ML) models for differentiating PD and PSP. Methods This is a retrospective study including total of 96 subjects (32 PD, 32 PSP and 32 healthy controls). The basal nuclei region such as substantia nigra (SN), caudate nucleus (CN), putamen, globus pallidus (GP), red nucleus (RN), and subthalamic nucleus (STN) were manually segmented in T2 weighted fluid attenuation inversion recovery (T2W-FLAIR) images and radiomics features (RF) were extracted. The top 20 RF were used to train and test ML models. Performance measures of ML models for differentiating PD, PSP and HC were evaluated. Results For differentiating PD from PSP, SN consistently showed highest performance achieving accuracy, F1score and AUC values of 0.95–1.00 across all the tested ML models. RN and STN also demonstrated near perfect classification with several ML models with accuracy and AUC values of 1.00. CN, putamen and GP showed moderate performance (accuracy 38% - 85%). Among classifiers, AdaBoost provided robust performance achieving AUC up to 0.89 and MCC up to 0.72 in test data sets. Conclusion MRI based radiomics models from SN, STN and RN provide the most accurate classification of PD and PSP. It can also help in diagnosis of PD and PSP which are undifferentiable in early stages of disease.

    Análisis por HPLC y capacidad antioxidante in vitro del extracto acuoso de Desmodium molliculum (Kunth) DC de Ayacucho [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Desmodium molliculum is an Andean medicinal plant traditionally used for various ailments, whose chemical composition and antioxidant activity require investigation. To determine the phytochemical profile using high-performance liquid chromatography with diode array detection (HPLC-DAD) and to evaluate the antioxidant activity of the aqueous extract of D. molliculum from Ayacucho, Peru. Methods Plant material was collected in Pansa, Ayacucho (2,524 m above sea level). The aqueous extract was obtained by decoction and concentration. Phytochemical characterization was performed by HPLC-DAD using a C18 column; antioxidant activity was assessed by the 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) radical scavenging assay and the 2,2′-azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid) (ABTS) assay (25–500 μg/mL), using Trolox as a reference standard, and was complemented with the ferric reducing antioxidant power (FRAP) assay. Conclusions D. molliculum exhibits a phytochemical profile rich in glycosylated flavonoids and phenolic acids with strong antioxidant activity.

    Beyond the eye patch: binocular game therapy versus occlusion for amblyopia in children aged 3–17 years — a systematic review of visual acuity, treatment efficiency, and adherence [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Amblyopia is the most common cause of monocular visual impairment in children. Patching the stronger eye has been the mainstay of treatment for decades, but it is burdensome and adherence is often poor. Binocular and dichoptic digital therapies have emerged as alternatives that engage both eyes simultaneously. This review compared binocular game therapy with patching for improving visual acuity in children with amblyopia. Methods We searched PubMed/MEDLINE, Cochrane Library, ScienceDirect, Web of Science, ClinicalTrials.gov, and the ISRCTN registry on 8 February 2025, with an updated search on 14 June 2026. Randomised controlled trials (RCTs) of children aged 3–17 years with amblyopia were eligible. Two reviewers screened records and extracted data independently using Rayyan and Zotero. Risk of bias was assessed with Cochrane RoB 2, and certainty of evidence with GRADE. Because of substantial clinical heterogeneity in treatment dose, duration, and outcome timing, we synthesised findings narratively rather than by meta-analysis. Results Seventeen RCTs involving 1,484 children were included. Visual acuity gains ranged from 0.074 to 0.33 logMAR for binocular games (2–24 weeks), 0.065 to 0.28 logMAR for patching (2–24 weeks), and 0.07 to 0.28 logMAR for passive dichoptic viewing (2–16 weeks). The largest single-device gains were with Occlu-tab (0.33 logMAR) and supervised virtual reality (0.30 logMAR). Most between-group differences were not statistically significant. Passive viewing and supervised platforms often matched patching with fewer treatment hours and higher adherence. Objective monitoring revealed that parental diaries overestimated patching adherence by up to 49%. Conclusions Binocular game therapy appears non-inferior to patching in the short term, with better adherence and greater treatment efficiency per hour. Passive dichoptic viewing may deliver early gains with less total treatment time. However, heterogeneity across devices, doses, and outcome measures, together with limited long-term follow-up, prevents firm conclusions. Larger, multicentre trials with standardised outcomes and extended follow-up are needed. Registration: Not registered.

    Assessing the Potential of Spirogyra as a Sustainable Biodiesel Feedstock: A Systematic Literature Review and Experimental Characterization of Its Fatty Acid Profile [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Freshwater filamentous algae are increasingly investigated as non-food biomass for renewable fuels, yet evidence on the use of Spirogyra as a biodiesel feedstock remains fragmented across cultivation, extraction, conversion, and fuel-evaluation studies. This study integrated a systematic literature review, a bibliometric analysis, and experimental characterization to assess the research landscape and the potential of Spirogyra sp. as a feedstock. A predefined Scopus search identified 81 records; after removal of one duplicate and three ineligible records, 77 studies were included. Biblioshiny/Bibliometrix and VOSviewer were used to analyze publication trends, thematic structure, and research networks. A dried Spirogyra sp. sample obtained from Cianjur, Indonesia, was characterized for fatty acids according to AOAC 969.33, screened for polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, and evaluated by Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR). The literature was dominated by biodiesel, biomass, transesterification, bioethanol, fermentation, fatty acids, pyrolysis, and nutrient-related topics, with publication activity peaking in 2023. The sample contained 31.56% w/w quantified fatty acids on the laboratory’s concentration-in-fat basis, with palmitic acid (C16:0) dominant at 25.54% w/w. Calculated SFA, MUFA, and PUFA totals were 28.78%, 2.39%, and 0.39% w/w, respectively. FTIR bands indicated hydroxyl, aliphatic C-H, carbonyl, and C-O/C-O-C functionalities, consistent with mixed lipid and carbohydrate components but not diagnostic of biodiesel formation. These findings support Spirogyra sp. as a candidate biodiesel feedstock, while confirming that total lipid determination, conversion trials, fuel-quality testing, and sustainability assessment are still required.

    A Pipeline for Mapping FDA-Approved Nucleic Acid Amplification Tests (NAATs) to NCBI Taxonomy [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background The United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) catalogs in vitro diagnostics, including Nucleic Acid Amplification Tests (NAATs), using unstructured clinical terminology. Because these regulatory labels often diverge from standardized biological taxonomy, they obstruct automated post-market surveillance and computational integration, particularly for in silico inclusivity audits against emerging pathogen variants. This study aimed to build a reproducible computational pipeline that translates historical FDA microbial NAAT records into machine-actionable biological identifiers. Methods Synthesizing clinical diagnostic logic, taxonomic normalization rules, and modern text mining, the workflow utilized the R ellmer package to interface with a Large Language Model (LLM) for Technology Classification, successfully isolating true NAATs from 510(k) summary documents. Subsequent algorithmic partitioning and LLM-driven Named Entity Recognition (NER) mapped complex syndromic labels and clinical abbreviations to National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) Taxonomy Identifiers (TaxIDs). Results While only 20% of initial FDA records mapped directly to NCBI Taxonomy, the automated deconvolution of syndromic panels and NER extraction expanded and normalized the dataset. The pipeline successfully generated FDA510kPathogens, a highly granular master repository containing 457 unique pathogen-to-submission entries formally linked to enriched NCBI TaxIDs and metadata. Conclusions By programmatically translating human-readable regulatory metadata into machine-actionable biological descriptors, this open-source digital infrastructure resolves a critical informatics bottleneck. This provides diagnostic laboratories and developers with the foundational data necessary to conduct continuous, automated validation of molecular assays.
