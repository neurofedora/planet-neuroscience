    Blockchain for Public Budget Transparency: Opportunities and Challenges in Governance A Contextual Determinants Framework [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Public budget transparency is a fundamental prerequisite for accountable governance and effective public financial management, yet conventional budgeting systems remain vulnerable to opacity, manipulation, and weak public oversight. Blockchain technology has been widely promoted as a transformative solution due to its decentralized architecture, immutable ledgers, and automated execution through smart contracts. However, existing studies often adopt a techno-centric perspective and fail to explain why blockchain initiatives succeed in some governance contexts while underperforming or fail in others. Addressing this gap, this study aims to identify the critical contextual factors that determine the effectiveness of blockchain technology in enhancing public budget transparency. This research employs a qualitative meta-synthesis approach, systematically reviewing and synthesizing evidence from 45 peer-reviewed studies published between 2015 and 2023. Following PRISMA 2020 guidelines, the selected studies were analyzed using a critical interpretive synthesis to uncover recurring patterns and contextual conditions influencing implementation outcomes. The analysis is theoretically grounded in Institutional Theory, enabling the integration of technological, institutional, and socio-political dimensions. The findings demonstrate that blockchain’s impact on budget transparency is strongly mediated by three interdependent contextual pillars: technological readiness, institutional maturity, and the socio-political environment. While blockchain offers opportunities such as enhanced auditability, reduced corruption risks, and increased citizen participation, these benefits are only realized in contexts characterized by adequate digital infrastructure, clear regulatory frameworks, strong inter-agency collaboration, political commitment, and sufficient public trust. The study proposes a Contextual Determinants Framework (CDF) that explains how these factors interact to shape implementation feasibility and outcomes.

    From Curriculum to Real-World Application: A Systematic Review of Transversal Competency Development, Assessment, and Transfer Across School Subjects [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Transversal competencies are increasingly important in primary and secondary education, where students are expected to apply knowledge and skills across contexts. However, research on their development, assessment, and transfer remains fragmented. Objective This study examines how transversal competencies are developed, assessed, and transferred in primary and secondary education and proposes an integrated conceptual framework. Methods A systematic literature review (SLR) was conducted using Scopus. The search on 29 October 2026 identified 2,472 records. Following bibliographic filtering, title and abstract screening, and full-text eligibility assessment, 334 journal articles were included. Descriptive analysis and narrative synthesis followed PRISMA 2020 and PRISMA-S guidelines. Results Critical thinking and reasoning (38.9%), problem-solving (36.8%), and collaboration (28.7%) were the most frequently examined competencies. STEM/STEAM was the most common pedagogical approach (27.5%), followed by project-based learning (13.5%). Tests and examinations were the most frequently reported assessment methods (68.3%), followed by performance, product, and portfolio assessments (61.4%), and surveys, questionnaires, and scales (45.2%). Transfer evidence was less common: learner perceptions and self-reports appeared in 29.9% of articles, followed by real-life application (19.2%), simulations or scenarios (15.6%), and application across subject areas or new tasks (10.8%). Conclusion Research attention is uneven across competency development, assessment, and transfer. Although development and assessment are widely examined, evidence of application beyond initial learning contexts remains less prominent, particularly in real-life settings and across different tasks or subject areas. The study proposes the Development-Assessment-Transfer (DAT) framework to integrate these dimensions. Further empirical research is needed to examine their relationships and evaluate the framework across educational contexts.

    Justice Deficits, State Fragility, and Digital Repression of Social Media Activism in Sub-Saharan Africa: A Two-Tier Panel Analysis, 2016–2024 [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Although social media is central to rights-based movements in Sub-Saharan Africa, judicial constraints on executive power and state fragility have not been comparatively assessed against internet shutdown frequency within a common cross-national framework. Objectives This study examines the relationships among justice deficits, state fragility, and digital repression in the context of social media rights activism and assesses whether findings extend beyond a small-country sample. Methodology A two-tier quantitative panel design analyses eight countries: Nigeria, Ethiopia, Uganda, Zimbabwe, Senegal, Kenya, Sudan, and the Democratic Republic of Congo, from 2016 to 2024 (n = 72), with validation across 49 Sub-Saharan African countries (441 country-years). Internet shutdown frequency from Access Now’s #KeepItOn dataset proxies digital repression. Institutional constraints and fragility are measured using the V-Dem judicial constraints dataset, the World Justice Project Rule of Law Index, and the Fragile States Index. Negative binomial regression with country-clustered robust standard errors controls for population, GDP, urbanisation, election timing, and regime type. Findings In the eight-country panel, judicial constraints correlate with shutdown frequency (r = −.342, p = .003), but the relationship becomes insignificant after accounting for severe multicollinearity. With reduced collinearity, the association remains significant (b = −2.027, p = .034) in the regional model (n = 414 after listwise deletion). These results indicate that the association between digital repression and constraints on executive power depends on model specification and cross-national heterogeneity. Conclusion Although causality cannot be established, judicial reform may help protect digital rights. Alternative measures, longer panels, and fixed-effects models are needed to test this relationship more rigorously.

    Building Resilient Universities: A Navigational Leadership Framework for Faculty Development and Academic Performance [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Universities face growing challenges in maintaining academic performance amid institutional uncertainty and organizational change. However, the relationships between adaptive leadership, faculty development, and institutional resilience remain insufficiently integrated. This study proposes a Navigational Leadership Framework connecting strategic foresight, adaptive decision-making, collaborative coordination, and resource orchestration with faculty development, university resilience, and academic performance. Methods An explanatory sequential mixed-methods framework was developed for higher education at Universitas Negeri Malang, Indonesia. The quantitative analytical demonstration employed 80 synthetic records comprising 24 questionnaire indicators across four constructs. Standardized regression and bootstrap mediation analyses examined direct and indirect relationships. Five provisional qualitative themes were formulated to guide subsequent interviews and contextual interpretation. Results Within the synthetic dataset, navigational leadership was positively associated with faculty development (β = 0.394), perceived university resilience (β = 0.334), and academic performance (β = 0.284). Faculty development demonstrated the strongest direct association with academic performance (β = 0.343) and a positive indirect association between navigational leadership and academic performance (β = 0.135). The direct relationship between university resilience and academic performance was not statistically significant (β = 0.187, p = 0.072). The proposed model explained 40.8% of the variance in self-reported academic performance. Sequential mediation remained uncertain. Conclusions The proposed framework identifies faculty development as a potentially important mechanism connecting navigational leadership with academic performance while distinguishing institutional resilience from individual academic productivity. Integrating strategic leadership, professional learning, and organizational adaptability provides a conceptual basis for examining university resilience. However, the findings represent an analytical demonstration rather than empirical validation. Further investigation using authentic survey and interview data is required.

    Gamified Strategies to Enhance Cognitive Development in Early Childhood Education [version 2; peer review: 2 approved with reservations]

    Background This study addresses the need to transform traditional teaching methods that still predominate in early childhood education and limit the development of cognitive skills such as attention, concentration, memory, language, and logical thinking. In response to this issue, the study proposes gamification as an active, meaningful, and student-centered pedagogical strategy, aligned with contemporary educational paradigms that emphasize inclusion, the use of ICT, and meaningful learning. Methods A quantitative approach was employed, with an applied and explanatory scope, using a quasi-experimental comparative design. A complementary teacher-perception survey was included as a descriptive quantitative component. Results Five teachers were surveyed, revealing highly positive perceptions of gamification, particularly highlighting its effectiveness in capturing attention, fostering active participation, and promoting meaningful learning. Gamified activities were designed and implemented over 16 weekly sessions, and the cognitive skills of 50 children (25 control and 25 experimental) were assessed after the intervention with a structured observation checklist; baseline records could not be used because of data-quality problems. Conclusions Statistical analysis using the Mann Whitney Wilcoxon test showed significant post-intervention differences between the control and experimental groups across all cognitive variables, particularly in attention (median difference of 1.6 points) and logical thinking (1.75 points), with large effect sizes (rank-biserial r = 0.96–1.00). These findings are consistent with a positive association between gamification and children’s cognitive development; however, given the non-randomized, single-institution, non-blinded design and the absence of usable baseline data, they should be interpreted as preliminary evidence of association rather than causation.

    Molecular Mechanisms of Antimicrobial Resistance in Infectious Diseases: Advances in Therapeutic Strategies and Public Health Interventions [version 2; peer review: 1 approved with reservations]

    Drug resistance in infectious diseases present a major global health concern, reducing treatment efficacy and increasing morbidity and mortality. Resistance arises from different molecular mechanisms including genetic mutations, enzymatic degradation of drugs, alterations in target sites, efflux pump overexpression, and reduced membrane permeability. These mechanisms contribute to the development of multidrug-resistant and extensively drug-resistant pathogens across bacterial, viral, and mycobacterial species. Scientist have developed therapeutic strategies to control these mechanisms of multidrug-resistant. These include the development of novel antimicrobials such as teixobactin and pretomanid, the application of β-lactamase inhibitors, rational drug combinations, host-directed therapies, and antimicrobial peptides. Advances in biotechnology have enabled precise-targeted approaches, including phage therapy, CRISPR-based antimicrobials, and nanocarrier-mediated drug delivery. Public health interventions are important in reducing the burden of AMR. These include global surveillance systems, antimicrobial stewardship programs, vaccination, infection control protocols, and regulatory policies governing antimicrobial use in humans and animals. Future directions emphasize the integration of precision medicine, artificial intelligence, environmental monitoring, and international governance to strengthen AMR control. A sustained and coordinated global response is essential to preserve the efficacy of current therapies and promote the development of new interventions. By linking each resistance route to its therapeutic vulnerability and control requirement, this review provides a mechanism-to-intervention framework for prioritizing diagnostics, stewardship, and public-health action.

    Profitability Determinants of Nepalese Microfinance Institutions: A Panel Data Analysis from the Institutionalist Paradigm [version 3; peer review: 3 approved with reservations]

    Background Microfinance Institutions (MFIs) primarily channel rural savings through microloans for individuals and small entrepreneurs, which helps to alleviate poverty, considered a major social challenge. The financial stability and sustainability of MFIs primarily depend on profitability. Methods This study empirically investigates the determinants of profitability as measured by ROA and ROE in Nepalese MFIs. The secondary data are acquired from the Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) for the period October 18, 2021, to July 16, 2025, with 15 quarters of 49 MFIs, leading to a total of 735 observations. This study employs the Fixed-effect (FE) estimator for baseline regression analysis and the POLS and Random-effect (RE) estimators for sensitivity analysis. Results The outcome reveals that both capital ratios—core capital and capital fund—boost profitability in Nepalese MFIs. Also, the earnings assets, such as loans, boost the profitability of MFIs. Conversely, borrowing and cash reserve ratio (CRR) do not impact the profitability of Nepalese MFIs. However, loans to the agriculture sector adversely impact profitability in the Nepalese MFIs. Notably, nonperforming loans (NPLs) adversely affect the profitability. Conclusions This study concludes that capital, loans, and NPLs are the major determinants of the profitability of Nepalese MFIs. Policymakers could encourage MFIs to strengthen their capital base, implement stricter credit management monitoring policies, and implement a credit guarantee scheme to protect MFIs from eroding profitability and to develop the agriculture sector. Contribution/Originality This study contributes novel empirical evidence to the discourse on Nepalese MFIs by examining the determinants of profitability through the lens of the institutionalist paradigm. This study area has received less attention in earlier studies, especially in the Nepalese context, which fills the research void and supplies additional literature in this field.

    Differentiated Digital Instructional Supervision: Developing and Validating an Instrument for Directive, Collaborative, and Non-Directive Teacher Support [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Differentiated instructional supervision requires professional support that accommodates teachers’ varying competencies, developmental needs, and autonomy within digital educational environments. This study aimed to develop and validate the Differentiated Digital Instructional Supervision (DDIS) instrument, integrating Directive, Collaborative, and Non-Directive approaches as complementary dimensions of teacher support. Methods An instrument-development design was organized using the Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, and Evaluation (ADDIE) framework, complemented by psychometric validation procedures. The needs assessment involved 320 teachers, principals, and supervisors, alongside interviews with nine key informants. Instrument development included construct specification, item formulation, expert validation, and field implementation involving 24 teachers and three supervisors. Psychometric evaluation employed exploratory factor analysis (EFA), confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), internal consistency assessment, and convergent and discriminant validity testing. Results Expert validation yielded an overall score of 3.67, supporting the instrument’s content, technical, and practical appropriateness. Field implementation indicated favorable usability, with satisfaction scores of 4.52 among teachers and 4.71 among supervisors. EFA identified three factors comprising 36 items and explaining 51.18% of the cumulative variance. CFA supported the proposed three-dimensional structure (CFI = 1.000; TLI = 1.001; RMSEA = .000; SRMR = .047), with standardized factor loadings exceeding.50. Cronbach’s alpha, McDonald’s omega, and composite reliability exceeded.90 across all dimensions. Discriminant validity was supported by heterotrait–monotrait ratios ranging from.262 to.475. Convergent validity was adequate for Collaborative and Non-Directive support, whereas Directive support demonstrated marginal average variance extracted (.459). Conclusions The DDIS instrument provides preliminary psychometric support for measuring three distinct but related dimensions of digitally mediated instructional supervision. Further refinement of the Directive dimension and validation across diverse educational settings are warranted.
