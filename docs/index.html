    TagGen: High-Performance Barcode Generator and Demultiplexer for High-Throughput and Long-Read Sequencing Applications [version 2; peer review: 1 approved, 1 not approved]

    Background Long-read sequencing platforms, particularly Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT), have transformed transcriptomics through direct RNA sequencing. However, their higher error rates – dominated by insertions and deletions – demand longer, more robust sequence barcodes. Existing barcode generation tools suffer from exponential complexity, becoming computationally infeasible at lengths above 12 bp. Methods To address this gap, we developed TagGen, a high-performance barcode generator implementing Monte Carlo candidate sampling with greedy diversity selection. TagGen includes an integrated demultiplexer that assigns ONT reads to their source barcodes regardless of the barcode position using a kmer voting and banded edit-distance matching pipeline. We benchmarked TagGen using Badread-simulated reads and validated barcode resilience using a literature-based nanopore error model. Results TagGen generates 96 diverse 12 bp barcodes from 100,000 candidates in under 100 milliseconds, outperforming exhaustive enumeration by up to 13,600-fold. TagGen successfully generates barcodes at 14–30 bp lengths where other available tools fail. Noise simulation demonstrates that TagGen-generated 30 bp barcodes (minimum Hamming distance ≥8) maintain 100% correct assignment at 20% total error rate, whereas traditional 10 bp barcodes degrade to 83%. At typical nanopore error rates (10–15%), TagGen-generated barcodes ≥14 bp achieve >97% theoretical resolution. When inserted within a read, our systematic benchmark shows that TagGen demultiplexer achieved >90% accuracy with zero wrong-sample assignments (“end” mode) for reads ≥20 bp. Levenshtein edit distance, recommended for ONT data, improved accuracy by 10–27 percentage points over Hamming distance at equivalent parameters. Conclusions TagGen offers a specific combination of capabilities for robust barcode design for nanopore and direct RNA sequencing applications, providing researchers with error-tolerant barcodes validated against realistic long-read error profiles, and an integrated anchor-free demultiplexer for flexible read assignment. The software is freely available at https://github.com/Arnaroo/taggen.

    The Potential Mitigating Effect of Curcumin Root Extract Against Synthetic Anabolic Steroid Boldenone-Induced Kidney Injury in Male Rats [version 3; peer review: 2 approved with reservations]

    Background The anabolic-androgenic steroid boldenone is widely abused and is strongly linked to nephrotoxicity, primarily mediated through oxidative stress and inflammation. Curcumin, a natural polyphenol, is renowned for its potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, but its protective role against boldenone-induced renal injury is not fully elucidated. This study aimed to investigate the potential nephroprotective effects of curcumin against boldenone-induced kidney damage in a rat model, focusing on biochemical parameters, oxidative stress markers, inflammatory response, and histological changes. Methods Thirty-two male Wistar rats were divided into four groups (n=8): control, curcumin-only (300 mg/kg/day), boldenone-only (5 mg/kg/week), and boldenone + curcumin. Over the experimental period, body weight, kidney weight, food/water intake, and serum renal function markers (urea, creatinine, electrolytes) were monitored. Renal tissue was analyzed for oxidative stress markers (MDA, GSH, CAT, SOD), the inflammatory cytokine TNF-α, and histological alterations. Results Boldenone administration significantly increased body and kidney weights, serum urea, creatinine, potassium, and chloride, while decreasing sodium and calcium. It induced marked oxidative stress (elevated MDA, depleted GSH, CAT, SOD) and inflammation (elevated TNF-α), resulting in significant tubular necrosis and hypertrophy. Post-treatment with curcumin substantially mitigated these abnormalities, normalizing serum markers, restoring antioxidant defenses and electrolyte balance, reducing TNF-α expression, and ameliorating histological damage. Conclusion Curcumin exerts significant nephroprotection against boldenone-induced toxicity through its antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and cytoprotective mechanisms. These findings justify its exploration as a potential adjunct therapy to prevent steroid-associated kidney injury. Future studies should focus on elucidating the precise molecular signaling pathways involved and validating these effects in clinical scenarios of AAS abuse.

    Complete Genome Sequence of Halorubrum Pleomorphic Virus 8 [version 2; peer review: 1 approved]

    Pleomorphic archaeal viruses are widespread and abundant in hypersaline environments and beyond. Still, a relatively low number of isolates are available for this viral group. Here, we present the complete genome sequence of Halorubrum pleomorphic viruses 8 (HRPV-8), which was previously isolated from the Samut Sakhon solar saltern, Thailand, on a Halorubrum species. The HRPV-8 genome was assembled into a circular 10,824-bp-long contig with a GC content of 63.2%. The genome annotation resulted in 12 open reading frames, including a VP4-like spike protein-encoding gene, which is a hallmark of pleomorphic archaeal viruses. Seven and three putative proteins in HRPV-8 were 99–100% identical to their counterparts in Halorubrum pleomorphic viruses 2 and 6 (HRPV-2 and HRPV-6), respectively, members of the genus Alphapleolipovirus. The average nucleotide identity values within these three viral genomes were 92–96%. According to VIRIDIC analyses of intergenomic similarities, HRPV-8 represents its own species. Thus, based on the observed pleolipoviral genome architecture and sequence similarities to other alphapleolipoviruses, we propose to classify HRPV-8 as a new species within the Alphapleolipovirus genus of the Pleolipoviridae family.

    Sustainability Disclosure, Governance, and Corporate Performance: Evidence from Accounting- and Market-Based Measures [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background This study examines the impact of sustainability disclosure — both environmental and social — on the financial performance of listed companies in Bangladesh. With increasing international attention on sustainability reporting, there is little evidence on how the quality of disclosure attributes is associated with corporate financial performance in developing markets with nascent governance systems. The investigation fills three main gaps: (i) lack of deep examination of the effects of environmental and social information separately on economic wealth; (ii) dearth in moderation analysis on governance, industry, and firm size characteristics; and (iii) weak methodology in controlling for endogeneity and contemporaneous error structures in emerging-market observations. Methods Set against a balanced-panel sample of non-financial firm-listed companies on the Dhaka Stock Exchange (2020–2023), financial performance is measured by return on assets, return on equity, Tobin’s Q, and market capitalization growth. The information quality was measured using GRI-based indices. The estimation, which utilizes the fixed effects, CS-ARDL, and system-GMM models, is also robust to heterogeneity and endogeneity. Results The findings indicate that environmental disclosure enhances both accounting- and market-based performance, whereas social disclosure has a partial effect, predominantly driven by labour and community commitments. These effects are strengthened by governance strength and firm size, which support the moderating role of internal structure and market coverage. Conclusions The study also adds value to the literature by combining deconstructed disclosure indices, moderating contexts, second-generation estimators, and stakeholder and legitimacy theories in an emerging market.

    When Law Becomes a Shield for Crime: Administrative Concealment in Indonesia's Nickel Mining Sector [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Indonesia's nickel industry encounters a regulatory dilemma in which governance mechanisms intended for oversight may be utilised for obfuscation. This article explores the conversion of mining documents from tools of regulation into frameworks for state-sponsored economic offences, utilising the Mandiodo case as a case study. Methods This doctrinal legal study integrates Indonesian mining, anti-corruption, and corporate legislation with judicial documentation from the Mandiodo case, official prosecutorial resources, and global benchmarks (EITI, OECD). It establishes a structure to examine the legal boundaries separating administrative non-compliance from criminal behaviour. Results The analysis presents a five-phase Licensing-to-State-Economic-Crime (LSEC) model: legitimate access, bureaucratic manipulation, origin alteration, financial transformation, and obfuscation. It illustrates that illegal behaviour emerges from the intersection of knowledge, intentional manipulation, and illicit gain, rather than solely from documentation inaccuracies. The document further examines the ramifications of Constitutional Court Decision No. 123/PUU-XXIII/2025 and the ne bis in idem concerns arising from the ensuing money-laundering cases. Conclusions The study suggests a Mining Governance Integrity System that incorporates authenticated beneficial ownership, anomaly identification, traceability, and gatekeeper due diligence. Its role is to connect the boundaries of criminal law with the framework of extractive governance, with the objective of averting the criminalisation of legitimate economic risks.

    Burnout, Workplace Factors, and Retention Indicators Among Nurses in a Rural District Hospital in the Eastern Cape, South Africa [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Burnout-related symptoms among nurses are a growing concern globally and may affect workforce well-being, patient care, and health system performance. In resource-constrained rural stableettings, heavy workloads, staffing shortages, and limited organisational support may increase nurses’ vulnerability to burnout-related symptoms. Evidence from rural district hospitals in South Africa remains limited. Objective To examine burnout-related symptoms, workplace and organisational factors, and nursing workforce retention indicators among nurses at St Elizabeth Hospital in the Eastern Cape Province, South Africa. Methods A quantitative cross-sectional study was conducted among 210 nurses working in selected clinical departments at St Elizabeth Hospital. Participants were selected using stratified random sampling from an eligible population of 362 nurses. Data were collected using a structured self-administered questionnaire incorporating the Burnout Assessment Tool (BAT-12), selected Maslach Burnout Inventory personal accomplishment items, and workplace-related measures informed by the Job Demands–Resources (JD–R) model. In addition, aggregate nursing workforce and retention indicators were collected from Human Resources records using a structured data extraction tool. Descriptive statistical methods were used to summarise survey data, while Human Resources data were analysed separately. Results High frequencies of burnout-related symptoms were reported, particularly exhaustion. Mental exhaustion was reported often or always by 71.0% of participants, difficulty recovering energy after work by 71.9%, and physical exhaustion by 72.4%. Heavy workload (78.6%), inadequate staffing (68.6%), regular overtime (68.1%), inadequate equipment (67.1%), and limited access to wellness services (48.6%) were common workplace challenges. Human Resources records indicated a 12-month nursing workforce retention rate of 90,49% and a turnover rate of 9,51%. Conclusions Burnout-related symptoms were common among nurses in this rural Eastern Cape district hospital, driven by high workplace demands and limited resources. Organisational improvements, such as better staffing, stronger workplace support, and access to wellness services, could improve nurse well-being and workforce sustainability. The study also highlights the need for longitudinal research on burnout and staff retention while contributing context-specific evidence from an under-researched rural South African setting.

    Comparative Chloroplast Genomics and Phylogenetic Relationships of Poa angustifolia and Poa lipskyi (Poaceae) [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Poa L. (Poaceae), comprising over 500 species, is taxonomically challenging due to morphological plasticity, hybridization, and polyploidy. To clarify plastome architecture and phylogenetic relationships within the genus, we sequenced and annotated complete chloroplast genomes of Poa angustifolia (accessions P10, P14) and Poa lipskyi (P17) from Uzbekistan. The three plastomes (135,310–135,609 bp) shared the typical quadripartite structure and an identical complement of 130 genes across self-replication, photosynthesis, and other functional categories. Codon usage and amino acid composition were highly conserved, with a consistent bias toward A/T-ending codons and leucine, isoleucine, glycine, and serine as the dominant residues. Sliding-window analysis identified two nucleotide-diversity hotspots, in the rpl32–trnL-UAG and trnC-GCA–rpoB regions, and SSR profiling showed predominant mononucleotide A/T repeats alongside a few taxon-specific pentanucleotide motifs. Maximum-likelihood phylogenomic analysis placed the P. angustifolia accessions in a strongly supported clade sister to P. lipskyi, consistent with shared membership in sect. Poa. These results indicate that Poa plastomes are structurally conserved yet retain informative variable regions useful for phylogenetics and barcoding.

    Sustainable digital transformation and business competitiveness in emerging-economy organizations: the moderating role of economic sector and organizational age in Piura, Peru [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Sustainable digital transformation is increasingly treated as a strategic lever for competitiveness, but evidence on the organizational conditions under which it pays off in emerging economies is limited. We estimated its direct effect on business competitiveness and tested whether economic sector and organizational age moderate that effect, separately and jointly. Methods In a quantitative, cross-sectional design, we surveyed 361 formally registered organizations in Piura, Peru, across the commerce, services, manufacturing, agro-industry, and construction sectors. A 30-item questionnaire measured sustainable digital transformation (economic, social, and environmental dimensions) and business competitiveness (operational efficiency, innovation capacity, and market positioning). Content validity was confirmed by expert judgment (Aiken's V 0.81–0.97). The measurement model was evaluated with exploratory and confirmatory factor analysis (maximum likelihood), and hypotheses were tested with hierarchical regression (Hayes' Model 3, HC3 robust standard errors, 10,000 bootstrap resamples). Results Sustainable digital transformation had a positive, significant direct effect on business competitiveness (β = 0.657; p < 0.001; R2 = 0.432; b = 0.660 in the full model). The six-dimension measurement model showed adequate fit (CFI = 0.950; RMSEA = 0.047) and discriminant validity (all HTMT ≤ 0.754). Neither economic sector (p = 0.816) nor organizational age (p = 0.691) moderated the effect alone, but their joint moderation was significant (robust Wald F(4,341) = 3.12; p = 0.015), concentrated in manufacturing, where the competitive return on digitalization weakened with firm age and lost significance after roughly eleven years. Conclusions Sustainable digital transformation strongly predicts business competitiveness in this emerging-economy setting, but its returns depend on the combination of economic sector and organizational maturity; digitalization strategies should be differentiated by context rather than applied uniformly.

    Effectiveness of Physical Activity Interventions on Mental Health Outcomes in Natural Disaster Survivors: A Systematic Review of Proximal and Complex Effects [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Backgrounds Natural disasters pose significant challenges to mental health, yet effective and scalable strategies to support psychosocial recovery remain limited. This study aimed to systematically examine the effects of physical activity, exercise, and sport-based interventions on mental health and psychosocial outcomes among disaster-affected populations, as well as to identify key characteristics of these interventions. Methods A systematic literature review was conducted in accordance with PRISMA guidelines, using Scopus and PubMed databases. The review protocol was prospectively registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO; CRD420261424835) Seven studies met the inclusion criteria, comprising randomized controlled trials, quasi-experimental studies, and qualitative studies. Results The findings indicate that physical activity interventions, particularly sport-based and game-based approaches delivered in group settings were consistently associated with improvements in psychological well-being and psychosocial functioning. A consistent pattern was observed in which reductions in anxiety were accompanied by improvements in sleep quality, suggesting interconnected psychological and physiological effects. However, findings related to post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) were inconsistent, and no significant improvements were observed in quality of life (QoL). These variations appear to be influenced by differences in intervention characteristics, participant profiles, and contextual factors. Conclusions Overall, physical activity interventions demonstrated greater effectiveness in improving proximal psychological outcomes, such as anxiety, sleep quality, and well-being, compared to more complex outcomes such as PTSD and QoL. These findings highlight the need for more comprehensive, multi-component approaches when targeting multidimensional aspects of mental health in post-disaster contexts. Integrating physical activity into disaster recovery programs may provide a practical and evidence-based strategy to support psychosocial well-being and resilience.

    Resting‐State Brain Entropy Reveals Altered Neural Dynamics and Cognitive Associations in Mild Traumatic Brain Injury: A Longitudinal Study

    Resting-state brain entropy mapping in mild TBI revealed dynamic, regional-specific changes from acute to chronic stages. Early hyperentropy in precuneus, a key hub of DMN, predicted short-term cognitive gains, while later shifts and entropy reductions aligned with long-term recovery, highlighting evolving functional changes after injury.


    ABSTRACT

    Mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI) is associated with persistent physical, cognitive, and emotional symptoms, yet the functional brain changes that accompany recovery remain incompletely understood. This longitudinal study investigated brain entropy (BEN), a resting-state measure of irregularity or complexity of spontaneous BOLD activity derived from resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI), during the first year following mTBI. Data of rs-fMRI were acquired from 48 patients with mTBI at the acute (< 14 days), 6-month, and 12-month post-injury visits, and 34 healthy controls (HCs). BEN was quantified using sample entropy. Patients with mTBI also underwent behavioral assessments evaluating quality of life and cognitive function. Behavioral assessments demonstrated progressive improvements in patient-reported quality of life and objective cognitive performance. Cross-sectional analyses revealed dynamic, time-dependent alterations in BEN. Compared with HCs, patients with mTBI exhibited higher BEN in precuneus, temporal, occipital, and sensorimotor regions during the acute and 6-month stages, whereas lower BEN was observed in sensorimotor, occipital, and frontoparietal regions at the 12-month follow-up. Longitudinal analyses demonstrated progressive regional reductions in BEN over time. Several associations between longitudinal changes in regional BEN and cognitive performance were nominally significant, particularly in the precuneus, but none survived multiple comparison corrections. These findings indicate that mTBI recovery is accompanied by dynamic, regionally specific changes in resting state BOLD irregularity. BEN may therefore provide a promising imaging biomarker for tracking the evolving neural dynamics underlying recovery following mTBI.

    Diurnal Regulation of Hippocampal Synaptic Plasticity in Aged Rats and the Role of the Circadian Clock Gene Per1

    ABSTRACT

    Period circadian regulator 1 (PER1) regulates circadian gene expression and is critical for spatial memory. However, its role in regulating synaptic plasticity between diurnal phases remains largely unknown. Here, we examined long-term potentiation (LTP) and long-term synaptic depression (LTD) in hippocampal slices prepared from aged male Per1 knockout (Per1 KO) rats and their wildtype (WT) littermates during the light/inactive and dark/active phases. In WT rats, theta-burst stimulation (TBS) consistently induced stable LTP that was greater during the dark relative to the light phase. In contrast, in Per1 KO rats, TBS resulted in synaptic potentiation that decayed to baseline levels within ~90 min in both phases. Notably, WT rats readily expressed LTD following 1 Hz low-frequency stimulation (LFS) during the dark phase but not the light phase. By comparison, in Per1 KO rats, LTD was inducible by LFS during both the dark and light phases. LTD in both WT and Per1 KO rats was dependent on the activation of N-methyl-d-aspartate receptors. Basal synaptic transmission was reduced and GluA1 S845 phosphorylation was increased during the dark phase in both WT and Per1 KO rats. Together, our findings identify PER1 as a key regulator of synaptic plasticity between diurnal phases in aged rats.

    The Complete Chloroplast Genome of Lepidium olgae, a Narrow Endemic Species of the Nuratau Range, Uzbekistan [version 2; peer review: 2 approved with reservations]

    Lepidium olgae Regel is a poorly studied narrow endemic of the Nuratau Range, Uzbekistan, for which genomic resources remain limited. In this study, we sequenced, assembled, and characterized its complete chloroplast genome and evaluated its phylogenetic position using 17 Lepidium plastome accessions representing 17 taxa, together with four outgroup plastomes. Four plastomes from Hornungia and Yinshania were included as outgroups in the phylogenetic analysis. The circular plastome of L. olgae was 154,837 bp in length and displayed the typical quadripartite structure, comprising an 83,881-bp large single-copy region, an 18,070-bp small single-copy region, and two inverted-repeat regions of 26,443 bp each. The overall GC content was 36.3%. Comparative analysis showed that plastome size, gene order, and overall genome organization were highly conserved among the sampled Lepidium taxa, with only limited variation at the single-copy and inverted-repeat junctions. Feature-level nucleotide-diversity analysis identified trnG-GCC–trnR-UCU and rpl32–trnL-UAG among the most variable non-coding regions, whereas ycf1 showed the highest diversity among the examined coding regions. Maximum-likelihood analysis of 21 complete plastomes placed L. olgae as a distinct lineage within the sampled Asian Lepidium assemblage. The newly generated plastome provides an important genomic reference for L. olgae, while the identified variable regions represent candidate loci for future taxonomic, phylogeographic, and population-level studies. Broader taxonomic sampling and nuclear genomic data will be required to clarify the evolutionary history of the genus and assess the conservation genetics of this narrow endemic species.
