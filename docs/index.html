    Statistical analysis of residue contact energies reveals folding determinants of ion channels

    by Svetlana I. Tarnovskaya, Boris S. Zhorov, Denis B. Tikhonov

    Tetrameric and pseudo-tetrameric cationic channels, which are targeted by many drugs and toxins, play key roles in the cell physiology and pathophysiology. This diverse superfamily of P-loop channels includes but is not limited to voltage-gated potassium, sodium and calcium channels, TRP channels, and ionotropic glutamate receptors. In hundreds of crystal and cryoEM structures of P-loop channels, the general folding is rather similar but subfamily-specific structural details vary greatly. The latter underlie atomic mechanisms of ion selectivity, gating, sensitivity to drugs and toxins, and dysfunction of thousands disease-associated variants. Understanding these mechanisms may benefit from comparative analyses of multiple structures. Here, we propose a statistical method to reveal structural determinants of folding in related proteins, which involves clustering of residue-residue contact energies. We applied the method to a pH-gated potassium channel KcsA and a voltage-gated sodium channel NavAb. Twenty 3D-aligned experimental structures of each channel with universal residue labels were downloaded from database www.plic3da.com and Monte Carlo energy minimized. Residue contact energies clustered in the multidimensional space of contacts automatically discriminated open vs. closed states of KcsA, and wild-type vs. chimeric structures of NavAb. We found and analyzed specific contacts that underlie structural peculiarities. The proposed approach may help detecting folding peculiarities and their structural determinants between subfamilies of P-loop channels, within individual subfamilies, and could be adapted to other proteins.

    Multi-model forecasting of respiratory disease activity in Germany during the 2024–2025 season

    by Johannes Bracher, Daniel Wolffram, Rodrigo Amaral Lind, Nils Bardeck, Micha Böhm, Seba Contreras, Philipp Dönges, Felix Günther, Rolf Kaiser, Jan van de Kassteele, Alexander Kuhlmann, Berit Lange, Barbora Němcová, Viola Priesemann, Ulrich Reinacher, Isti Rodiah, Frank Sandmann, the RESPINOW Study Group , Melanie Schienle

    Respiratory diseases cause considerable morbidity in autumn and winter and are a priority in public health monitoring. In Germany, they are subject to a number of surveillance systems, including both pathogen-specific and syndromic indicators. In this paper we present a collaborative multi-target and multi-model real-time forecasting system rolled out during the 2024/25 season, and discuss differences to earlier efforts carried out during the COVID-19 pandemic. We focus on general practitioner consultations for acute respiratory infections (ARI) and hospitalizations for severe acute respiratory infections (SARI), where a total of eight models were run to generate nowcasts and forecasts. In addition, we consider confirmed cases of seasonal influenza and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) where one forecasting model each was available. As all indicators were subject to retrospective revisions, forecasting models were combined with a nowcasting step. For the SARI and ARI indicators, we combined the available models into an ensemble and conducted detailed quantitative evaluations. Nowcasts overall showed convincing performance, even though for some models Christmas break effects led to an upward bias in early January. Forecasts were overall well-calibrated and most models outperformed simple benchmark models. When taking into account age-stratified targets, most observed performance differences to a persistence baseline were statistically significant. Anticipating the peak timing and magnitude, however, proved to be challenging, with many models predicting too flat curves with a too early turnaround (e.g., in late January rather than mid-February for SARI). The combined ensemble forecast was among the best-performing approaches, but unlike in previous related projects did not consistently outperform individual models. We conclude by discussing learnings on the organization of collaborative forecasting projects in post-COVID-19 times.

    AlphaFold models of host-pathogen interactions elucidate the prevalence and structural modes of molecular mimicry

    by Delora Baptista, Lidia Gomez-Lucas, Jürgen Jänes, Nevan J. Krogan, Maria João Amorim, Ylva Ivarsson, Pedro Beltrao

    Pathogens exploit host cellular machinery through protein-protein interactions (PPIs), often using molecular mimicry to hijack host cellular processes. While there have been thousands of host-pathogen PPIs determined to date, the lack of structural information for these impedes the study of the prevalence of molecular mimicry and convergent evolution of protein interaction interfaces. To address this, we benchmarked AlphaFold2 and 3 for prediction of structures of host-pathogen interactions observing that accurate models can be retrieved when ranking by modelling confidence, despite an overall low performance. We predicted structures for 6,782 pathogen-human PPIs yielding 803 models of higher confidence. Most pathogen proteins interacting with a common human protein are predicted to do so via the same interface, suggesting a high degree of convergent evolution of protein interaction interfaces. When comparing structural models from host-pathogen and host-host interactions, we observe that a majority of pathogen proteins are predicted to target existing human PPI interfaces. We categorized instances of mimicry into different modes, occurring at different frequencies: 1) via the same domain family (least common); 2) via a similar structural motif; and 3) via a similar linear peptide (most common). We selected examples of linear peptide interactions for binding assay testing, confirming 8 out of 12 predicted interfaces, including 3 viral linear peptide interactions. This validates AlphaFold’s ability to model some host-pathogen interactions and the mechanisms underlying molecular mimicry. This work showcases the value of large-scale structural modelling to study convergent evolution of host-pathogen interactions and how molecular mimicry may contribute to infection or host defense.

    Large-scale analysis of AlphaFold structures reveals organism-specific physicochemical signatures

    by Michael J. Stam, Diego A. Oyarzún, Nadanai Laohakunakorn, Christopher W. Wood

    Recent advancements in protein structure prediction methods have ushered in a new era of abundant and accurate structural data, providing opportunities to analyse proteins at a scale that has not been possible before. Here we performed a large-scale analysis of physicochemical properties across 373,779 confident AlphaFold structures. Our analysis revealed systematic variation between proteins from different organisms to such an extent that major features of the tree of life could be recovered from these data. Surprisingly, this relationship persisted even when we restricted our analysis to eukaryotic, cytosolic proteins and proteins from single AlphaFold Database Foldseek clusters. Given the strong functional constraints on protein chemistry, this finding is unexpected and suggests that protein structures have organism-specific physicochemical signatures, which might reveal evolutionary constraints imposed by the cellular environment and could have significant implications for the design and engineering of novel proteins.

    When should adaptation arise from a polygenic response versus few large-effect changes?

    by William R. Milligan, Laura K. Hayward, Guy Sella

    When will adaptation involve a few genetic changes of large effect versus a polygenic response? There are compelling reasons to expect polygenic adaptation to be common, yet direct evidence is lacking. Conversely, there are hundreds of examples of large-effect adaptations across species, but it is unclear whether they occur frequently within any given species. These different lines of evidence are hard to synthesize, in part due to the lack of theory that relates them. To address this gap, we frame the question in terms of the properties of an adaptive trait. We ask how the genetic basis of adaptation depends on key aspects of genetic variation in the trait and on the degree to which its selection pressures have changed. To this end, we consider a quantitative trait subject to stabilizing selection and model its response when a population at mutation–selection–drift balance experiences a sudden shift in the trait optimum. Using this model, we delimit how the contributions of large-effect and polygenic changes to adaptation depend on the genetics and ecology of adaptive traits, as well as on other salient factors. Thus, we formulate testable predictions for when different modes of adaptation are expected.

    Digital financial literacy and financial resilience among gastronomic entrepreneurs in the Chiclayo district, Peru [version 3; peer review: 1 approved, 1 approved with reservations]

    Background the rapid growth of digital financial services has not been matched by an equivalent development of digital financial literacy among small entrepreneurs, which may compromise their financial resilience in adverse scenarios. This study was aligned with Sustainable Development Goal 8, target 8.3, and aimed to examine the association between digital financial literacy and the financial resilience of gastronomic entrepreneurs in the Chiclayo district, Peru, in 2026, considering the control variables age, gender, educational level, business tenure, and formality status. Methods applied study, quantitative approach, non-experimental cross-sectional design, and correlational scope. The sample consisted of 179 owners of gastronomic businesses, selected through simple random probability sampling from a population of 333 businesses (95% confidence level, 5% margin of error). A structured 24-item questionnaire with a five-point Likert scale was applied, validated through expert judgement with high reliability in the pilot test (Cronbach’s alpha = .973 for digital financial literacy and.968 for financial resilience). Data were analysed using Pearson correlation, hierarchical linear regression, and partial least squares structural equation modelling (PLS-SEM). Results digital financial literacy was not statistically significantly associated with financial resilience (r = −.110, p = .144; hierarchical regression R2 = .056, F(6,172) = 1.707, p = .122; PLS-SEM β = −0.246, t = −0.951, R2 = .061); none of its four dimensions was significant either (p > .05 in all cases). Both variables were predominantly at a medium level (67.0% and 69.8% of entrepreneurs, respectively). Conclusions within this sample of gastronomic micro-businesses in Chiclayo, there was insufficient evidence that digital financial literacy is associated with business financial resilience; this does not show that no association exists. Other conditions that this study did not measure (for example, access to formal financing) may matter, a possibility for future research to test.

    “It’s wrong, but…”: business formality and the conditional justification of tax evasion among Peruvian bodega owners [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Applications of the Theory of Planned Behavior to tax evasion often measure intention with scenarios describing when evasion would be acceptable and treat informality as registration. We examined how subjective norms, perceived tax ease and the degree of business formality relate to anti-evasion attitude and to the conditional justification of tax evasion among operators of bodegas, the neighborhood grocery stores of Peru. Methods In a cross-sectional survey, 249 bodega operators in Metropolitan Lima and Callao answered a questionnaire in October–November 2024 (non-probability sample). Business formality was measured as a second-order formative construct built from practices in four areas of the business. We estimated the model with partial least squares structural equation modeling, following a dated analysis plan, with two-tailed bootstrap tests, thirteen robustness checks and an open replication in Python. Results Subjective norms (β = 0.329) and business formality (β = 0.567) were positively related to anti-evasion attitude; perceived tax ease was not. Business formality was negatively related to conditional justification (β = −0.773), and this effect held in every robustness check, including one that excluded the tax-related items. Attitude was negatively related to justification in the canonical model (β = −0.275) but not once formality was included (β = 0.054), and no indirect effect through attitude was significant. Although respondents rejected evasion on average, 61.4% endorsed at least one scenario for not paying taxes. Women justified evasion more than men (b = 0.247), above all for business-related reasons in an exploratory comparison. Conclusions Rejecting tax evasion in general does not prevent operators from justifying it in specific situations. The degree of formality of a microenterprise’s practices was the strongest correlate of justification in every specification. Policies may be more effective if they address specific justifications and the practices that tie small stores to formal institutions.

    A multi-source and multi-scale framework for diagnosing building vacancy and underutilization by integrating neighborhood-wide perception, parcel-level field assessment, spatial statistics, and UAV-LiDAR-derived urban morphology. [version 1; peer review:

    Background Building vacancy and underutilization in consolidated urban centralities are multidimensional conditions that cannot be adequately characterized through occupancy status alone. This study develops a multi-source and multi-scale framework for diagnosing these conditions in La Mariscal, Quito, Ecuador. Methods The framework integrated citizen perception, systematic building-level field assessment, spatial statistics, and unmanned aerial vehicle-based light detection and ranging (UAV-LiDAR) data. A citizen-perception survey (n = 531) examined safety, urban activity, deterioration, and underutilization. Field assessment comprised 753 observations, consolidated into 691 unique parcels for spatial analysis. Getis–Ord Gi* statistics identified spatial concentrations, while UAV-LiDAR-derived building vertical extent was compared across vacancy/underutilization spatial groups using non-parametric tests. Results Citizen perceptions revealed the coexistence of urban activity and underutilization, with the strongest association between perceived deterioration and underutilization (Spearman’s ρ = 0.540, p < 0.001). Field observations revealed heterogeneous conditions, and spatial analysis identified differentiated clusters rather than uniform patterns of decline. Cross-source analysis of 685 parcels showed substantial overlap in building vertical extent among vacancy/underutilization cold spots, non-significant areas, and hot spots. Although the overall difference was marginally significant (p = 0.047), its effect size was very small (ε2 = 0.009), and hot spots did not differ significantly from the other groups. Conclusions Urban underutilization involves differentiated combinations of activity, physical deterioration, street interaction, urban image, and revitalization potential. Building vertical extent alone does not provide a distinctive morphological signature of concentrated vacancy or underutilization. The proposed framework integrates perception, field evidence, spatial patterns, and three-dimensional morphology to support urban-regeneration diagnosis and adaptive-reuse prioritization.

    Mapping Challenge-Based Learning in Sustainability Education: A Bibliometric Analysis of Research Trends, Intellectual Structure, and Emerging Themes [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Challenge-Based Learning (CBL) is gaining increasing attention as a pedagogical approach that connects learning with complex, real-world challenges. Although research on CBL in education continues to evolve, a comprehensive mapping of publication trends, scholarly contributions, thematic developments, and intellectual structure is still needed. This study aims to analyze the evolution and knowledge structure of CBL in education, as well as to identify future research opportunities. Methods This study employs a bibliometric analysis approach, examining 163 documents published between 2016 and 2026 across 86 publication sources. The analysis includes performance analysis, keyword co-occurrence, thematic mapping, citation and co-citation analysis, and bibliographic coupling to identify research trends, conceptual structures, and intellectual foundations. Co-citation analysis reveals three reference clusters and two author clusters, which together form the intellectual structure of the research. Conclusion Research on CBL (Challenge-Based Learning) in sustainability education is growing rapidly and is characterized by an increasingly diverse conceptual and intellectual structure. Although this research remains dominated by engineering and higher education contexts, evolving themes point to opportunities to expand the integration of CBL with sustainability competencies, interdisciplinary approaches, and action-oriented learning. These findings provide a bibliometric foundation for guiding future research on CBL in sustainability education.

    Rich-Poor Financial-Inclusion Gaps and Subsequent Digital-Payment Changes: A Cross-Country Measurement Study of Global Findex, 2014–2024 [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Account ownership has expanded faster than equal use across income groups. We test whether rich-poor gaps in account/card ownership and formal saving/borrowing are associated differently with subsequent changes in the digital-payment gap, and whether nonlinear diffusion accounts for the ownership association. Methods We link public-use Global Findex microdata for 2014, 2017, 2021/22, and 2024 into 359 country-period transitions from 144 countries. Rich-poor gaps compare the higher-income 60% with the lower-income 40%. Regressions relate baseline ownership and activity gaps to the next digital-payment-gap change, controlling for the initial gap, national participation, period, and region. Sensitivities use alternative measures, official outcomes, nonlinear adoption-stage controls, within-country models, split samples, and fixed-seed sampling-uncertainty procedures. Results A 10-point wider ownership gap is associated with a 3.858-point larger subsequent digital-payment-gap change. The activity-gap coefficient is −2.426; the slope difference is 6.284 points (95% CI 3.115–9.453; p < 0.001). Nonlinear adoption-stage controls reduce the difference to 4.584–5.730. Pooled tertile-interaction tests do not reject equality of ownership slopes (p = 0.60 when only ownership varies; p = 0.18 when ownership and baseline-payment-gap slopes vary). Country fixed-effects estimates are much less precise, and separating the gaps lowers country-grouped predictive RMSE by only 1.60%. Conclusions Ownership and activity gaps have different pooled associations, but nonlinear adoption-stage controls materially attenuate their difference. Diffusion stage therefore remains a credible competing explanation, while tertile interactions do not establish slope heterogeneity. Weak within-country evidence precludes interpreting the pooled association as a mechanism.

    Artificial Intelligence-Based Prognostic Models for Predicting Stone-Free Status After Retrograde Intrarenal Surgery: a systematic review [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Abstract Background Stone-free status (SFS) is a key outcome after retrograde intrarenal surgery (RIRS), influencing recurrence risk and the need for further intervention. However, its assessment remains variable, with no standarized guideline or definition. Artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) offer a promising approach to improve the prediction of SFS. This study aimed to systematically review AI/ML-based prediction models for SFS following RIRS. Methods A systematic review was conducted using three databases (PubMed, SciELO, and Cochrane). Studies were screened using Rayyan software. Only peer-reviewed original articles published within the last 10 years. Eligible studies involved adult patients undergoing RIRS and applied AI/ML models to predict SFS, with available full-text original articles. Risk of bias was assessed using the Quality of Prognosis Studies tool. The review protocol was registered with PROSPERO (CRD420261352702; registered 6 April 2026). Results Four studies comprising 7,707 patients were included. The studies evaluated several approaches, including LDA, LightGBM, Extra Trees Classifier, and a clinical-radiomics nomogram model. Reported performance varied across models and metrics, including area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.759 to 0.95. Validation accuracy ranged from 77.09% to 81%. F1-scores ranged from 61.97% to 85.34% among studies. All studies were assessed as having a moderate risk of bias, mainly related to incomplete follow-up, insufficient confounder adjustment, and limited model validation. Conclusion AI/ML-based prediction models show potential for predicting SFS after RIRS. However, the limited number of studies, moderate risk of bias, heterogeneity between AI/ML models with different performance metrics, and limited model validation restrict confidence in their clinical applicability. Further prospective evaluations are needed before broader clinical implementation.

    Graphene Oxide-Based Composite Scaffolds for Bone Tissue Engineering: A Systematic Literature Review on Synthesis, Fabrication Methods, Mechanical Performance, In Vitro Biological Activity, and In Vivo Regenerative Cascades [version 1; peer review: awaiti

    Bone regeneration in cases of extensive damage remains challenging due to the bone’s limited capacity for self-healing. Graphene Oxide (GO) is a two-dimensional carbon material containing numerous oxygen-functional groups (hydroxyl, epoxy, carboxyl) that holds potential as a nanofiller scaffold in bone composites because it can enhance mechanical properties while actively modulating cellular behavior. However, the existing evidence remains scattered across various types of matrices and heterogeneous testing protocols. This systematic review aims to compile evidence on GO-based composite scaffolds used in bone tissue engineering, covering synthesis, fabrication techniques, physicochemical characterization, mechanical properties, in vitro osteogenic activity, and in vivo regeneration outcomes. The search was conducted in PubMed, ScienceDirect, and Scopus (n = 1,590), plus gray literature (n = 62). After removing duplicates, screening titles and abstracts, and evaluating the full texts, 97 studies met the inclusion criteria. GO is generally synthesized using a modified Hummers method, and this approach consistently enhances the tensile and compressive strengths of the scaffolds. Furthermore, this method also helps improve osteoblast adhesion, ALP activity, the expression of osteogenic genes such as Runx2, BMP-2, and OCN, as well as the in vitro biomineralization process. In vivo, GO-based scaffolds increased bone volume to total volume (BV/TV) by 1.5 to 2 times compared to controls in various matrix types, supporting the integration of angiogenesis and osteogenesis, and promoting macrophage polarization from M1 to M2. These three aspects exhibit dose-dependent response patterns, with the best effects observed at low to moderate GO concentrations, while adverse effects (cytotoxicity, pro-inflammatory responses) occur at higher doses. GO-based scaffolds are a promising option for bone regeneration; however, further validation in large-scale preclinical models is required, and long-term toxicology studies remain necessary to support their translation into clinical trials.

    Inside the Boardroom: Board Structure and Corporate Tax Avoidance in an Emerging Market [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background This study examines the association between board structure and tax avoidance among non-financial firms listed in Indonesia and responds to the future research agenda of a prior systematic literature review. Methods The study uses panel data of non-financial firms listed in Indonesia over the 2019–2025 period. Tax avoidance is measured by the effective tax rate (ETR). The independent variables are board size, board independence, board gender diversity, interlocking directors, audit committee, and sustainability committee, with firm size, firm age, leverage, and profitability as control variables. Results Estimates from the common effect model on 947 firm-year observations from 279 firms show that board size and interlocking directors are associated with a lower ETR (higher tax avoidance), whereas board independence, board gender diversity, audit committee, and sustainability committee are associated with a higher ETR (lower tax avoidance). All six hypotheses (H1–H6) are supported at the 1% significance level. Robustness tests by sector and pandemic period show consistent coefficient signs for board size, board independence, board gender diversity, and sustainability committee, whereas support for interlocking directors and the audit committee is weaker. Conclusions The study covers only non-financial firms and focuses on board structure; therefore, executive attributes and CEO duality are not included in the model. The findings provide information for regulators, tax authorities, investors, and firms regarding the role of governance mechanisms in corporate tax behavior. This study addresses the limited evidence from developing countries, adds the sustainability committee as a board committee besides the audit committee, and tests board gender diversity in the Indonesian corporate context.

    Background Hernández-Nieto’s Content Validity Coefficient (CVC) includes a correction for chance agreement (Pei) suited to small expert panels, the usual scenario in education, psychology and health. Conclusions CVC-Calculator provides a verified, auditable and reproducible CVC computation, and delimits empirically when the chance correction matters.

    Relational-Strategic Digital Leadership Capability and Digital Transformation in Indonesian Local Governments Led by Female Regional Heads [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Digital transformation in local government is often framed as technology adoption, while the leadership capability that enables elected officials to turn digital policy into institutional change has received limited empirical attention. This study conceptualizes Relational-Strategic Digital Leadership Capability (RSDLC) as a higher-order capability that combines a relational-political dimension (Relational Public Leadership) and a strategic-digital dimension (Digital Leadership Practices). It examines the association between RSDLC and digital transformation in Indonesian local governments led by female regional heads. Methods Drawing on Dynamic Capability Theory, we analyzed cross-sectional survey data from 300 civil servants in 12 local governments. We used Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling with a disjoint two-stage higher-order approach, in which RSDLC was specified as a formative construct. Digital transformation was measured as perceived by respondents. Results The measurement model showed satisfactory reliability, convergent validity, and discriminant validity (heterotrait-monotrait ratio ≤ 0.42). Relational Public Leadership (outer weight = 0.52) and Digital Leadership Practices (outer weight = 0.68) both contributed significantly to RSDLC. RSDLC was positively associated with digital transformation (β = 0.36, 95% confidence interval [0.26, 0.46]; effect size f2 = 0.15), but it explained only 12.9% of the variance in digital transformation. Conclusions Relational and strategic-digital leadership capabilities are modestly but reliably associated with perceived digital transformation. Technological, organizational, and contextual factors not measured here probably account for most of its variation. The cross-sectional, single-source design does not allow causal interpretation. Because all respondents worked in local governments led by female regional heads, whether RSDLC applies regardless of the leader’s gender should be tested in comparative studies using measurement invariance analysis.

    Sustainable Cold Chain Logistics for Fisheries: A Systematic Review of Facility Location–Allocation and Coastal Spatial Planning [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Abstract* Fishery products are prone to quality deterioration during post-harvest handling and distribution. Therefore, the development of a sustainable cold chain requires the integration of facility planning, distribution efficiency, coastal zone management, and socio-ecological considerations. This study aims to synthesize these approaches and identify gaps in their integration. A systematic review was conducted through a literature search in the Scopus database, with reporting guided by PRISMA 2020. The initial search yielded 396 records; 222 records were retained after applying the publication year restriction of 2016–2026. Screening for document type, language, and access resulted in 134 records for title and abstract evaluation. A total of 37 publications proceeded to the eligibility assessment stage, and 12 publications were reported in a qualitative-thematic synthesis based on four research questions. The synthesis identified four main themes: location-allocation optimization and low-emission distribution; coastal spatial-ecological planning; facility accessibility and socioeconomic resilience; and carbon traceability and accountability. The results show that technical, ecological, and social approaches make complementary contributions, but their integration into a single fisheries cold chain planning model has not been comprehensively demonstrated in the reviewed literature.
