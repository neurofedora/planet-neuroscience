    Does Green Governance Strengthen the Climate Finance  Low Carbon Development Nexus? The Mediating Role of Renewable Energy [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Climate finance plays an increasingly important role in supporting sustainable low-carbon development. However, limited empirical evidence explains how climate finance translates into sustainable development outcomes and how institutional governance influences its effectiveness. This study examines the relationship between climate finance and sustainable low-carbon development, focusing on the mediating role of renewable energy and the moderating role of green governance. Methods An international panel dataset covering 112 countries from 2010 to 2024, comprising 1,498 country-year observations, was analysed using fixed-effects and dynamic panel estimation techniques. Sustainable low-carbon development was measured using a composite index developed through principal component analysis. Mediation analysis assessed the indirect effect of climate finance through renewable energy, while moderation analysis examined the conditional influence of green governance. Alternative model specifications and sensitivity analyses were employed to assess the robustness of the findings. Results Climate finance exhibited a positive and statistically significant relationship with sustainable low-carbon development. Renewable energy partially mediated this relationship, indicating that renewable-energy development represents an important pathway through which climate finance contributes to low-carbon transitions. Green governance positively moderated the relationship between climate finance and sustainable low-carbon development, suggesting that stronger regulatory quality, institutional effectiveness, and environmental governance enhance the benefits associated with climate-finance investments. The findings remained consistent across alternative model specifications and robustness assessments. Conclusions This study provides an integrated empirical framework linking climate finance, renewable energy, green governance, and sustainable low-carbon development. The findings highlight the importance of combining climate-finance mobilisation with clean-energy investment and effective environmental governance. Policymakers, international financial institutions, and development agencies should consider strengthening institutional capacity and directing climate-finance resources toward renewable-energy initiatives to support sustainable low-carbon transitions.

    Serum Neurogranin as a Diagnostic Biomarker for Acute Ischemic Stroke: Performance Comparison Between Thrombotic and Embolic subtypes [version 3; peer review: 2 approved with reservations]

    Background Ischemic stroke (IS) is a severe neurological disorder that can lead to disability and mortality in adults. The management of acute ischemic stroke (AIS) requires the ability to predict functional outcomes. Blood biomarkers are valuable prognostic tools because of their rapid assessment, cost-effectiveness, and clinical accessibility. Given that Neurogranin (Ng) is a small postsynaptic neural protein, and when the blood-brain barrier (BBB) is damaged, Ng levels are altered in the bloodstream. Methods This study aimed to assess the concentration of Ng in patients with acute ischemic stroke. We investigated forty-six thrombotic patients, forty-five embolic patients, and forty-five healthy individuals. Serum concentrations of Ng, D-dimer, random blood sugar (RBS), lipid profile, renal function tests, liver function tests, complete blood count (CBC), and electrolyte tests were performed for all participants. Results The results revealed that the embolic group had the highest serum level of Ng compared to the thrombotic and control groups (0.600±0.339 ng/ml, 0.464±0.121 ng/ml, and 0.304±0.065 ng/ml, respectively). Moreover, correlation analysis revealed that serum Ng was positively correlated with some biomarker parameters, specifically platelets, total cholesterol (TC), and low-density lipoprotein (LDL), in the embolic group. Meanwhile, Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) Curve analysis yielded area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.879 vs. 0.897 for the thrombotic and embolic groups, respectively. Multivariate regression analysis confirmed that serum Ng was independently associated with stroke after adjusting for hypertension and diabetes (p≤0.01). Conclusion These results led us to conclude that the assessment of serum Ng levels suggests that it can be used as a promising diagnostic parameter for ischemic stroke patients during the early stages of the disease.

    Enhancing Women’s Financial Empowerment through Savings Strategies: Evidence from MAMIDECOT, Masaka District, Uganda [version 2; peer review: 2 approved, 1 approved with reservations]

    Background Women’s financial empowerment is central to achieving inclusive and sustainable development in sub-Saharan Africa. Savings strategies, particularly within Savings and Credit Cooperative Organizations (SACCOs), play a pivotal role in enhancing women’s financial inclusion, independence, and decision-making capacity. Despite progress, rural women in Uganda continue to face barriers such as limited financial literacy, irregular income, and restrictive social norms. This study therefore examined the influence of savings strategies on women’s financial empowerment in Masaka District, Uganda, focusing on structured saving mechanisms and the moderating role of cultural factors. Methods A convergent mixed-methods design grounded in the pragmatic paradigm was employed. Quantitative data were collected from 340 women members of MAMIDECOT SACCO through structured questionnaires, while qualitative data were obtained from interviews with 10 SACCO managers and focus group discussions. Quantitative data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation, and regression analysis in SPSS v25, whereas qualitative data were thematically analyzed to complement statistical results. Reliability (Cronbach’s α ≥ 0.70) and validity were confirmed, and ethical approval was obtained from the Kampala International University Research Ethics Committee. Findings Results revealed that savings strategies significantly enhanced women’s financial empowerment (R2 = 0.304, F(4,335) = 36.526, p < 0.001), strengthening financial independence, decision-making, and household influence. Group savings fostered mutual trust, accountability, and self-reliance among members. Cultural factors did not significantly moderate the relationship, indicating that structured savings mechanisms were broadly effective across diverse socio-cultural contexts. Conclusions Savings strategies are a critical driver of women’s financial empowerment in rural Uganda. Policymakers and SACCOs should expand gender-sensitive savings products, strengthen governance, and scale up financial literacy programs to sustain empowerment outcomes and promote inclusive economic participation among women.

    Prevalence of Asymptomatic Bacteriuria Among People Living in Imo State, Nigeria [version 2; peer review: 1 approved with reservations, 1 not approved]

    Background Asymptomatic bacteriuria (ASB) is an under-reported condition in low-resource settings. This study estimated the single-culture screening prevalence and described the bacterial profile, ASB awareness, and hygiene practices among public-housing residents in Obinze, Imo State, Nigeria. Methods A descriptive cross-sectional study included 150 randomly selected asymptomatic adults, from whom 150 clean-catch midstream urine samples were collected. Urine was examined by dipstick urinalysis and semi-quantitative culture on cystine-lactose-electrolyte-deficient agar; isolates were characterized by Gram reaction and conventional biochemical tests. Questionnaire and laboratory data were summarized using counts and percentages. Results All 150 screening cultures yielded significant bacteriuria and 150 isolates were recovered. Escherichia coli was most frequent (84/150, 56.0%), followed by Staphylococcus aureus (42/150, 28.0%), Enterococcus spp. and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (10/150, 6.7% each), and Klebsiella spp. (4/150, 2.7%). Only 3/150 participants (2.0%) identified the diagnostic threshold for ASB, and reported sanitation and genital-hygiene practices were variable. Antimicrobial susceptibility data were not available; therefore, no study-specific resistance or multidrug-resistance conclusions were drawn. Conclusion The study found a high single-culture screening prevalence and a predominance of E. coli. Confirmation with guideline-concordant repeat cultures and standardized antimicrobial susceptibility testing is required before persistent ASB prevalence or resistance patterns can be inferred.

PubMed, Google Scholar, Dimensions, Scopus, and Cochrane Library databases were searched until October 1, 2026, without any language restrictions. Conclusions Sedation has proven its ability for frequent treatment completion; however, cardiorespiratory adverse events and heterogeneous recovery remain problematic. Clinical and statistical heterogeneity, along with methodological limitations, have prevented conclusions regarding safety and effectiveness.

    Designing a community-based hypertension prevention intervention in rural, low-resource South Africa: a participatory mixed-methods study protocol [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Abstract* Background Hypertension is a leading modifiable risk factor for cardiovascular disease, with a growing burden in low- and middle-income countries. In rural South African communities, poverty, limited healthcare infrastructure, and fragmented preventive services hinder effective hypertension prevention and control. Although community-based interventions show promise, many have been developed with insufficient community involvement and limited methodological transparency. There is a need for reproducible protocols that integrate epidemiological surveillance, qualitative inquiry, and participatory intervention development in resource-constrained settings. Protocol We describe a participatory, convergent, parallel mixed-methods protocol to develop a culturally appropriate, community-based hypertension prevention intervention in Mopani District, Limpopo Province, South Africa. Discussion This protocol provides a transparent and reproducible methodological framework for integrating epidemiological evidence, community perspectives, and participatory intervention design. It is expected to generate context-specific evidence to inform culturally appropriate hypertension prevention strategies and to offer methodological guidance for mixed-methods intervention development in rural and resource-constrained settings. Findings may inform future implementation studies, policy development, and community-based prevention programmes for hypertension and other non-communicable diseases.

    Background Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) is a chronic disorder associated with multiple morbidities. Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) is the gold standard treatment, but adherence is often suboptimal. Aim This study aimed to evaluate the CPAP adherence levels in Jordanian OSA patients. It also identified the demographic, clinical, and socioeconomic predictors of adherence. Methods A cross-sectional study was conducted in three Jordanian hospitals with 232 adult patients diagnosed with moderate-to-severe OSA and prescribed CPAP for at least one month. Adherence was assessed by device-recorded data and self-reports. Statistical analysis included descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation, multiple regression, and ANOVA. Results The adherence rate was 58.6%, with an average nightly use of 4.2 hours. Adherence was positively predicted by longer duration of CPAP use (β = 0.48; B = 0.51, 95% CI 0.21 to 0.81; p < 0.001), higher monthly income (β = 0.22; B = 0.19, 95% CI 0.08 to 0.30; p = 0.001), older age (β = 0.10; B = 0.08, 95% CI 0.00 to 0.16; p = 0.046), and higher body mass index (β = 0.12; B = 0.14, 95% CI 0.02 to 0.26; p = 0.021). Patient-reported barriers to adherence included mask discomfort (36.2%), lack of support (23.3%), cost (20.3%), and noise (20.3%). Conclusion CPAP adherence rates among OSA patients in Jordan are moderate and influenced by some factors such as socioeconomic, clinical, and device-related factors. Targeted interventions to address financial barriers, improved device comfort, and psychosocial support are needed.

    The Role of the Constitutional Judge in Deriving Unenumerated Rights: A Comparative Study with Digital Rights as a Test Case [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Constitutions list the rights they protect, but no list foresees every interest that later needs protection. Courts in several systems have therefore protected rights the text does not name, and some have since drawn back. Digital claims, chiefly the right to be forgotten and the fate of personal data after death, now put the same question to courts whose constitutions predate the internet. This article asks how far a constitutional judge may derive rights that the constitution does not state without taking the place of the constituent authority. Methods This doctrinal and comparative study analyses the constitutional texts and leading judgments of six jurisdictions (the United States, Germany, France, Ireland, India and Egypt) from 1965 to 2022, alongside the non-exhaustive rights clauses of the Ukrainian and Estonian Constitutions. Each is examined against four criteria: footing, means of derivation, rights derived, and the limit the court placed on its own power. Results In none of the six jurisdictions does the silence of the constitution leave a right unprotected. They differ in the footing of the derived right, which tracks the durability of the case law. Rights drawn from a general clause written into the constitution have lasted and been built upon, whereas case law resting on a footing the judges supplied themselves proved more exposed to reversal, as Dobbs showed in 2022. Conclusions Three cumulative conditions are proposed: the derived right must attach to a text or principle in the constitution, be necessary to give that text its effect, and be framed in general terms. On this test the right to be forgotten is a controlled derivation, and digital inheritance requires no new right, only a balance between two rights the constitution already contains. The article recommends a non-exhaustive rights clause tied to human dignity and legislation on post-mortem data.

Implications affirmed that higher education institutions should establish dedicated industry liaison positions and adopt agile curriculum development cycles. Policymakers are encouraged to develop comprehensive quality assurance frameworks and diversified funding models. Future research should adopt longitudinal designs across diverse geographical contexts to deepen understanding in this domain.
