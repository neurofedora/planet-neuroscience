    Development of a Reading–Viewing Assessment Rubric for Popular Articles Integrating DRVTA, Sisingaan Local Wisdom, and Multimodal Literacy Materials [version 2; peer review: 2 approved with reservations]

    Background Reading-viewing assessments in Indonesian junior high schools focus on final results and literal comprehension, under-assessing students’ thinking, engagement, and cultural relevance. The Directed Reading-Viewing-Thinking Activity (DRVTA) model is mainly used for instruction, while local-wisdom instruments and multimodal-text assessments have developed separately. No rubric yet combines DRVTA and local wisdom to assess popular articles in line with Deep Learning principles. Methods This study used a Design and Development Research (DDR) approach with analysis, design, and evaluation stages. The rubric integrated critical reading-viewing theory, visual literacy, Barrett and Ruddell’s comprehension taxonomy, DRVTA stages, and Deep Learning principles into six aspects and 15 indicators. Four validators—two academic experts and two middle school Indonesian language teachers—assessed content validity. Lawshe’s Content Validity Ratio (CVR) was interpreted against the panel-size critical value and supplemented by qualitative analysis of comments. No student field testing was conducted. Results CVR values ranged from 0.00 to +1.00 (50%–100% validator agreement, mean CVR = 0.57). Five indicators received unanimous agreement (CVR = +1.00) and were classified as core indicators: identifying the topic and purpose, naming key terms, inferring implicit meaning, analyzing text structure, and assessing the relevance of local cultural values to modern education. Since a four-member panel cannot yield statistically significant CVR values, the seven indicators with CVR = +0.50 (75%) were retained as provisional indicators reflecting majority agreement. The three indicators with CVR = 0.00 (50%) were retained as provisional or supporting indicators requiring operational refinement. Conclusions The DRVTA-based assessment rubric, which incorporates Sisingaan local wisdom and multimodal literacy materials, demonstrates preliminary evidence of content validity based on expert evaluations. However, its practical feasibility, interrater reliability, and impact on students’ reading-viewing performance have not yet been assessed. Accordingly, the rubric’s provisional indicators should be refined and field-tested before wide implementation in junior high schools.

    A Comprehensive Needs Analysis for Developing a Traditional Game-Based Group Guidance Model to Enhance Emotional Regulation among Junior High School Students [version 2; peer review: 2 approved with reservations]

    Background Emotional regulation is an important socio-emotional competency that supports adolescents’ psychological well-being and positive social adjustment. However, students may require more interactive and contextual opportunities to develop emotional regulation through meaningful social experiences. This study aimed to conduct a comprehensive needs analysis as an empirical basis for developing a traditional game-based group guidance model to support emotional regulation among junior high school students. Methods This study employed a mixed-methods approach using a convergent parallel design. Participants consisted of 30 seventh-grade students and three guidance and counseling teachers from one public junior high school in Makassar, Indonesia. Quantitative and qualitative data were collected through questionnaires, semi-structured interviews, observations, and documentation. Quantitative data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, while qualitative data were analyzed thematically. Results The findings identified four interconnected dimensions informing model development: students’ needs, teachers’ needs, school readiness, and product specification needs. Students’ emotional regulation obtained an overall score of 58.77%, with the lowest scores observed in modifying emotions (56.83%) and evaluating emotions (58.33%). The student needs analysis indicated high interest in game-based methods (66.83%) and substantial needs related to emotional regulation (62.50%). Teachers’ needs analysis revealed a high overall score (66.56%), with the highest score recorded for needs and expectations for a new model (80.00%). Qualitative findings provided contextual information regarding existing guidance practices and school conditions. The integration of these findings informed the selection of three traditional Indonesian games—Bentengan, Bakiak, and Estafet Sarung—as experiential activities for the proposed model. Conclusions This study provides an empirically informed needs analysis framework for developing a culturally responsive traditional game-based group guidance model. The findings establish initial design requirements integrating students’ needs, teachers’ perspectives, and school readiness. Further research is required to develop, validate, implement, and evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed model in supporting students’ emotional regulation.

    Which Classroom Conditions Matter for Mathematics Learning: Bayesian Evidence from PISA 2022, in Colombia [version 2; peer review: 1 approved with reservations]

    Understanding which features of classroom instruction are associated with mathematics learning is a central question for education systems worldwide, yet distinguishing instructional associations from differences in family background and prior schooling remains challenging. This study examines the relationship between instructional quality and mathematics achievement using an international assessment framework. Colombian adolescents are among the lowest performers in the OECD mathematics assessment, and substantial variation in achievement occurs across schools, making Colombia a relevant context for examining these relationships. Drawing on PISA 2022, we analyzed 7,367 students nested within 260 schools using a Bayesian two-level model with a random school intercept. Three instructional constructs aligned with the ICALT framework were examined at the school level: classroom orderliness, perceived teacher support, and two facets of cognitive activation. Socioeconomic status, sex, grade of enrolment, and grade repetition were included at the student level. Approximately one-third of the variance in mathematics achievement occurred between schools before predictors were included (ICC = 0.36), declining to approximately one-quarter after their inclusion. Classroom orderliness and reasoning-oriented cognitive activation were positively associated with mathematics achievement, whereas perceived teacher support and thinking-oriented cognitive activation showed negative conditional associations when the other instructional indices were held constant. At the student level, socioeconomic advantage, male sex, higher grade placement, and not having repeated a grade were associated with higher achievement. The findings indicate that orderly classroom environments and reasoning-oriented instruction are positively associated with mathematics achievement, while the negative conditional associations highlight the importance of considering overlap among correlated, perception-based instructional measures when interpreting school-level estimates.

    Understanding the Inner Workings of Large Language Models in Medicine [version 3; peer review: 1 approved, 2 approved with reservations, 1 not approved]

    Background Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly influencing medical practice, education, and research. Their responsible integration into healthcare requires expertise in medical, ethical, practical, and theoretical domains. Methods We prompted GPT-o1 to generate examples illustrating how understanding transformer architecture can facilitate output interpretation. Key topics were extracted from its responses, and illustrative cases were identified and cross-checked against the peer-reviewed literature using Consensus.app, an AI-assisted literature-search tool. Results Five key topics were identified: (1) anticipating contextual focus in medical reasoning, (2) explaining “generic” or “textbook” responses, (3) understanding strengths and weaknesses in differential diagnosis, (4) explaining ambiguous or contradictory responses, and (5) identifying hallucinations in unfamiliar scenarios. Case examples highlight both benefits and limitations, including accurate attention to salient clinical details, reliance on generalized patterns, risks of base rate neglect in differential diagnosis, challenges of ambiguous prompts, and hallucinations in rare or underrepresented cases. Conclusions A theoretical understanding of LLMs is crucial for responsible clinical integration. Distinguishing between well-represented (short head) and underrepresented (long tail) knowledge, recognizing generic responses, and identifying hallucinations are essential competencies. Coupled with medical and ethical expertise, these skills will enable healthcare professionals to leverage LLMs effectively while mitigating risks.

    Predictive Models for Cognitive Decline Assessment [version 2; peer review: 1 approved with reservations]

    Background Cognitive decline has become a common sign of mental ability loss in mild cognitive impairment (MCI) and dementia-related diseases, such as Alzheimer’s disease (AD). However, early detection is difficult because traditional diagnostic tools such as brain scans are expensive, time-consuming, and depend on experts. Existing machine learning models show high prediction scores; however, they are uninterpretable because their logic is hidden, which limits trust. Therefore, this study aimed to develop a reliable and interpretable tool named COGNEUTEST, a clinical support cognitive decline assessment system. Methods This study utilized a tuned Random Forest classification model trained on comprehensive clinical features from the National Alzheimer’s Coordinating Center (NACC) cohort dataset. This framework focuses on the top 26 features, including patient demographics, medical conditions, lifestyle, and influential cognitive variables, to predict the objective cognitive status results. To ensure clinical accountability, a state-of-the-art SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) technique was integrated to interpret each contribution. Results Experimental findings proved that tuned Random Forest showed the highest performance in identifying three cognitive classes, Normal Cognition, MCI and Alzheimer’s disease dementia, on the NACC dataset, which achieved 86.05% accuracy and an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.9534. The system efficiently runs machine learning predictions and PDF report creation in an average of 13 s, and database lookups require less than 1 s. Conclusions This study contributes to the healthcare field by demonstrating the potential of providing a fast, transparent, and affordable assessment system that is accessible to low-income populations and clinics with limited budget. Limitations include reliance on a single cohort dataset and dependence on informant availability for Functional Activity Questionnaire (FAQ) variables. Future work should involve testing the model on other databases, exploring some deep learning models, and developing a separate model for cases in which a patient can perform self-testing at home.

    FUNCTIONAL MISALIGNMENT OF SECONDARY EDUCATION AUTHORITY IN INDONESIA’S 3T (FRONTIER, OUTERMOST, AND UNDERDEVELOPED) ARCHIPELAGIC REGIONS: A LEGAL AND GOVERNANCE ANALYSIS OF THE TANIMBAR ISLANDS REGENCY [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Law Number 23 of 2014 on Regional Government transferred the management of secondary education from district/city governments to provincial governments to standardize services and improve administrative efficiency. In frontier, outermost, and underdeveloped (3T) archipelagic regions, however, the transfer has weakened institutional responsiveness, lengthened administrative chains, and widened educational inequalities. This study examines the governance bottlenecks that arise from provincial management of secondary education in these regions, focusing on the Tanimbar Islands Regency, and proposes an adaptive governance framework for educational decentralization. Methods This normative legal study used statutory, conceptual, and analytical approaches to examine how Law Number 23 of 2014 divides authority over secondary education and how that division operates in practice in the Tanimbar Islands Regency. The analysis was complemented by a review of Constitutional Court Decisions No. 30/PUU-XIV/2016 and No. 31/PUU-XIV/2016 and a descriptive analysis of official sub-district education statistics (BPS Tanimbar Islands Regency, 2026). Results The statute attributes authority clearly, so there is no normative conflict. The problem is a functional mismatch between the provincial government, which holds formal authority, and the district government, which is closest to the schools. In archipelagic 3T areas, this mismatch widens the span of control and lengthens the administrative chain for school facility management. It coincides with a concentration of vocational schools and teachers in the regency capital and with a declining senior secondary net enrollment rate (from 62.44% to 52.80%, against 94.90% at the elementary level), although these descriptive data do not establish causality. Conclusions An asymmetric approach to educational decentralization, through shared authority mechanisms, archipelagic education units, or legislative amendments that return management to local governments, can align formal legal authority with local responsiveness while fulfilling the constitutional mandate on education.

    Implementing an Institutional Open Science Strategy: The Case of the University of South Bohemia in České Budějovice [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Open Science is becoming an important part of responsible research policy in Europe. Universities are expected to support open access to publications, adequate research data management (including their FAIRification), citizen science, and broader recognition of open research practices. The University of South Bohemia in České Budějovice (USB) has already undertaken several Open Science activities, but these activities have so far been only partly coordinated at the institutional level. Methods This case study describes the development and planned implementation of the Open Science Strategy at the University of South Bohemia in České Budějovice. It is based on the final institutional strategy, mapping of the current situation, consultation with faculties, and alignment with European and international Open Science frameworks. The case is structured around five key areas: policy implementation, infrastructure development, public engagement, career integration, and researcher support. Results The strategy defines a coordinated institutional framework for Open Science. Its main goals include a comprehensive open access policy, FAIR-compliant research data, mandatory Data Management Plans, a professional institutional repository for publications and data, stronger support for citizen science, recognition of Open Science activities in career assessment, and regular training for PhD candidates and research staff. The implementation framework also includes planned monitoring mechanisms, faculty-level coordination, and support from a dedicated Open Science Team. Conclusions The case shows how a medium-sized university can move from scattered Open Science activities to a structured institutional framework. The main lesson is that implementing Open Science requires not only technical infrastructure but also clear policies, training, coordination, long-term support, and recognition of Open Science practices in academic careers.

    Expanding the Boundaries of Climate Adaptation: Evidence from a Participatory Case Study in an Italian Military Prison [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Climate change is intensifying existing social and environmental inequalities, disproportionately affecting populations with limited access to resources, decision-making processes, and adaptive capacity. While climate adaptation governance emphasizes inclusive approaches, prison populations remain systematically excluded, despite their structural dependence on total institutions and heightened exposure to climate risks. Moving beyond a purely educational framework, the study explores two central research questions: how does the condition of confinement, characterized by near-zero environmental agency, shape the perception of global climate risks? And what can carceral facilities teach us about limits and possibilities of inclusive adaptation? Methods Building up on the Adaptation AGORA approach the study was conducted through an informal guided discussion, and a qualitative survey with twelve incarcerated participants explored the condition of confinement in relation to climate change perception. Results The research reveals that climate change is perceived as an immediate, physical threat, mediated by infrastructural rigidity and institutional dependency. Findings indicate that participants act as “territorial witnesses”, leveraging memories of environmental degradation from their regions of origin to validate climate data. Notably, their responses highlight a profound link between social dignity and climate engagement, where the desire for “institutional responsibility” reflects a demand for recognition as human subjects. Furthermore, concern for “future generations” emerges as a form of paternal generativity, through which incarcerated individuals exercise engaged agency—reconnecting with the outside world by advocating for the protection of their offspring. Despite its exploratory nature and small sample size, this methodological pilot demonstrates the feasibility of participatory dialogue in highly constrained environments. Conclusions The study contributes empirical evidence to the field of carceral geography and climate justice, arguing that extending adaptation processes to prisons is not only a technical necessity but a crucial step toward inclusive citizenship and social rehabilitation.

    Beyond the hospital gate: a One Health narrative review of health care waste in Nigeria and its risks to workers, communities and the environment [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    The harm from health care waste does not stop at the ward. This narrative review brings together, from a One Health perspective, published Nigerian evidence on how health care waste is generated, segregated, treated and disposed of, who is exposed in the process, and what is known of its effects on communities and the environment. PubMed, the Directory of Open Access Journals, African Journals Online and the web were searched in October 2026, and 30 studies published between 2006 and 2026 were included. Health workers generally knew the principles of segregation, but practice lagged: in one hospital 21.7% always used colour-coded containers, and compliance was lowest for pharmaceutical waste. Written procedures, budgets and trained staff were present in a minority of facilities, and most waste was burned in pits, in the open or in small incinerators. Waste handlers were often injured, seldom reported injuries and were rarely vaccinated against hepatitis B, and workers exposed to incinerator emissions had higher blood levels of cadmium, mercury, lead and arsenic. Beyond the facility, studies found polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons and toxic metals above international limits in air near incinerators, elevated lead in farmland where hospital waste was burned, arsenic and lead in a river below a teaching hospital, drug residues in nearby water, and antibiotic-resistant bacteria in hospital waste water. No facility in a Lagos study treated waste containing antimicrobials separately. Health care waste should be managed as a One Health responsibility, with procedures and budgets in every facility, protection for waste workers, control of pharmaceutical waste and waste water, and a move from burning to safer central treatment.

    Beyond Adoption: Drivers and Barriers of Customer Mobilization in Digital Logistics Ecosystems. A PRISMA 2020 Systematic Review (2020–2026) [version 2; peer review: 1 approved, 1 approved with reservations, 1 not approved]

    Background Customer mobilization encompassing adoption, continued use, satisfaction, and loyalty has emerged as a central challenge in digital logistics ecosystems. Despite a growing empirical literature, no prior systematic review has synthesised evidence on drivers, barriers, and trends across the full range of digital logistics technologies and geographic contexts, leaving the field theoretically fragmented. Methods Following PRISMA 2020 guidelines, a three-cluster Boolean search in Scopus retrieved 125 records. After title/abstract screening and eligibility assessment on extended Scopus records against pre-specified criteria, 66 peer-reviewed articles (2020–2026) were included. Quality was assessed using an adapted JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist (mean score: 7.71/8). Data were synthesised through narrative synthesis with thematic analysis and frequency-based construct mapping; frequencies indicate how often a construct was reported, not its effect size. Results Five trend clusters were identified: proliferation of autonomous and contactless delivery technologies, expansion of platform-based and crowdsourced logistics, mainstreaming of sustainability as a consumer concern, reconceptualisation of service quality toward experiential and trust-based dimensions, and growing geographic diversification of research. Perceived usefulness (n = 17), service quality (n = 10), perceived ease of use (n = 10), and trust (n = 7) were the most frequently reported drivers. Complexity, infrastructure limitations, perceived costs, and low perceived usefulness were each reported as barriers in seven studies. Five systematic gaps were identified, centred on longitudinal evidence, cross-cultural comparison, policy and institutional research, supply-side analysis, and methodological diversification. Conclusions The synthesis yields three propositions for future testing: perceived usefulness as a recurring mediating construct, trust deficits and complexity as co-occurring barriers, and an attitude-behaviour gap in digital logistics sustainability, with early experimental evidence favouring moral nudges over financial incentives. Implications are outlined for platform operators, service providers, policymakers, and investors.

    Mind-wandering preserves word-level coupling while disrupting comprehension

    arXiv:2611.14814v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Readers can move their eyes through a page but retain little of its meaning when their minds wander. We tested whether word properties still guide their eyes during these lapses. We analyzed public eye-tracking and electroencephalography (EEG) data from 44 adults who read articles. They marked the text read during each lapse they noticed. We linked these spans to the sentences needed for later comprehension questions. Word frequency, length and surprisal still predicted which words readers skipped and how long they looked at them. Compared with on-task reading, these effects met equivalence bounds of $\pm$10% for skipping and $\pm$20% for gaze duration. Readers spent 14.6% longer on words during lapses. Yet readers answered less accurately when lapses covered sentences needed for a later question. The adjusted difference was 25.7 percentage points for lapses covering all versus none of these sentences. The fixation-related EEG response was 42.7% smaller during lapses. At left posterior-lateral sensor P7, the median increase in alpha power was 10.0%. Occipital sensor Oz showed no reliable increase. These results suggest that word properties can guide the eyes while readers fail to form or retain a useful representation of the text.

    Same currents, different field: $\Delta f = 0$ carrier-only stimulation is not temporal interference without the beat

    arXiv:2611.14839v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Carrier-only stimulation ($\Delta f = 0$) is generally considered the most rigorous control for temporal interference (TI) stimulation, assumed to reproduce TI's kilohertz carrier field without its low-frequency beat.     arXiv:2611.15046v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Deep learning has driven substantial progress in modeling fMRI responses, yet most existing approaches are optimized for a single objective---such as neural encoding, stimulus decoding, or post hoc interpretation---making it difficult to obtain a coherent account of neural representations.     Dynamical mean-field theory of latching Potts networks

    arXiv:2611.16999v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Spontaneous transitions between memories, as observed in internally generated cognition such as mind-wandering and free association, require neural dynamics capable of destabilizing one attractive memory state and moving towards another. We ask what minimal representational structure is required for such transitions to emerge in random associative networks. To address this question, we study Potts associative networks with $S$ active states per unit and slow adaptive thresholds. The $S=1$ case can model a standard Hopfield-like network of binary units. We employ dynamical mean-field theory in the extensive-load regime, where the number of stored patterns scales linearly with network size and connectivity, and we focus on transitions between two condensed memories, which are singled out in the connectivity matrix. In the finite-load regime, we show that transitions are governed by an effective drive term, whose sign and temporal evolution distinguish high-latching (structured), low-latching (random), and non-latching regimes. The resulting phase diagram shows that adaptation alone is not sufficient: transitions only occur within certain ranges of the overlap between the two memories. In the binary case $S=1$, in particular, no unit can be active in both memories but in different Potts states, thus strongly supressing the high-latching, structured transitions that exist for $S>1$. In the extensive-load regime, non-condensed memories generate colored fluctuations and retarded self-interactions through an effective memory kernel. We find that latching trajectories change the effective timescale of this kernel, a dynamical signature of transitions in the extensive-load regime.
