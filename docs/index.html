    Digitalización de documentos públicos: Hacia la eficiencia y la reducción de los gastos municipales [version 3; peer review: 2 approved with reservations, 1 not approved]

    The digitization of documentation has become relevant in city halls as a means to maximize management and reduce costs in the context of fiscal austerity. In Latin America (and most particularly in Peru), local governments are still confronted with many structural problems, including procedural fragmentation, a very high reliance on paper-based documentation, and widely varying levels of technology-based solutions. In that sense, digitizing documents is not only about advancing technology but also uses revised information as organizational solutions that reduce the risk that municipalities face legally and operationally and promote more efficient use of limited municipal resources. This study followed a quantitative methodology based on a non-experimental cross-sectional design. The study took place in a city government in the Peruvian Amazon. The population consisted of 125 administrative staff; a sample of 95 respondents was considered representative. Information was obtained through a structured questionnaire and was subjected to descriptive statistics and inferential statistics—regression and analysis of variance—to analyse the relation between the digitization of documents and cutting of municipal costs. The findings indicate a significant positive relationship between documentary digitization and operating cost reduction, especially cost savings in terms of paper, physical storage, and administrative processing time. Yet the results suggest that the effect of digitization differs according to the dimension of quality. Legal risk and knowledge management-related components better explain cost reduction than simple technology adoption. Simultaneously, information security and auditing procedures were rated as moderately performing, implying a possible operational gap, which refines the financial gains of digitization. The results showed statistically significant relationships between certain aspects of document digitization and the perceived reduction in expenses in the municipality studied. Given the cross-cutting and inter-institutional nature of the research, the results should be interpreted as contextual evidence and not as conclusions that can be directly generalized to other municipalities or countries. The results indicate that financial benefits are less tied to a simple presence of digital tools and more to the organizational practices around using these tools. In terms of policy, the results are consistent with a phased, fiscally realistic digitization approach that emphasizes staff training, standardized digital processes, and basic legal risk controls in a limited budget municipality. The potential contribution of this research is that it presents context-specific evidence from a Peruvian municipality in the Amazonas region, which could be used by other similar local governments to extract practical lessons for achieving efficiency gains in the face of resource constraints.

    Awareness and Perceptions of Health Promotion Strategies Among Caregivers to Reduce Child Mortality in Mount Fletcher, Eastern Cape, South Africa [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Child mortality remains a major public health challenge in low- and middle-income countries, with Sub-Saharan Africa carrying a disproportionate share of the global burden. In South Africa, rural and under-resourced areas such as Mount Fletcher in the Eastern Cape continue to record high rates of preventable under-five deaths. The effectiveness of national and provincial health promotion strategies depends heavily on local awareness, acceptance, and participation, yet little is known about how caregivers in rural communities such as Mount Fletcher understand and experience these interventions. Objective To explore the awareness and perceptions of health promotion strategies among caregivers to reduce child mortality in Mount Fletcher, and to identify the barriers and facilitators influencing their uptake. Methods A qualitative descriptive study was conducted among 20 mothers and primary caregivers of children under five years attending Taylor Bequest Clinic in Mount Fletcher, Elundini Local Municipality. Participants were selected through purposive sampling and took part in semi-structured, audio-recorded interviews conducted in isiXhosa or English. Data were transcribed verbatim and analysed thematically using ATLAS.ti (version 8), guided by the Health Belief Model. Results Participants demonstrated good awareness of core child-survival interventions, including immunisation, exclusive breastfeeding, nutrition, hygiene, and clinic attendance, and generally held positive attitudes toward health promotion services and healthcare workers. However, participants identified substantial barriers to the uptake of these strategies, including poverty, transport costs and distance to health facilities, long clinic waiting times and staff shortages, and the continued influence of traditional and cultural beliefs on health-seeking behaviour. Conclusion Caregivers in Mount Fletcher are broadly aware of, and hold favourable attitudes toward, child health promotion strategies, but structural, economic, and cultural barriers continue to limit their consistent use. Strengthening community health worker outreach, addressing health system constraints, and engaging traditional and community leaders alongside biomedical health education may help translate existing awareness into more consistent uptake of child mortality reduction interventions in similar rural South African settings.

    Sex Differences in the Prevalence and Correlates of Sleep Disturbance Among Indonesian Stroke Survivors: A Population-Based Study of 7,147 Individuals [version 3; peer review: 2 approved with reservations, 1 not approved]

    Objectives Sleep disturbance is a prevalent issue among those who have experienced a stroke. The impact of gender on the prevalence of sleep disturbance within Asian stroke populations remains ambiguous. This research seeks to examine the prevalence of sleep disturbance among stroke survivors in Indonesia. Methods This quantitative prevalence analysis was based on the 2018 Indonesian Basic Health Research. A total of 7,147 stroke survivors who answered the sleep disturbance questions were included. The estimated prevalence and regression models were examined utilizing SPSS software. Results The total prevalence of sleep disturbance among stroke survivors in Indonesia was 28.4% (95% CI: 27.4%–29.4%). Females exhibited a greater frequency of sleep disturbance than males (34.4% versus 26.1%, respectively). When categorized by age group, both females and males exhibited a trend of rising sleep disturbance prevalence with time. Moreover, daily smoking, low educational attainment, rural residency, and insufficient physical activity were correlated with sleep disturbance symptoms in the male cohort. In females, the presence of comorbidities such as cardiovascular disease and asthma was associated with sleep disturbance. Conclusions This study’s findings revealed a significant prevalence of sleep disturbance among stroke survivors in Indonesia. Our findings indicate a gender influence on sleep disturbance prevalence, with females exhibiting a higher prevalence than males. These findings underscore the significance of sleep assessment and treatment in clinical or community environments.

    A Work Cycle with Thermal Efficiency Exceeding the Carnot Limit in a Centrifugal Field [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Classical thermodynamics and the Carnot theorem apply only to uniform, field-free gases, relying on separate hot/cold reservoirs and artificial compression with temperature-limited efficiency. Centrifugal force creates spontaneous radial pressure, density and temperature gradients unaccounted for in traditional theory, merging hot and cold regions without isolated heat sinks. While gravity-based high-efficiency gas cycles have been validated, centrifugal-field equivalents remain poorly studied. This paper develops a centrifugal gas cycle to test if its thermal efficiency surpasses the Carnot limit and assesses miniaturization using high-molecular-mass working fluids. Methods Using molecular dynamics and energy equipartition, we derived isothermal and non-isothermal radial temperature-pressure relations for ideal gases in quasi-static centrifugal fields. Two cycle modes were modelled: external work-driven radial gas transport and buoyancy-assisted heating circulation. We calculated input work (W1), output expansion work (W 2), net work, direct thermal efficiency and heat pump-boosted efficiency across varied rotation speed, gas molar mass, molecular freedom and heating temperature rise (△T). A lab device filled with octafluoropropane (C3F8) measured motor and semiconductor heating power to validate theoretical efficiency. Results Centrifugal equilibrium generates radial temperature gradients that violate the Zeroth Law’s uniform-temperature premise; heavier gases and faster rotation strengthen stratification. In work-driven cycles, buoyancy offsets most centrifugal force, making W2/W1 reach hundreds to over 1000, and heavy fluorocarbons produce large net work at low angular velocity (180–310 rad/s). Direct heating yields efficiencies of 1.55–1.75 at small △T, far above Carnot limits; coupling with a reverse Carnot heat pump delivers efficiencies up to hundreds at △T = 10 K. Lab tests record an average conversion ratio of 2.69 (P value =0.002), exceeding Carnot combined cycle bounds. Conclusions Centrifugal force fields invalidate classical thermodynamics’ core assumptions, restricting the Zeroth and Second Laws to field-free systems. This cycle removes artificial compression and cold heat sinks, outperforming Carnot efficiency, and heavy working fluids enable low-speed compact hardware. Modelling and experiments confirm self-sustaining energy harvesting, proving feasible miniaturized second-kind perpetual motion machines for low-grade waste heat and distributed renewable thermal utilization.

    External Counterpulsation for Non-Cardiac Conditions: Current Evidence from a Rapid Review [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    External Counterpulsation (ECP) is a non-invasive therapy traditionally used for refractory angina and coronary artery disease. The evidence for non-cardiac applications remains limited, despite the well-established cardiovascular benefits. The objective of this review is to evaluate the efficacy and therapeutic potential of ECP in non-cardiac conditions through a rapid review of current literature. A rapid review was conducted on PubMed for articles published up to December 2025. Eligible articles included clinical trials, observational studies, narrative reviews and systematic reviews that assessed ECP in noncardiac disease populations. Data were extracted based on the study design, patient demographics, intervention protocols, outcomes, side effects, and limitations. From an initial pool of 52 records, 16 articles were ultimately incorporated, encompassing a range of conditions including type 2 diabetes, long COVID, ischaemic stroke, vertebrobasilar insufficiency, retinal artery occlusion, intracranial stenosis, kidney dysfunction, fractures and exercise recovery. Most articles reported improvements in functional capacity, endothelial function, and cerebral and blood biomarkers. Limitations include the heterogeneity of treatment protocols and durations, as well as the lack of long-term follow-up. ECP seems to offer widespread benefits beyond cardiac health, particularly in ameliorating endothelial dysfunction, improving circulation, and enhancing regenerative capacity and neurological function. However, robust trials and disease-specific protocols are essential to validate efficacy and optimise clinical integration.

    Comparison of Mazdutide and Dulaglutide for Type 2 Diabetes: a GRADE assessed systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) remains a major global health burden. Although glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) such as dulaglutide improve glycemic control and promote weight loss, many patients do not achieve optimal metabolic targets. Mazdutide, a dual GLP-1 and glucagon receptor agonist, may provide greater efficacy. This systematic review and meta-analysis compared the efficacy and safety of mazdutide versus dulaglutide in adults with T2DM. Methods PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and ScienceDirect were searched through May 2026 for randomized controlled trials comparing mazdutide with dulaglutide. Outcomes included glycemic control, weight loss, cardiometabolic parameters, and safety. Data were pooled using fixed- or random-effects models according to heterogeneity, and certainty of evidence was assessed using the GRADE framework. Results Three RCTs involving 990 participants (mazdutide, n=683; dulaglutide, n=307) were included. Mazdutide significantly reduced HbA1c (mean difference [MD] −0.25%; 95% CI −0.38 to −0.12; p=0.0002) and body weight (MD −3.74%; 95% CI −5.29 to −2.19; p<0.00001) compared with dulaglutide. It also increased the likelihood of achieving ≥5% and ≥10% weight loss and improved blood pressure and triglyceride levels. No significant differences were observed in fasting plasma glucose or HbA1c target achievement (<7%). Treatment-emergent adverse events were more frequent with mazdutide (OR 1.69; 95% CI 1.16–2.45), mainly due to gastrointestinal effects, while serious adverse events were similar between groups. GRADE certainty was high for primary efficacy outcomes, moderate for most safety outcomes, and low to very low for infrequent adverse events. Conclusions Mazdutide demonstrated superior glycemic control, weight reduction, and cardiometabolic benefits compared with dulaglutide, with more frequent but generally mild gastrointestinal adverse events. These findings support mazdutide as a promising treatment option for T2DM, although longer-term comparative studies are needed.

    Background As commercial banks increasingly channel service delivery through digital platforms, employees’ own digital competence has emerged as a candidate strategic resource for customer satisfaction, yet its independent, branch-level effect remains underexamined relative to interpersonal and communication competencies. This study examined the effect of employee digital literacy on customer satisfaction at Stanbic Bank, Mbarara Branch, Uganda. Methods Anchored in the Resource-Based View (RBV) of the firm, the study adopted a quantitative, descriptive-correlational design. Data were collected from 199 respondents’ operational managers, frontline staff, field officers, and customers using a structured, pilot-tested questionnaire, and analyzed using descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation, and multiple linear regression in SPSS. Conclusions The study concludes that digital literacy is a statistically significant but currently under-leveraged determinant of customer satisfaction, and recommends structured digital-skills training, platform-specific certification, and closer alignment between technology investment and employee digital readiness. Implications for RBV theory, banking practice, and future research are discussed.

    Adaptive self-organized criticality in deep neural networks

    arXiv:2608.28431v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Deep neural networks are high-dimensional dynamical systems whose function depends on the stable propagation of activity and perturbations across many layers. Maintaining suitable dynamical regimes may therefore be essential for robust learning and for preventing dynamical instabilities during training. Here, we show that the global dynamical state of a deep neural network can be autonomously regulated by purely local homeostatic plasticity. Neuronal activity is inferred from responses across inputs, and individual synapses are strengthened or weakened using only the activity of their postsynaptic neuron. Without measuring any global network property, this rule drives networks from both subcritical and supercritical initial conditions toward a common critical state, characterized by conserved activity propagation and a vanishing largest finite-time Lyapunov exponent. When combined with gradient-based learning, homeostatic adaptation counteracts the training-induced drift toward supercritical dynamics, while revealing a competition between dynamical regulation and task optimization. Our results demonstrate how adaptive self-organization can be implemented in deep neural networks and how local plasticity can control their collective dynamical operating point.

    Bits per Spike as a Betting Game: An Interpretable Unit for Held-Out Log-Likelihood in Neural Data Analysis

    arXiv:2607.28779v2 Announce Type: replace-cross Abstract: Held-out log-likelihood is the standard currency for comparing statistical models of neural spike trains, and is often reported as bits per spike relative to a homogeneous Poisson baseline. The units of this metric are difficult to reason about: it is rarely obvious whether an improvement of, say, $0.34$ bits per spike is a large effect or a negligible one. This note develops an interpretation of held-out log-likelihood borrowed from game-theoretic statistics. If $L$ denotes a model's expected log-likelihood ratio to the homogeneous Poisson baseline, then we show that $-\log(\alpha) / L$ represents the number of heldout time bins of recording needed to reject the baseline at level $\alpha$ under a particular null hypothesis testing procedure based on a betting game. Thus, a simple re-scaling of the log-likelihood yields an intuitive metric of model performance in units of recording time which answers "how much held out data would I need, on average, to disprove the null." We illustrate the construction on head-direction cells recorded in mouse anterior thalamus, where a generalized linear model reaches significance in roughly $120$ ms of held-out data for a strongly tuned cell and roughly $11$ s for a moderately tuned cell.
