    Tutor Competence as the Pathway from Learning Service Quality to 4C Skills: Evidence from Urban, Coastal, and Highland Community Learning Centers in Indonesia [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Community learning centers (Pusat Kegiatan Belajar Masyarakat, PKBM) are Indonesia’s main providers of second-chance upper-secondary (Package C) education, yet little is known about how their learning services develop learners’ 21st-century skills. This study examined whether learning service quality develops learners’ critical thinking, creativity, collaboration, and communication (4C skills) directly or through tutor competence. Methods A cross-sectional survey was completed by 354 final-level Package C learners drawn by proportional geographic stratification from 4,530 learners in urban, coastal, and highland PKBMs in South Sulawesi, Indonesia. Adapted, expert-reviewed instruments measured the three constructs, and data were analyzed with partial least squares structural equation modeling using 5,000 bootstrap resamples. Results The measurement model met reliability and validity criteria, and full collinearity tests indicated that common method bias was unlikely. Learning service quality predicted tutor competence (β = 0.545, p < 0.001), and tutor competence predicted 4C skills (β = 0.548, p < 0.001), whereas the direct effect of service quality on 4C skills was not significant (β = 0.170, p = 0.146). The indirect effect was significant (β = 0.298, 95% confidence interval 0.188 to 0.408), indicating indirect-only (full) mediation; the model explained 43.1% of the variance in 4C skills. Learners reliably rated tutors’ professional, social, and personality competence but not their pedagogical competence. Conclusions Learning services in PKBMs appear to foster 4C skills only when tutors convert them into competent and relational facilitation. Tutor development, including visible pedagogical practice, should be the central lever of PKBM quality improvement, and tutors should be evaluated with multiple sources that combine learner surveys with classroom observation.

    Gut Dysbiosis at the Intersection of Depression and Addiction: A Microbiota–Gut–Brain–Reward Framework and Digital Precision Behavioural Health Agenda for the United Arab Emirates [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background The microbiota–gut–brain axis provides an emerging framework for understanding neuropsychiatric disorders. Depression and addiction, despite substantial comorbidity, are frequently investigated separately. This review examines whether gut dysbiosis contributes to vulnerability, symptom persistence and recovery through shared inflammatory, endocrine, metabolic and reward-related mechanisms. Methods A structured narrative review was conducted using PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science and PsycINFO, supplemented by citation searching. Priority was given to systematic reviews, meta-analyses, human cohorts, randomised trials and mechanistic studies. Evidence was evaluated for methodological quality, confounding, replication, biological plausibility and clinical relevance. Results Convergent evidence implicates altered short-chain fatty acid production, intestinal barrier dysfunction, dysregulated tryptophan–kynurenine metabolism, systemic inflammation, hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal-axis disturbance and altered affective and reward signalling. Human evidence is strongest for major depressive disorder and alcohol use disorder, whereas evidence involving opioids, psychostimulants, cannabis and polysubstance use remains comparatively limited. Dietary interventions, probiotics, prebiotics, synbiotics and faecal microbiota transplantation demonstrate preliminary therapeutic potential. However, methodological heterogeneity, uncertain causality, limited replication and insufficient evidence of sustained benefit prevent routine clinical recommendation. Digital phenotyping, wearable technologies and artificial intelligence may facilitate integration of microbial, metabolic, behavioural and longitudinal clinical information, although prospective validation and demonstrated clinical utility remain limited. Conclusions The proposed Microbiota–Gut–Brain–Reward framework conceptualises dysbiosis as a potentially modifiable contributor rather than an independent cause of depression or addiction. Clinical translation requires longitudinal multi-omic research, harmonised methodologies, mechanistically informed trials and integration with established pharmacological, psychological and social interventions. Digitally enabled precision approaches may support personalised recovery monitoring, provided interpretability, privacy, equity and clinical governance are prioritised. The United Arab Emirates offers opportunities for culturally responsive translational research, although implementation must remain evidence-driven, ethically governed and subject to rigorous prospective evaluation before clinical adoption.

    Sustainable Digital Transformation and Business Competitiveness in Emerging-Economy Organizations: The Moderating Role of Economic Sector and Organizational Age in Piura, Peru [version 3; peer review: 2 approved with reservations]

    Background Sustainable digital transformation is increasingly treated as a strategic lever for competitiveness, but evidence on the organizational conditions under which it pays off in emerging economies is limited. We examined its association with business competitiveness and tested whether economic sector and organizational age moderate that effect, separately and jointly. Methods In a quantitative, cross-sectional design, we surveyed 361 formally registered organizations in Piura, Peru, across the commerce, services, manufacturing, agro-industry, and construction sectors. A 30-item questionnaire measured sustainable digital transformation (economic, social, and environmental dimensions) and business competitiveness (operational efficiency, innovation capacity, and market positioning). Content validity was confirmed by expert judgment (Aiken’s V 0.81–0.97). The measurement model was evaluated with exploratory and confirmatory factor analysis (maximum likelihood), and hypotheses were tested with hierarchical regression (Hayes’ Model 3, HC3 robust standard errors, 10,000 bootstrap resamples). Results Sustainable digital transformation was positively and significantly associated with business competitiveness (β = 0.657; p < 0.001; R2 = 0.432; b = 0.660 in the full model). The six-dimension measurement model showed adequate fit (CFI = 0.950; RMSEA = 0.047) and discriminant validity (all HTMT ≤0.754). Neither economic sector (p = 0.816) nor organizational age (p = 0.691) moderated the effect alone, but their joint moderation was significant (robust Wald F(4,341) = 3.12; p = 0.015), concentrated in manufacturing, where the association was markedly lower in mature than in young firms (slope difference = −1.01; 95% CI [−1.59; −0.42]). The moderation held with a categorical young/mature age coding (F(4,341) = 3.42; p = 0.009) and across robustness checks. Conclusions Sustainable digital transformation was strongly associated with perceived business competitiveness among organizations in Piura. The association varied jointly by sector and organizational age, particularly in manufacturing; the cross-sectional evidence does not establish causal effects or generalizability beyond the study setting.

    OpenMBD: An Open-Source Multibody Dynamics Simulator for Biomechanics Research and Education [version 2; peer review: 1 approved, 1 approved with reservations]

    Background Multibody dynamics simulation is a core technique in computational biomechanics and mechanical systems research. Existing open-source platforms are either poorly suited to biomechanics researchers or lack contact mechanics and graphical user interfaces. No single lightweight Python-native package provides forward dynamics, penalty contact, physiological joint constraints, and a graphical interface in combination. Methods OpenMBD is implemented in Python 3 with three external dependencies (NumPy, Matplotlib, Pillow). The dynamics engine applies the Principle of Virtual Power with analytic Jacobians and a Recursive Newton-Euler algorithm to assemble and solve the equations of motion with minimal coordinates. Contact mechanics use a penalty-based nonlinear viscoelastic model with hysteresis, applied to ellipsoid body geometry. Physiological joint range-of-motion limits for all major joints of the human body are enforced by continuous penalty spring-dampers parameterised from normative data. Passive joint restraints (elastic, viscous and frictional) are defined per joint about the parent-body axes and are parameterised from published measurements. Numerical integration uses the Symplectic Euler scheme at a set time step, with the joint restraint terms treated implicitly. A Tkinter graphical user interface and a standalone browser-based JSON model and simulation editor are provided. Results OpenMBD is distributed with model files: an adult male, an adult female, a humanoid robot, a car and a bicycle, defined in an open JSON format. Use cases are presented covering fall, sport collisions and custom model definition using the JSON format and browser-based editor. Three output files are automatically generated per simulation run: an output CSV, a summary text file, and a GIF animation. Conclusions OpenMBD addresses a genuine gap in the open-source biomechanics software landscape by providing a Python-native forward dynamics simulator that is installation-trivial, GUI-accessible, and biomechanically parameterised. The software is released under the GNU General Public License v3 and is available at https://gtbiomech.github.io/OpenMBD/.

    Crisis-Adjusted Evidence on Public Debt and Economic Growth in Indonesia: An Open-Economy ARDL Approach [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Empirical evidence on the public debt and economic growth nexus remains mixed. In emerging open economies, the estimated relationship may be distorted by major crises, reverse causality, and denominator effects in the debt-to-GDP ratio. This study re-examines the association between government debt and economic growth in Indonesia while explicitly accounting for crisis-induced instability. Methods Annual data for 1990 to 2024 from the World Development Indicators and the IMF World Economic Outlook were used. An open-economy Autoregressive Distributed Lag (ARDL) model relates real GDP growth to the change in the government debt ratio, world GDP growth, and changes in the current account balance and the interest rate. A baseline ARDL(4,4,4,4,3) is compared with a crisis-augmented ARDL(3,1,0,0,0,1) that adds a dummy for 1998 to 1999, 2008, and 2020 to 2021. Unit root, bounds, residual diagnostic, and CUSUM of squares tests are applied. Results Real GDP, the debt ratio, and world GDP are integrated of order one and stationary in first differences. The bounds F-statistics (10.45 and 14.13) exceed the 1% upper bounds, although the t-statistics support a long-run relationship only at the 10% level in the crisis-augmented model. The error-correction terms are negative and significant (−0.44 and − 0.69). The long-run debt coefficient is negative but insignificant in the baseline model (−0.23) and negative and significant in the crisis-augmented model (−0.21, p < 0.05). A rise in the debt ratio has no measurable effect on growth in the same year but lowers growth one year later (−0.14, p < 0.01). The long-run debt coefficient loses significance when the large debt-ratio changes of 1997 and 2000 are controlled for. Conclusions Accounting for crises yields a negative long-run association between debt accumulation and growth in Indonesia, with the growth drag appearing after one year, but the evidence is sensitive to the debt surge around the Asian Financial Crisis and should be read as suggestive. The estimates are dynamic associations rather than causal effects. Debt sustainability assessments should monitor the pace of debt accumulation and distinguish crisis-related borrowing from ordinary accumulation.

    Performance Analysis of Simulated Annealing and Genetic Algorithm on Systems of Linear Equations [version 2; peer review: 1 approved, 1 not approved]

    Solving large-scale systems of simultaneous linear equations remains a fundamental constraint in real-time engineering applications. While deterministic numerical methods provide exact solutions for well-conditioned systems, their computational efficiency drastically deteriorates (O(n3)) or completely fails under ill-conditioned, non-square, or highly sparse structural constraints. This study presents a rigorous performance analysis comparing a single-point trajectory-based meta-heuristic, Simulated Annealing (SA), and a population-based meta-heuristic, the Genetic Algorithm (GA), for optimizing solutions to simultaneous linear equations. Simulated in MATLAB across varying sample sizes of unknown variables, both algorithms generated randomized initial solution sets and candidate populations to locate global optima via residual norm minimization. Our strengthened empirical findings reveal that while GA maintains a significantly lower average generation count due to parallelized population diversity, SA consistently and systematically outperforms GA by demanding a vastly lower total fitness function evaluation count and demonstrating superior mathematical escape parameters from premature local minima. This structural analysis provides an essential quantitative computational baseline for scaling meta-heuristics to complex, non-linear simultaneous engineering systems.

    Performance-Metacognition Dissociation during Fear Memory Recall: Role of Task Sequence and Prefrontal TMS

    Metacognitive processing—the ability to monitor and evaluate one's decisions—has been extensively studied in perceptual tasks but never examined in fear conditioning. We investigated whether metacognitive sensitivity can be quantified in emotional learning and how testing order and neuromodulation of the anterior prefrontal cortex (aPFC) influence metacognitive access to the memory of a shock-associated cue. Eighty-one participants (51 females and 30 males) underwent fear conditioning and received either active or sham TMS over the aPFC. They were tested with implicit (skin conductance) and explicit (two-interval forced-choice with confidence ratings) tasks in counterbalanced order. Behavioral performance (d') and metacognitive sensitivity (meta-d') were quantified using signal detection theory. While d' remained stable across conditions, meta-d' and confidence were significantly higher when the explicit task preceded the implicit task. TMS did not affect metacognitive sensitivity. To our knowledge, these results provide the first evidence that metacognitive frameworks can be applied to a fear-conditioning paradigm, revealing that confidence judgments are dynamically modulated by task sequence and remain intact even when bodily arousal is reduced. We propose that metacognitive sensitivity may decline faster than first-order performance, pointing to a functional dissociation between decision accuracy and metacognition in emotionally salient contexts.

    Entangled stoichiometric objectives shape microbial catabolism

    by Maaike Remeijer, Frank J. Bruggeman

    The understanding of fundamental relationships between energetic and biosynthetic parameters of catabolism and anabolism is a central challenge in microbiology. The large diversity of microbial metabolisms can be compared on the basis of our current definitions of catabolism and anabolism. Catabolism has two functions: synthesising charged energy carriers and carbon precursors. Energy carriers, however, are also involved in precursor synthesis, complicating the analysis. We introduce an unbiased method to quantify stoichiometric relationships between catabolism and anabolism in genome-scale metabolic models. We compare catabolic strategies across diverse (heterotrophic) microbial species and growth conditions, focusing on states of maximal biomass yield per unit of energy source. Catabolic networks turn out to vary greatly, due to variation in the energy and carbon source for growth and the requirement to make suitable precursors for anabolism. We find that the ATP requirement for 1 gram biomass ranges between 72.8 and 148.0 mmol, precursor sets range between 4 and 14 in size, and acetyl-CoA is the only universal precursor across species. An important varying factor is the amount of ATP that is produced or consumed during precursor synthesis. This causes the remaining ATP demand, which is produced in catabolism, to vary. We conclude that the diversity of heterotrophic catabolism results from entanglement between charging of energy carriers and precursor synthesis. The state of maximal energetic efficiency is reached by maximizing the yield of charged energy carriers during precursor synthesis and simultaneously minimizing the demand for charged energy carriers in the conversion of precursors into macromolecules. Our formal analysis of catabolic diversity using a rigorous definition of catabolism’s two functions shows that microbial metabolism cannot be simplified in this manner; it rather shows that these two functions are intertwined due to the precise biochemistry.

    Handshake: Partner-specific protein-protein binding site prediction at scale using ProstT5 and cross-chain attention

    by Nurit Haspel

    Partner-specific protein-protein binding site prediction, identifying which residues of a protein form the interface when bound to a specific partner, remains a challenging task with significant implications for drug discovery and understanding of protein structure and function. Existing computational methods are limited by small training datasets, inconsistent redundancy filtering, and reliance on three-dimensional structural information at test time. Here we present a sequence-only, partner-specific protein-protein interface predictor called HandShake. It combines ProstT5, a protein language model pre-trained on structural data, with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), a cross-chain attention mechanism and a contact supervision head. Our method can detect both binding interfaces and pairwise contact matrices. We trained our model on very large datasets of non-redundant protein-protein pairs derived from the PPInterface dataset, the most comprehensive structural protein-protein database to date, and evaluated it on systematically filtered benchmarks at four redundancy thresholds (30%–90% sequence identity). We demonstrate that sequence redundancy inflates reported AUROC by up to 0.079 and MCC by up to 0.145 on identical models, representing a substantial methodological confound in the field. Even at 30% redundancy threshold, our results (AUROC = 0.811, MCC = 0.367, F1 = 0.45) exceed the best published sequence-only result on this convention. Our method also achieves comparable performance to existing partner-specific methods that use explicit structural information. The comprehensive training and evaluation dataset, in addition to the systematic redundancy inflation, can help gain insight into protein-protein interactions and the abilities and limitations of current detection methods.

    Children with developmental language disorder show selective impairments in low-frequency neural tracking of speech

    by Mahmoud Keshavarzi, Georgia Feltham, Susan Richards, Lyla Parvez, Usha Goswami

    Neural tracking of slow temporal modulations in speech supports extraction of the prosodic and syllabic structure thought to be critical for language acquisition by Temporal Sampling (TS) theory, yet whether these automatic cortical tracking mechanisms are altered in children with developmental language disorder (DLD) remains unclear. We recorded MEG while children with and without DLD listened to a story, and quantified source-level lagged speech-brain coherence and frequency-specific cortical functional connectivity across bilateral cortical regions. As expected a priori by TS theory, children with DLD showed significantly reduced coherence in the 0.9–2.5 Hz range associated with prosodic information in the story, spanning bilateral auditory and speech-related cortex. The strongest regional group differences were in left supramarginal gyrus (p < 0.001) and right inferior frontal gyrus (p = 0.001). In the 2.5–5 Hz range, linked to syllabic-rate modulations in the story, children with DLD showed reduced coherence in right temporal regions (all p < 0.02). Stronger low-frequency speech-brain coherence was associated with better language performance, indicating that individual differences in cortical speech tracking were behaviourally meaningful. No reliable differences were observed at higher modulation rates (5–9 Hz or 12–40 Hz). Exploratory functional connectivity analyses did not reveal network-level group differences that were robust across ROI time-series extraction methods. These findings indicate that atypical low-frequency neural tracking of speech is functionally relevant to language outcomes in DLD.
