    Dissemination of health research findings to local communities in low- and middle-income countries: a scoping review protocol [version 2; peer review: 1 approved, 1 approved with reservations]

    Background Dissemination of health research findings to local communities is an important component of responsible and high-quality research. Expectations to share findings with study participants and local stakeholders are increasingly made explicit in guidance from researchers, research ethics organisations, and research funders. However, observations from practice suggest that research findings are not always shared with the communities involved. This raises questions about accountability and how knowledge from local communities is represented in research outputs. Suboptimal dissemination may undermine trust, contribute to misrepresentation of local knowledge, reduce local ownership of findings, and limit their use in policy and practice. Objective This scoping review aims to map and analyse studies that examine dissemination practices in health research conducted in low- and middle-income countries. Methods We will conduct a scoping review of qualitative, quantitative, and mixed-methods studies, as well as relevant policy documents and reports. Searches will be carried out in PubMed, Web of Science, and the EBSCO Discovery Service platform, complemented by targeted searches of organisational websites. Two reviewers will screen records and extract data using a structured form. Findings will be synthesised narratively, focusing on reasons for dissemination, forms of dissemination, and the factors and mechanisms that shape whether and how dissemination takes place. Conclusions This review will provide a structured overview of how dissemination practices are described and analysed in the literature. It is expected to generate insights into factors and mechanisms that shape dissemination and to inform context-aware approaches to sharing research findings with local communities.

    Self-Regulated Learning in STEM Education: A Bibliometric Analysis [version 2; peer review: 1 approved with reservations]

    Abstract* Self-regulated learning (SRL) is one of the most important factors that support effective learning in the field of STEM education. The aim of this research is to document the present state of the world of research, determine the main research areas of SRL research in STEM education, and analyze future trends of SRL study for STEM education. The bibliometric analysis was conducted on the articles published in the journals indexed in the Scopus database using VOSviewer and Biblioshiny. Using Scopus data retrieved on 10 January 2026, English-language peer-reviewed empirical journal articles focusing on self-regulated learning in STEM education (2010–2026) were screened following PRISMA 2020 guidelines. Good interrater reliability was achieved during title-abstract (κ = 0.693) and full-text screening (κ = 0.832), resulting in 228 eligible studies for analysis. Keyword co-occurrence mapping links SRL to cognitive-metacognitive processes instructional design and technology-supported learning environments. Long-term themes and their focus include learning motivation academic achievement and metacognition defined as the thinking processes that play a role during the learning process. However recent topics emerging as key focuses have a more technological aspect than SRL as a whole including generative AI virtual reality and gamification which occur less prominently in the analyzed keyword network. The results recommend that a combination of the traditional SRL paradigm and new technology can be used in making the learning process more adaptive and process-oriented in learning STEM.

    Applications of Artificial Intelligence in Managerial Decision-Making for Sustainable Agro-Export Operations in Latin America: A Systematic Review and Bibliometric Analysis, 1996-2025 [version 2; peer review: 2 approved with reservations]

    Background Artificial intelligence is transforming agro-export management by converting production, commercial, climate, and logistics data into support for more timely decisions. In Latin America, however, evidence on its managerial applications and contribution to sustainable operations remains fragmented. Objective To synthesize evidence on the application of artificial intelligence to managerial decision-making and its contribution to the efficiency, sustainability, traceability, resilience, and competitiveness of sustainable agro-export operations in Latin America from 1996 to 2025. Methods A systematic review and bibliometric analysis were conducted in accordance with PRISMA 2020. Scopus and Web of Science were searched on July 26, 2026, yielding 2,160 records. Two corpora were analyzed separately: a bibliometric corpus (the initial export of 2,160 records, with the 1996–2025 filter applied to the annual series) and a systematic review corpus (73 studies included after database filtering, title and abstract screening, and full-text eligibility assessment). Bibliometrix in RStudio was used to analyze annual output, authors, countries, and sources and to apply Bradford's and Lotka's laws. VOSviewer was used to examine keyword co-occurrence and thematic clusters. This review is not a living systematic review. Results Scientific output increased markedly after 2021 and reached its highest level in 2025 (594 Scopus records). Scopus accounted for 98.95% of the 2,007 records published from 1996 to 2025. In the Scopus bibliometric corpus, China led with 170 publications (8.0%). Brazil, with 149 (7.0%), was the only Latin American country among the five most productive. Four thematic areas were identified: prediction and classification using artificial intelligence and machine learning; logistics, supply chains, and suppliers; sustainable resource management and climate risks; and digital transformation through Agriculture 4.0/5.0. Across the literature, artificial intelligence was found to reduce uncertainty and improve decision-making, although its effectiveness depends on data quality, digital infrastructure, and organizational capabilities. Conclusions The reviewed studies describe artificial intelligence as a capability that can strengthen sustainable agri-food and agro-export operations. Its impact depends on integrating algorithms with reliable data, technological infrastructure, organizational capabilities, and sustainability criteria. Only a subset of the included studies addressed a Latin American context, underscoring the need for more empirical research tailored to regional conditions.

    Development of a Value Clarification Technique (VCT) Learning Model Based on Tunjuk Ajar Melayu to Strengthen Civic Competence Among Senior High School Students in Riau Province, Indonesia [version 2; peer review: 2 approved with reservations]

    Background Civic competence, comprising civic knowledge, civic skills, and civic dispositions, is the core outcome of Pancasila and Civic Education in Indonesian secondary schools, yet students in Riau Province show limited proficiency across these components. Existing instructional models rarely draw on regional cultural resources, notably Tunjuk Ajar Melayu, the traditional Malay ethical corpus, as a source of civic values. Objective This study developed, validated, and evaluated a Value Clarification Technique (VCT) learning model that integrates Tunjuk Ajar Melayu (VCT-TAM) as the value source for Pancasila and Civic Education among senior high school students in Riau Province. Method A research and development design based on the ADDIE model was combined with a quasi-experimental pre-test and post-test control group design. Participants were 140 Grade XI students (70 experimental, 70 control) from four senior high schools in Riau Province. Civic competence was measured with the 30-item Civic Competence Scale for Riau Secondary Students (CCS-RSS), covering civic knowledge, civic dispositions, and participatory civic skills. Model validity was rated by content and instructional design experts. Results The VCT-TAM model obtained high expert validity (content M = 3.79/4.00; media M = 3.74/4.00). After adjusting for pre-test scores, the experimental group outperformed the control group in overall civic competence (F(1, 137) = 114.06, p < .001, partial η2 = .45), with significant gains in all three components. The adjusted effect was large (d = 1.81, 95% CI [1.47, 2.14]) and was largest for civic dispositions (d = 1.83), followed by participatory civic skills (d = 1.32) and civic knowledge (d = 1.24). Conclusion Within the limits of a quasi-experimental design, VCT-TAM is a valid and promising approach for strengthening civic competence. The model offers a transferable procedure for grounding values clarification in local value traditions, while its effects require confirmation through randomised and multi-site studies.

    This study aimed to evaluate the periodontal health status of geriatric patients with type II diabetes mellitus, evaluate its association with quality of life (QoL) using OHIP-14, and compare it with the periodontal health status of non-diabetic geriatric patients. Methods The patients were divided into two categories. Group A: geriatric patients diagnosed with type II diabetes mellitus (92); Group B: non-diabetic geriatric patients (92). All participants received a clinical periodontal examination. Measurements of probing pocket depth (PPD), clinical attachment loss (CAL), and bleeding on probing (BOP) were done. Conclusion More advanced stages of periodontitis were associated with poorer oral health-related QoL, whereas diabetes status was not independently associated with total OHIP-14 after adjustment for relevant covariates.

    Generative AI and Student Freelancing: Skills, Income Opportunities, and Barriers in Somalia [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background In fragile, post-conflict economies facing severe youth unemployment, digital freelancing offers an essential pathway to economic self-reliance. This study investigates how Generative AI Competence (GAIC) drives Freelance Digital Skill Augmentation (FDSA) and Platform Market Competitiveness (PMC) to enhance Student Freelance Income Generation (SFIG), while assessing the moderating impact of Structural, Financial, and Institutional Barriers (SFIB) in Somalia. Methods Drawing on Augmented Human Capital Theory, Platform Labor Theory, and the Capability Approach, a cross-sectional survey collected primary empirical data from N = 415 active university student freelancers across Mogadishu, Hargeisa, Garowe, and Kismayo. Data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM) in SmartPLS 4 with 5,000 bootstrap resamples, psychometric validation, and interaction moderation analyses. Results Generative AI Competence exerts significant positive direct effects on Freelance Digital Skill Augmentation (β = 0.548, p < 0.001) and Platform Market Competitiveness (β = 0.312, p < 0.001). Digital skill augmentation strongly predicts Student Freelance Income Generation (β = 0.384, p < 0.001) and mediates GenAI's economic impact. However, structural barriers significantly dampen this conversion (β = -0.198, p < 0.001), causing a 68.0% drop in income velocity under high-barrier conditions. International payment exclusion (lack of PayPal/Stripe) and platform KYC geoblocking are identified as the most severe institutional constraints. The model explains 61.4% of variance in student freelance income (R² = 0.614). Conclusions Generative AI serves as a powerful leapfrogging mechanism that mitigates local pedagogical voids and enhances global market access for youth in fragile economies. Nevertheless, technological competence alone cannot overcome structural exclusion. Developing accessible cross-border payment gateways and resolving platform geoblocking are urgent imperatives to unlock Somalia's digital youth dividend.

    Rethinking outcome-based education in economics teacher preparation: A technical-reflective-adaptive competence framework [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Outcome-Based Education (OBE) organizes curricula around measurable graduate outcomes, yet critical scholarship warns that outcomes frameworks tend to narrow toward what is easiest to specify and assess. This study develops a Technical-Reflective-Adaptive (TRA) competence model intended to strengthen, not replace, OBE in economics teacher education. Methods A two-phase qualitative design was used. Phase 1 applied a model-building approach, integrating critical curriculum theory, constructive alignment theory, graduate employability scholarship, and sustainability competence frameworks. Phase 2 used an illustrative single case, an Indonesian Economics Education program, to check the premises of the model. Item-level summaries of two audio-recorded lecturer interviews, shared with a companion study, were analyzed thematically and translated into design requirements. Results Four case patterns informed the model: a complete outcome chain on paper that was applied unevenly at course level, with final grades often not reported per course outcome; competence understood mainly in technical terms and OBE equated with project work, with no reference to reflection; assessment dependent on individual course plans without an institutional attainment system; and adaptive outcomes, such as entrepreneurship and internships, present but loosely tied to graduate outcomes. Each pattern was traced to a design requirement. On this basis the TRA model specifies three competence dimensions, five design principles (tri-dimensional backward design, course-level alignment audit across dimensions, mandatory authentic and reflective assessment, experiential learning paired with reflection, and a continuous quality improvement loop), and a curriculum architecture compatible with Indonesian OBE instruments. Conclusions The model converts the critique of outcome reduction into an auditable design requirement. Because the case evidence is small and preliminary, the model is a theoretically grounded proposal that requires validation through expert review, pilot implementation, and longitudinal study.

    Dynamic Effects of Renewable Energy Consumption on Unemployment in Africa: Evidence from a Panel ARDL-PMG Model [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background The global transition to renewable energy presents opportunities and challenges for African economies, where energy poverty persists despite abundant renewable resources. Evidence on the employment effects of renewable energy consumption in Africa remains limited, particularly regarding differential effects on youth versus total unemployment. Aim This study examines the dynamic effects of renewable energy consumption on total and youth unemployment in 14 African countries over 2000–2021. Setting The study covers Algeria, Botswana, Egypt, Ghana, Kenya, Morocco, Mozambique, Namibia, Nigeria, Rwanda, Senegal, South Africa, Tunisia, and Zambia, using annual panel data from the World Development Indicators and Worldwide Governance Indicators. Method The Pooled Mean Group (PMG) estimator within a Panel ARDL framework is employed, distinguishing short-run dynamics from long-run equilibrium relationships while allowing heterogeneous short-run responses across countries. Diagnostic tests include cross-sectional dependence, serial correlation, heteroskedasticity, and multicollinearity. Results Renewable energy consumption is associated with a statistically significant reduction in both total and youth unemployment in the long run, with the youth effect nearly twice as large in magnitude. Error-correction coefficients indicate that 50.5% of total-unemployment disequilibrium and 57.2% of youth-unemployment disequilibrium are corrected within one year. Credit to the private sector, political stability, and GDP growth are associated with lower unemployment, whereas FDI inflows and electricity access are positively associated with unemployment. Conclusion Renewable energy consumption reduces unemployment in Africa, with a stronger effect on youth. Policy should integrate renewable energy promotion with financial development, institutional strengthening, skills development, and targeted youth employment programs. Contribution This study provides empirical evidence from a region that has received limited attention, demonstrating that renewable energy consumption has a larger employment effect on youth than on the total labour force, and highlighting the need for integrated policy approaches to enhance the employment benefits of Africa’s energy transition.

    Case Report: Aortic valve Haemophilus parainfluenzae infective endocarditis complicated by a fistulating aortic root abscess and refractory atrial fibrillation with a rapid ventricular response [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    This report details the case of a gentleman in his 50s, with a background of previous bicuspid aortic valve infective endocarditis 28 years prior, presenting to hospital with non-specific symptoms, including shivers, headaches and myalgia. Objective clinical findings included pyrexia, hypotension and atrial fibrillation with a rapid ventricular response (AF RVR). He was initially provided with empirical intravenous antibiotics and isotonic fluids to treat an infection of unclear origin. Despite equivocal preliminary imaging findings, blood cultures isolated Haemophilus parainfluenzae, an organism implicating endocarditis, and his antibiotics were switched to Ceftriaxone. Even with this change in antibiotics, he consistently remained febrile, developed a significant Troponin-I leak, and his AF RVR exceeded 200 bpm – unamenable to pharmacological rate and rhythm control therapy. Transoesophageal echocardiography later confirmed an aortic root abscess fistulating into his left ventricular outlet tract, where the patient thereupon underwent an aortic valve replacement with patch reconstruction. Post-procedure, his AF was now responsive to Amiodarone, successfully restoring sinus rhythm. His inflammatory markers additionally improved before he was discharged home to complete his four-week Ceftriaxone course under the virtual ward – achieving a successful outcome. Especially in patients with overt risk factors, this case reinforces the need to maintain a high index of suspicion for infective endocarditis in patients presenting to the acute medicine with pyrexia of unknown origin. This case was, however, also remarkable for capturing significant AF RVR instead of the anticipated heart blocks with abscess formation and serves to emphasise the heterogeneity of Haemophilus parainfluenzae endocarditis cases.

    Cultural invariance in music-evoked emotions

    arXiv:2611.25189v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Worldwide, people produce and listen to music to induce, express, and regulate their own and others' emotions. To what extent culture influences the emotions evoked by music is, however, contested. The documented global variation in musical behaviours and emotion concepts suggests substantial cross-cultural differences in how listeners relate to music emotionally, while shared physiology and evolutionary history would predict broad global agreement on the emotional information extracted from music. Some evidence suggests shared processes but experiments on the subject do not represent global diversity in either music or participants. Here, we report a large citizen-science experiment wherein listeners (N = 124,394 in 189 countries; conducted in English) rated their emotional responses to popular Western music and to vocal music produced in mostly small-scale societies drawn from a globally representative corpus (i.e., songs assumed to be unfamiliar in style). We found strong similarity in listeners' emotional responses to music: Average agreement between pairs of countries' listeners was high. Furthermore, across both popular and unfamiliar types of music, emotional responses were shaped far more by individual listeners and the specific songs than by cultural background: Both between-person differences within a country and within-subject differences across songs explained substantially more variance in emotional responses than country-wise differences. Together, these results indicate that a listener's cultural background has only a marginal influence on the emotions they feel in response to music, raising the possibility of universal structure in emotional responses to music.

    System-level nonequivalence between integrated information and expected free energy in noisy permutation networks

    arXiv:2610.03735v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: The free-energy principle (FEP) and active inference describe prediction and adaptation using generative models, whereas integrated information theory (IIT) examines intrinsic causal organization. Can knowledge of a network's prediction in its current state determine its intrinsic integration? We address this question using a fixed one-step state-risk expected free energy (EFE) and IIT system integrated information. Noisy binary permutation networks allow causal wiring to vary while local transition reliability remains fixed. From a homogeneous current state, different architectures have identical complete one-step predictive distributions, and thus identical EFE under common preferences and observation models. Nevertheless, multicycle architectures have zero whole-system IIT integration, while single cycles can be integrated. For an n-unit noisy single cycle with reliability {\eta}, we obtain the exact expression $\phi_s^{(n)}(\eta) = \min\{2\eta^n\log_2(2\eta), -n\log_2\eta\}$. We prove that the minimum-information partition cuts exactly two causal edges, establish a unique interior maximum, and show that, in the large-n limit, the optimal reliability approaches unity, the joint specified-state probability approaches one half, and the maximum system integrated information approaches one ibit. Independent computation corroborates these results. Local surprisal-coordinate identities can be expressed through optimized variational free energy under exact inference, but this algebraic identity does not establish that a neural system performs variational inference. The counterexample establishes a state-conditioned non-identifiability limit for the specified EFE, not an incompatibility between IIT and the FEP. Bridging the two descriptions requires intervention-sensitive information beyond this single predictive distribution.

Expanding them to first order in the theta and gamma variables yields ten possible interaction terms; only the two friction type terms change gamma energy and produce PAC, and depending on the term and its sign the gamma bursts sit at the peak, trough, rising or falling phase of theta. We study a gamma friction that follows theta velocity as a simplified example. Because the time integral of theta velocity is theta coordinate, the gamma amplitude rises and falls with theta coordinate as exp(gvvx{theta}/2). The fluctuation dissipation theorem turns noise and damping into a theta effective temperature, and the PAC strength becomes a closed form function of one dimensionless number, kappa , that combines the coupling with this temperature. Without fitted parameters, this prediction accounts for 51,095 simulated records (R2 = 0.993) and for the median PAC of 5,160 combinations of coupling and damping (R2 = 0.989). The coupling also drives gamma out of equilibrium: its kinetic temperature grows far above the one inferred from its response. All coefficients enter linearly so that they can, in principle, be estimated from recordings by linear regression.     Why and When to Adjust for Intracranial Volume in Structural Brain Imaging

    Structural neuroimaging research is marked by substantial methodological variability, with important implications for inference and reproducibility. A central but often under-justified source for this variability is whether intracranial volume (ICV) is included as a covariate. We argue that ICV adjustment is not a neutral analytical step, but a modeling decision that fundamentally alters the research question by redefining the variance under investigation, and it can even change the very direction of the estimated effect. We evaluate five common research questions in the literature and argue that each is underpinned by distinct assumptions about where ICV sits in the causal structure linking neuroanatomy with other complex behavioral traits. We identify contexts in which ICV adjustment is necessary to define the construct under study, as well as situations in which it removes meaningful variance or introduces interpretational and statistical complications. We argue that ICV adjustment should explicitly be justified, and we provide practical guidance for deciding when such adjustment is appropriate. We hope to stimulate debate and improve consistency, interpretability, and reproducibility of population-level neuroimaging research.

    High-Fat Diet Feeding Rapidly Induces Sex-Specific Neuronal Plasticity in Lateral Hypothalamic Glutamatergic Neurons

    Hypothalamic circuits play a central role in coordinating feeding and energy balance, yet how these circuits adapt to dietary excess to regulate body weight remains poorly understood. Lateral hypothalamic area glutamatergic (LHAVglut2) neurons critically mediate food intake and body weight, but how they adapt their function to acute overfeeding and whether such adaptation is sexually dimorphic are unknown. Here, we examined the impact of high-fat diet (HFD) on LHAVglut2 neurons in mice. Whole-cell recordings revealed baseline sex differences in neuronal properties: female LHAVglut2 neurons exhibited higher basal excitability and differential synaptic input compared with males. HFD feeding produced distinct forms of neuronal plasticity between sexes within 1 d. In males, HFD increased intrinsic excitability while excitatory synaptic input was decreased. In contrast, excitability of female LHAVglut2 neurons remained unchanged, but inhibitory synaptic strength was selectively reduced. To test the causal relationship between enhanced LHAVglut2 neuron excitability and weight gain during overfeeding, we expressed the depolarizing sodium channel NaChBac to chronically increase LHAVglut2 neuron excitability. Chronic elevation of LHAVglut2 neuron excitability suppressed body weight gain during acute HFD exposure in males, whereas females displayed significant weight loss during HFD exposure. Together, these results suggest that female mice may be protected from diet-induced obesity via sexually dimorphic LHAVglut2 neurons. In males, overfeeding rapidly induces compensatory changes in LHAVglut2 neuron excitability that may act to limit body weight gain. Further enhancement of LHAVglut2 neuron excitability can suppress weight gain in an obesogenic environment for both males and females.
