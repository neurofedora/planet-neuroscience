    Prunus africana (Hook. F.) methanol bark extract ameliorates inflammatory activity and oxidative stress in asthmatic Wistar rats with diabetes mellitus [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Inflammation and oxidative stress are associated with many diseases such as asthma and diabetes mellitus [DM]. Prunus africana is used to manage various conditions due its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Aim This study assessed the effects of Prunus africana assessed oxidative stress and inflammation parameters in asthmatic model Wistar rats coexisting with DM. Method Thirty male Wistar rats [150–250 g] were weight matched into six groups; Control, asthmatic group induced with DM, asthmatic DM rats treated with insulin, asthmatic DM rats treated with insulin and salbutamol, asthmatic DM rats treated with insulin and methanolic bark extract of Prunus africana at a dose of 5 IU and 600 mg/kg respectively daily. Conclusion Prunus africana treatment showed some degree of potential in mitigating oxidative stress and inflammation in asthmatic Wistar rats induced with diabetes mellitus.

    Organizational and Psychological Determinants of Nursing Performance: A PLS-SEM Analysis Among Registered Nurses in Saudi Arabia [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Abstract* Background Maintaining high nursing performance is increasingly challenging in modern healthcare systems due to heavy workloads, workforce shortages, and growing psychological demands. Although work-life balance has been recognized as an important organizational resource, limited evidence has examined how it influences nursing performance through employee well-being and stress reduction, particularly in Saudi Arabian healthcare settings. This study investigated the relationships among work-life balance, employee well-being, stress reduction, and nursing performance among registered nurses. Methods A cross-sectional study was conducted among 427 registered nurses employed at The Medical City of Medina, Saudi Arabia. Participants were selected using proportionate stratified random sampling. Data were collected using validated self-administered questionnaires and analysed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM). Direct and indirect relationships were evaluated using a bootstrapping procedure with 5,000 resamples. Results Work-life balance demonstrated significant positive effects on employee well-being (β = 0.885, p < 0.001), stress reduction (β = 0.888, p < 0.001), and nursing performance (β = 0.219, p < 0.001). Both employee well-being (β = 0.283, p < 0.001) and stress reduction (β = 0.371, p < 0.001) significantly mediated the relationship between work-life balance and nursing performance. Conclusions Work-life balance contributes to nursing performance both directly and indirectly by improving employee well-being and reducing occupational stress. These findings highlight the importance of organizational strategies that promote healthy work environments, flexible work arrangements, and psychological support programmes to strengthen nursing performance and workforce sustainability in tertiary healthcare settings.

    Bridging the Perception–Performance Gap in Scientific Literacy: Evidence from Junior High School Students’ Self-Reports and Objective Assessment [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Scientific literacy involves not only students’ knowledge of scientific concepts but also their ability to explain phenomena, interpret scientific information, draw conclusions, and design investigations. However, students’ perceptions of their own scientific competence may not always correspond to what they can demonstrate through objective assessment. Examining both perspectives can provide a more comprehensive understanding of students’ scientific literacy and learning needs. Purpose This study aimed to examine junior high school students’ perceived and demonstrated scientific literacy and to identify discrepancies between the two across directly comparable domains. Methods A descriptive comparative design was employed with 30 junior high school students from two schools in Luwu Regency, South Sulawesi, Indonesia, selected through purposive sampling. Data were collected using the Junior High School Science Learning Needs and Initial Profile Questionnaire, which examined students’ science learning interest, project-based learning experience, and perceived scientific literacy. Students also completed a 15-item objective scientific literacy assessment scored dichotomously. Descriptive analyses were conducted, followed by domain-level comparisons across scientific explanation, data/graph/table interpretation, conclusion drawing, and experimental/investigation design. Results Students demonstrated generally positive engagement with science learning and substantial experience with project-based learning. Their overall perceived scientific literacy was 68.50%, whereas demonstrated scientific literacy was 39.80%, indicating a 28.70-percentage-point discrepancy. Across the four comparable domains, perceived competence consistently exceeded demonstrated performance. The largest discrepancy occurred in conclusion drawing, followed by experimental/investigation design and scientific explanation, while data/graph/table interpretation showed the smallest discrepancy. Conclusion The findings indicate that students’ perceived scientific literacy provides a more favorable picture than their demonstrated performance, particularly for higher-order scientific reasoning competencies. Combining self-report and objective assessment can therefore provide a more informative basis for identifying students’ scientific literacy needs. Science instruction should place greater emphasis on evidence-based conclusion drawing, experimental reasoning, scientific explanation, and students’ ability to reflect on their actual performance.

    Background Social conflicts mediated by local authorities represent a central governance challenge in rural and peri-urban districts of Peru. Although prior studies have separately examined institutional weakness, community participation and conflict transformation in Peru, none has quantified how deputy governors’ community representation practices specifically shape their mediation effectiveness; this gap motivated the present study. Methods A quantitative, applied, non-experimental, cross-sectional correlational-explanatory design was used. Conclusions Strengthening community representation management substantially improves the conflict mediation capacity of deputy governors, with direct implications for training programmes and inter-institutional coordination policies aligned with Sustainable Development Goal 16.

    Pulling Illusion in Individuals with Neurological Disorders

    arXiv:2609.10566v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: The pulling illusion induced by asymmetric vibration stimuli has attracted attention for its potential applications in rehabilitation and sensory assessment. However, the underlying mechanism of the pulling illusion remains unclear. This study addressed the central question of whether peripheral vibrotactile sensitivity alone is sufficient for the illusion to emerge or whether processing beyond basic vibration detection is also required. Neurological disorders can involve impairments at different levels of the nervous system, providing an opportunity to examine this question. Accordingly, we evaluated directional discrimination performance for the pulling illusion and fingertip vibration detection thresholds in 25 participants with diverse neurological disorders affecting different levels of the nervous system, from peripheral to central. Clustering analysis identified contrasting profiles, with high directional discrimination performance despite elevated vibration detection thresholds and chance-level performance despite relatively low-to-intermediate thresholds. In the generalized linear mixed model, motor-related signs, including hemiplegia and tremor, showed a robust negative association with directional discrimination performance, whereas vibration detection threshold was not robustly associated with performance. Furthermore, in participants with hemiplegia, directional discrimination performance was around chance level on the affected side and close to 100% on the unaffected side, despite stimulus amplitudes well above the measured vibration detection thresholds on both sides. Collectively, these findings suggest that the pulling illusion depends on perceptual processing beyond basic vibration detection, through which asymmetric vibration is experienced as directional pulling.

    Cortical information transfer reveals conserved hemispherical network dynamics across human handedness

    arXiv:2609.10870v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Whether human motor and brain lateralization arises from fundamentally distinct neural architectures or emerges from conserved network dynamics remains a central question at the intersection of network science and neurobiology. Conventional measures of cortical activation often fail to resolve how directed information exchange adapts to manual preference during complex motor tasks. This ambiguity leaves it unclear whether left-handed individuals possess atypical neural organization or follow shared dynamical principles. Here, we combine Permutation Transfer Entropy (PTE) with network-based indices to map directed cortical signal flow using electroencephalography (EEG) during high-precision motor execution (handwriting) in both right- and left-handed individuals. We demonstrate that cortical communication is strictly scale-dependent regardless of handedness: ipsilateral dominance occurs primarily in general, network-wide interactions, whereas inter-hemispheric interactions maintain a contralateral profile. Crucially, left-handed individuals exhibit inter-hemispheric information dynamics that are functionally equivalent to right-handers when executing tasks with the same anatomical hand, refuting assumptions of mirrored or atypical lateralization. By demonstrating that human cortical lateralization relies on dynamical principles conserved across manual preferences, these findings suggest that functional asymmetries reflect adaptive optimizations governed by epigenetic or social flexibility, rather than fixed structural divergences.

    Degeneracy along the sensorimotor hierarchy: motor control within a framework larger than redundancy

    arXiv:2609.11325v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Motor control has described the surplus of solutions available to the nervous system as redundancy, a term that names duplication: interchangeable elements, robust to loss but incapable of differential adaptation. Biology has had a second term for twenty-five years. Degeneracy names elements that are not interchangeable and are nonetheless isofunctional with respect to a given output, and it supports adaptability, since non-identical elements necessarily diverge in some context. Circuit neuroscience has relabeled its own results accordingly, while motor control has kept the older vocabulary. Neuromechanical models, by placing a spinal circuit in the loop with a musculoskeletal apparatus, bring the two traditions onto the same class of objects. We restate the Edelman and Tononi distinction for sensorimotor systems and derive an operational requirement: not the existence of multiple solutions, but their divergence in contexts they were not selected for. Three influential studies each meet part of that requirement and none meets all. We then argue that degeneracy and redundancy coexist along the sensorimotor hierarchy in a proportion that varies continuously, and that this proportion is measurable: computing degeneracy twice for the same configuration, once with muscle activation as the output and once with the movement produced, isolates what the musculoskeletal apparatus contributes. Five predictions follow, with the single outcome that would refute the proposal. We set out the adaptations the measurement requires in a nonlinear, non-stationary, closed-loop system, and what changes for motor control once solutions are no longer assumed equivalent: the question shifts from which rule selects a command to what the repertoire of the system still allows.

    Stationary covariance spectra of discrete-time non-normal random recurrent dynamics

    arXiv:2606.31944v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Principal component analysis is widely used to characterize structure in the dynamics of recurrent neural networks. For stationary noise-driven dynamics, the distribution of variance among the principal components is determined by the spectrum of the stationary covariance matrix. While the spectral properties of this matrix are well-understood for linear networks with normal synaptic weight matrices, our understanding of the stationary covariance spectrum for random non-normal dynamics remains incomplete. In this note, we use a free-probability approach to formally derive a closed functional equation for the moment generating series of the limiting stationary covariance spectrum of discrete-time dynamics with random non-normal Gaussian weights. This characterization allows us to analyze the behavior of tail eigenvalues in the critical regime: they decay with the inverse-square of their rank. In contrast, applying the same approach to the analogous continuous-time dynamics leads to an infinite hierarchy of Schwinger-Dyson equations, rather than a closed scalar equation. We conclude with some comments regarding the relevance of these results to comparisons of models of non-normal dynamics to neural data.
