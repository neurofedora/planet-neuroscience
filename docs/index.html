    Effect of positive psychology interventions on psychosocial health outcomes of school-aged adolescents: A quantitative systematic review and meta-analysis protocol [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background PPIs have been increasingly recognized for their potential to enhance adolescent psychosocial health outcomes. These interventions aimed to foster well-being and reduce psychological distress by promoting positive human attributes. This systematic review and meta-analysis aim to provide evidence for the efficacy of Positive Psychology interventions (PPIs) in educational settings and highlight their impact on adolescents’ psychosocial health outcomes. Methods A systematic literature search of electronic databases, including PubMed (MEDLINE), Scopus, CINAHL, Embase, PsycINFO, and Google Scholar, will be conducted following the PRISMA guidelines to retrieve interventional studies published in English up to 2025 to assess the efficacy of PPIs on psychosocial health outcomes in school-age adolescents. Interventional/experimental studies will be considered eligible if they report the efficacy of PPIs compared to a control group in school-aged adolescents. Studies with unclear definitions of PPIs, those with no control group, and those that relied on newspaper articles or Google data will also be excluded. Studies published in languages other than English and those with unclear data, case reports, conference proceedings, reviews, and letters will not be eligible for inclusion. The quality of the included interventional studies will be evaluated using the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) for interventional studies. The pooled effect sizes will be estimated using a random-effects model for heterogeneity. Heterogeneity and publication bias will be evaluated using I-squared index and Egger’s test, respectively. Discussion This review fills key gaps by systematically synthesizing evidence on how positive psychology programs in schools affect adolescents’ mental and social health. Despite some limitations, this study incorporates diverse data sources to offer a detailed and accurate picture. The findings will inform targeted interventions and policy responses to cultivate the impact of PPIs on health issues. Systematic review registration: PROSPERO CRD42024595381.

    TOS²CA: The Thematic Observation Search, Segmentation, Collation and Analysis System [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Thematic Observation Search, Segmentation, Collation and Analysis (TOS2CA) is an end-to-end system that allows the user to define, collect, and visualize data relevant to naturally occurring phenomena that can be used for current and future Earth science missions and research. Methods TOS2CA is a cloud-native, Python-based software library that allows users to define physical phenomena using a conditional threshold to identify occurrences of them in a dataset, tracking them spatially and temporally, and generating a series of masks for the user. The user can then continue through TOS2CA’s workflow and select a variable from any regularly gridded dataset in the system and request a subset of data that is interpolated and constrained to the bounds of the mask. This curated data can then be delivered to the user or visualized in web-based analysis tools that allow users to view multivariate plots and interrogate statistical conditions. Results TOS2CA has been developed for two distinct use cases – preparing to use data for studies related to NASA’s upcoming INvestigation of Convective UpdraftS (INCUS) mission and developing climatologies over wildfire burn areas in California. Testing the system with both use cases produced scientifically valid output files that were then used with TOS2CA’s visualization tools to perform multivariate analyses. Discussion TOS2CA can be used for a wide array of interdisciplinary research and applications tasks by allowing users to collect relevant data from multiple sources over lengthy time periods and wide spatial regions, to ultimately visualize results in a single, self-contained, end-to-end fashion that does not currently exist elsewhere.

    The Impact of Industrial Development on Rural Community Resilience: A Multidisciplinary Systematic Review [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Industrialization in rural landscapes, particularly within mining and resource-extraction sectors, presents a fundamental paradox: it simultaneously offers pathways for economic survival while undermining the socio-ecological foundations of long-term community resilience. Adopting the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses framework, this study systematically synthesizes 35 peer-reviewed articles published between 2011 and 2024 to provide a multidisciplinary assessment of these dynamics. The review identified four interconnected pillars that shape rural transformation. First, governance and power dynamics reveal how centralized decision-making, authoritarian environmentalism, and entrenched traditional structures constrain local agency and participation. Second, economic volatility highlights the persistence of the resource curse, which is characterized by livelihood displacement, income instability, and uneven benefit distribution ecological integrity underscores the progressive degradation of natural capital, including land, water systems, and biodiversity, essential for sustaining rural livelihoods. Fourth, as demographic shifts, labour migration, and socioeconomic inequalities contribute to community fragmentation, social cohesion is increasingly strained. Across these dimensions, the findings demonstrate that the perceived “benefits” of industrialization are frequently short-term financial gains that obscure the accumulation of long-term resilience debt, a condition in which immediate economic advantages are offset by enduring socio-ecological vulnerabilities. This study advances a multidisciplinary integrative framework that bridges governance studies, political ecology, rural sociology, and sustainability science. The core strategies of participatory governance, livelihood diversification, ecological restoration, and strengthening of social capital are emphasized. The framework provides actionable insights for policymakers seeking to reconcile industrial development with community-cantered sustainability, particularly in resource-dependent rural regions of the Global South.

    EEG-Based Discrimination of Autism Spectrum Disorder in Youth Ages 10–20 Using a Wearable Headband: A Pilot Study of Temporal Beta and Gamma Power [version 2; peer review: 1 approved with reservations]

    Background Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) diagnosis relies on behavioral assessment, which can be time-consuming and subjective. This proof-of-concept study asks if minimal EEG band measurements can differentiate youth with ASD from neurotypical peers, a step toward possible screening approaches; this small, single-site sample cannot itself establish screening utility. Methods Twelve participants aged 10–20 (5 controls, 7 ASD) were recorded with a four-sensor Muse 2 headband covering frontal and temporal regions. Five EEG bands — alpha, beta, gamma, delta, theta — were measured and analyzed per participant. No offline artifact-rejection pipeline (e.g., Independent Component Analysis) was applied beyond the device’s onboard preprocessing; this is treated as a central limitation, addressed explicitly in the Discussion and Limitations. Results Absolute gamma power at the right temporal sensor (TP10) and absolute beta power at the left temporal sensor (TP9) showed the largest group differences and the strongest cross-validated classification performance. Because gamma is highly susceptible to electromyographic (EMG) and motion contamination, and no validated artifact-rejection pipeline was applied, these findings are reported as candidate physiological screening features rather than confirmed neural correlates of ASD; movement or facial-muscle activity during unconstrained recording cannot be excluded as a contributor. Individual-level values for both features, overlaid on group summary statistics, are shown in Figure 5. Conclusion Beta at TP9 and gamma at TP10 emerge as candidate physiological features warranting further study, whether the underlying signal is purely neural or partly reflects physiological/behavioral activity such as muscle tension. Given the small sample (n = 12), the absence of validated artifact rejection, single-site recruitment, and no independent-sample testing, this study does not establish clinical or educational screening value, and no such application should be inferred here. Confirming utility will require replication in larger, multi-site, demographically broader samples, with simultaneous EMG/EOG recording, validated artifact-rejection procedures, and prospective testing in independent participants.

    Sentiment Analysis of Acceptance TVET Online Courses on the Skill Academy App from Google Play: Leveraging Text Mining with Comparison Machine Learning Model [version 2; peer review: 1 approved, 2 approved with reservations]

    Background Online Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) has expanded rapidly in Indonesia through platforms such as Skill Academy. User reviews provide valuable insights into user acceptance, but their volume makes manual analysis impractical. Therefore, this study applies text mining to analyze user sentiment and compare eight machine learning models for sentiment classification. Methods The study used 3,000 reviews collected via web scraping using the google-play-scraper library. The data was then anonymized, cleaned, translated into English, and automatically sentiment-labeled using VADER. The validity of the labeling was verified by comparing it with TextBlob and the original star-rating categories using Cohen’s Kappa. Eight classification algorithms Naive Bayes, SVM, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Decision Tree, KNN, XGBoost, and LightGBM were trained using a TF-IDF pipeline with GridSearchCV and five-fold cross-validation. Results The labeling validation results showed moderate agreement, with a Cohen’s Kappa of 0.500 for TextBlob and 0.533 for the original star rating categories. On the test data, Logistic Regression achieved the best performance with an accuracy of 85.48%, a macro-F1 score of 76.08%, a balanced accuracy of 80.86%, and a macro-AUC of 0.9486, followed by SVM and XGBoost. The sentiment distribution was dominated by positive reviews at 75.31%, followed by negative reviews at 15.57%, and neutral reviews at 9.12%. The SMOTETomek experiment improved balanced accuracy and recall for the minority class but reduced macro-precision. Word cloud and word frequency analysis revealed that the main complaints were related to the app being slow, resource-intensive, and prone to errors, while user appreciation centered on ease of learning and the quality of the material. Conclusions Research shows that logistic regression achieved the best classification performance. While user sentiment was largely positive, improving application stability remains essential for sustaining user acceptance, enhancing learning experiences, and supporting long-term adoption of online TVET platforms.

    Youth Labour Market Participation in Africa: A Cross-National Analysis of Employment, Unemployment, Sectoral Distribution, and Educational Attainment Among the 15–35 Age Cohort, 2026 [version 3; peer review: 1 approved, 3 approved with reservations]

    Background Africa hosts the world’s largest youth cohort, yet its labour markets remain characterised by structural imbalances, agricultural dependence, gender inequalities, and educational credential mismatches. This study provides a cross-national analysis of youth labour market participation across the 54 African Union member states in 2026, disaggregated by sex, age group, economic sector, and educational attainment. Methods A cross-sectional descriptive-correlational study was conducted using harmonised International Labour Organization (ILO) modelled estimates for youth aged 15–35 years. Youth unemployment (YUR), labour force participation (LFPR), and not-in-employment, education, or training (NEET) rates were analysed by country, sub-region, sex, age group, and education level. Pearson’s correlation assessed the association between YUR and NEET rate, while four nested ordinary least squares (OLS) models identified cross-country correlates of YUR. Results Among 545.1 million African youth, 57.1% were employed, 4.2% unemployed, 15.0% inactive, and 23.6% in education. The continental YUR was 6.91%, ranging from 0.4% in Niger to 37.4% in Eswatini, with higher unemployment in North Africa (11.09%) than sub-Saharan Africa (6.40%). The NEET rate reached 19.23%, agriculture accounted for 45.9% of youth employment, and female unemployment exceeded male unemployment (7.56% vs. 6.40%). Upper secondary and tertiary graduates represented 52.7% of unemployed youth. YUR was strongly correlated with the NEET rate (r = 0.720, p < 0.001). OLS models showed that agricultural employment share was the strongest negative correlate of YUR (β = −0.255, p < 0.001), whereas the share of educated unemployed was positively associated with YUR (p = 0.043). Conclusions African youth labour markets exhibit substantial cross-country heterogeneity, persistent informality, and education–employment mismatches. Policies promoting structural transformation, skills alignment, gender equality, and social protection are needed. These findings highlight the limitations of unemployment rates alone as indicators of youth labour market performance.

    Optimal Design for a Psychosocial Intervention on Severely Acute Malnourished Children in Humanitarian Settings: Results of an Expert Survey [version 2; peer review: 1 approved with reservations]

    Background Common psychosocial interventions focus on feeding, stimulation, emotional responsiveness and childcare and parenting. Inclusion of such a component in the outpatient management of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) children is recommended. However, evidence remains scarce on both the optimal modalities and the potential impact of psychosocial interventions integrated into SAM treatment protocols. This survey aimed to gather expert opinions on the optimal design of a combined psychosocial and nutrition intervention feasible in humanitarian settings. Methods From March to May 2018, an online survey was emailed to international experts in nutrition and mental health and psychosocial support, mainly from academia and international non-governmental organisations (INGOs). It included multiple choices questions on the key components of an optimal combined intervention. Results Of the 76 experts targeted, 20 responded. 11 (55%) belonged to INGOs, 2 (10%) to academia, and 4 (20%) to international organizations and donors. For most respondents, a combined intervention should be provided in weekly 45-minute counselling sessions, provided individually (rather than in a group) and at home (rather than at a health center). No consensus emerged regarding the ideal overall duration of the intervention. Experts thought that 35% of staff training should be in “Active listening for psychosocial support”, and 30% in “Early child development”, 25% in “Maternal depression” and 9% in “Anthropometric measurements”. Respondents estimated a mean expected recovery rate of 74% (n = 17), corresponding to an expected 14 percentage-point increase compared with the 60% reference recovery rate. Qualitative results highlighted the importance of tailoring the intervention to the individual, the population and the settings; as well as considering feasibility and scalability at the design stage. Conclusion Among the proposed modalities, the most frequently selected options were weekly 45-minute psychosocial sessions, individual rather than group delivery, and delivery at home rather than at a health centre. Respondents also emphasized that duration and delivery modalities should be adapted to context. Further research should refine these components through formative work or a consensus-building process before assessing feasibility, effectiveness and cost-effectiveness in humanitarian SAM programmes.

    Pediatric Dentist Attire as a Behavioral Strategy for Managing Dental Anxiety: A Systematic Review [version 2; peer review: 2 not approved]

    Background Dental anxiety is a common challenge in children and may adversely affect cooperation, treatment acceptance, and future dental attendance. Dentist attire represents a potentially modifiable environmental factor that may influence children’s perception of the dental setting. This systematic review aimed to evaluate the association between pediatric dentist attire and dental anxiety and behavioural responses in children, with particular emphasis on conventional professional attire compared with colourful, patterned, or child-friendly attire. Methods A systematic review was conducted according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines. The review protocol was registered in PROSPERO (CRD420251229652). PubMed/MEDLINE, Cochrane Library, Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar were searched for studies published from January 1985 to August 2026. Studies involving children evaluating different types of pediatric dentist attire and reporting dental anxiety, fear, behavioural responses, or related outcomes were eligible. Two reviewers independently screened studies, extracted data, and assessed risk of bias. Observational studies were assessed using a modified Newcastle–Ottawa Scale, while the randomized clinical trial was assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool. Results The search identified 513 records, of which 10 studies involving 4,398 children were included in the final synthesis. Individual study sample sizes ranged from 120 to 1,155 children, with participants predominantly aged 6–12 years. Most studies used cross-sectional or observational comparative designs, while one randomized clinical trial was identified. Conventional white attire was compared with coloured, cartoon-themed, child-friendly, casual, or surgical attire. Dental anxiety was assessed using the Modified Child Dental Anxiety Scale, Children’s Fear Survey Schedule–Dental Subscale, Facial Image Scale, Children’s Fear Scale, and Child Anxiety Questionnaire. Overall, coloured and child-friendly attire demonstrated a favourable trend towards lower anxiety compared with conventional white attire. The effect appeared more pronounced among younger children in several studies. However, findings were not consistent across all attire modifications, and some studies primarily reported children’s attire preferences rather than direct comparative anxiety outcomes. Differences in study design, attire interventions, anxiety measures, assessment timing, and outcome reporting limited direct comparison between studies and precluded a reliable estimate of the magnitude of effect. Conclusion Pediatric dentist attire may influence children’s dental anxiety, with coloured, cartoon-themed, and explicitly child-friendly attire showing the most consistent favourable trend. The effect may be particularly relevant in younger children and may form part of a broader child-centred dental environment. However, the current evidence is heterogeneous and should be interpreted cautiously. Well-designed randomized controlled trials using standardized attire classifications and validated anxiety measures are required to establish the clinical effectiveness of modifying pediatric dentist attire.     How Does Indonesian Headline News Represent Donald Trump’s Power through Threat and Diplomacy Discourse? [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Donald Trump has become one of the most prominent political figures frequently represented in global media discourse, particularly in issues related to international conflict and diplomacy. This study investigates the representation of Donald Trump’s power through threat and diplomacy discourse in Indonesian news headlines. Using the perspectives of Systemic Functional Linguistics (SFL) and Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA), the objectives of this study are to identify the language choices used in representing Donald Trump and to analyze the power relations and ideologies constructed by Indonesian media through news headlines. This research employs a qualitative approach with descriptive and interpretative methods. The data consist of Indonesian news headlines related to Donald Trump published in Antara News. Data were collected by searching headlines using the keyword “Donald Trump” and then classifying them into threat and diplomacy discourse categories. The data were analyzed using a qualitative data analysis model, including compiling, disassembling, reassembling, interpreting, and concluding. This study shows that Indonesian news headlines represent Donald Trump through threat and diplomacy discourses. Threat discourse is constructed through verbal and material processes indicating pressure, intimidation, and coercive actions, while diplomacy discourse is represented through verbal, mental, and material processes reflecting negotiation and cooperation. These representations position Trump as a dominant political actor with military and diplomatic authority. The findings also reveal that media language reflects power relations and reproduces the ideology of United States hegemony. Therefore, news headlines shape public understanding of global power, conflict, and diplomacy.

    Mapping Mathematics Teaching Skill Indicators: A Systematic Review and Bibliometric Analysis [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    The increasing emphasis on measurable teacher competencies has intensified scholarly attention toward mathematics teaching skills (MTS). However, the indicators underpinning effective mathematics teaching remain fragmented across the literature. This study aims to map global research trends and identify key indicators of mathematics teaching skills through a systematic review and bibliometric analysis. Scopus-indexed publications published between 2015 and 2025 were searched. Studies explicitly addressing mathematics teaching skills were considered eligible for inclusion. A total of 212 records were identified and screened following the PRISMA 2020 framework through identification, screening, eligibility, and inclusion stages. After applying the selection criteria, 18 studies were included in the final review. To provide a broader overview of the field, bibliometric analysis was conducted on 59 records that passed title and abstract screening. VOSviewer was used to analyze publication trends, co-authorship networks, keyword co-occurrence, and citation patterns, while the included studies were synthesized thematically to identify mathematics teaching skill indicators. The findings reveal a steady increase in research output over the past decade, reflecting growing international attention to the professionalization of mathematics teachers. The most dominant dimension identified across the literature was the integration of Content Knowledge (CK) and Pedagogical Knowledge (PK), accounting for 53.7% of the studies. This was followed by Content Knowledge (CK) as a standalone dimension (20.9%). The third most prominent dimensions were the integration of Content Knowledge and Technological Knowledge (CK–TK), Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge (TPCK), and Continuous Professional Development (CPD), each representing 7.5% of the studies.     Curriculum for Bioimaging Data Stewardship: A Systematic Review of Existing Training Resources [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Abstract The increasing complexity and volume of biological imaging data require structured and sustainable research data management to ensure reproducibility, reusability, and compliance with FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable) principles. In this context, Data Stewards (DaSts) support researchers throughout the bioimaging data lifecycle by facilitating data organization, documentation, standardization, preservation, and sharing. This work presents a curriculum for becoming a Data Steward in biological imaging. It is intended for educators, learners, and institutions. Openly available training materials were systematically identified through (i) targeted searches of domain-specific repositories and community platforms, (ii) keyword-based web searches, and (iii) citation and reference tracing. Searches were conducted between December 2025 and May 2026. Materials were evaluated using a qualitative scoring framework and mapped to defined learning objectives. This systematic review showed that many relevant educational resources exist but are distributed across diverse platforms and communities. The search focused on openly available English-language resources and may therefore underrepresent region-specific or non-English materials. Additionally, qualitative scoring involves subjective judgment despite the structured evaluation framework. This curriculum complements previous work defining the role of bioimaging Data Stewards by providing a structured overview of the interdisciplinary knowledge, competencies, and training resources required across the bioimaging data lifecycle. It systematically identifies and organizes existing resources into a coherent learning pathway while highlighting gaps, particularly in interdisciplinary collaboration and bioimaging-specific data standards. As bioimaging technologies, data infrastructures, and regulations evolve, the curriculum should remain a living framework, continuously updated through community-driven resources like the NFDI4BIOIMAGE Training Material Hub. This systematic review was conducted in accordance with the Prisma 2020 statement where applicable, was preregistered [https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/XEJNC] and financially supported by the German Research Foundation and the University of Konstanz.

    AI-Driven Neural Surrogates for In Silico Design of Cognitive-Affective Neuromodulation Targets

    arXiv:2609.27729v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: In neuropsychiatry, the primary goal is often not only to decode brain activity but to change it, for example to lessen a negative affective bias or an overly salient memory. Motivated by control theory, we develop an AI-driven neural-surrogate framework that proposes candidate representational changes and tests their predicted perceptual effects from snapshots of stimulus-evoked fMRI activity, without physical stimulation. The framework combines fMRI decoding, deep generative modeling, and constrained latent-space steering. Valence and memorability are used only as worked examples. Using more than 36,000 image-fMRI observations from four deeply sampled Natural Scenes Dataset participants, subject-specific models recovered coarse generative structure from visually responsive cortex (two-way identification, 0.79-0.88; chance, 0.5). Graded perturbations were reconstructed as images and evaluated with automated scorers and human ratings from 7,200 trials by 18 participants. In the primary VDVAE model, valence shifted from -0.61 to +1.03 SD and memorability from -1.34 to +1.45 SD; a later Versatile Diffusion refinement reduced or altered these effects. Across five perturbation levels, human valence ratings moved in the predicted direction under the linear time-correction model (mean slope, 0.038 SD per unit of alpha; 95 percent CI, 0.003-0.074; positive in 16 of 18 participants). Perceived memorability did not change reliably. Baseline agreement with the automated assessor was suggestive for valence (r = 0.30) and weak for memorability (r = 0.10). Extreme perturbations drifted from the original stimulus, so intended change must be weighed against loss of fidelity. These findings provide a falsifiable upstream method for designing and behaviorally testing candidate representational targets for future neuromodulation in psychiatry, while marking the limits of the present static approximation.

    arXiv:2605.00746v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Reliable control in motor imagery brain-computer interfaces (MI-BCIs) requires the precise decoding of user-specific neural rhythms, which vary significantly across individuals.     Critical Flicker Fusion Frequency As An Experience-Restricted Constraint On Visual Temporal Resolution: What Does And Does Not Change It

    arXiv:2607.29068v2 Announce Type: replace Abstract: Experience-dependent plasticity is fundamental to adaptive behaviour, yet the conditions under which basic sensory timing can be modified in adulthood remain poorly specified. Critical flicker fusion frequency (CFFF), the threshold at which flicker is perceived as continuous, is unusually informative here: what fails to change it is as well documented as what does. Reviewing the stability and training literature, we argue CFFF is best characterised neither as non-plastic nor as held near a physiological ceiling, but as modifiable only by a specific class of experience, not by amount. Repeated testing, cognitive training without temporal content, and incoherent-flicker exposure leave the threshold unchanged regardless of duration. By contrast, a narrow class of perceptual-learning paradigms -- pairing coherent directional motion with a task-relevant target -- reportedly raises the threshold substantially, with gains retained at one year in a small subsample. Gains are largest below typical values, as in amblyopia, and absent in normally sighted observers under the same protocol. Three qualifications apply: the evidence rests on small samples; the threshold-raising paradigms have been assessed almost exclusively with heterochromatic flicker photometry rather than luminance-defined CFFF, leaving construct equivalence unestablished; and a minority of untrained controls show comparable changes. We examine whether the restriction originates at peripheral, thalamocortical, or cortical levels; available data do not adjudicate between them. Functional arguments for why such a restriction might be adaptive are offered as rationales, not evidence. Proposed links to working-memory precision and metacognition are stated as predictions; the first test of them was largely negative.

    The Computational Value of Sensory-Aligned Receptive Fields Depends on Neuronal Expressivity

    arXiv:2609.26940v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Biological sensory neurons have selective receptive fields organized along meaningful stimulus coordinates, such as frequency, motion direction, or retinotopic position. Such structure may arise from efficient coding and biological constraints on activity, connectivity, and wiring, as computational studies of simple neurons have shown across modalities. This raises a question: do structured receptive fields confer a computational advantage beyond resource efficiency itself, and does this advantage persist when individual neurons are highly expressive? We address this question in recurrent networks of Expressive Leaky Memory neurons, where we can independently vary neuronal complexity and the organization of feed-forward receptive fields.
